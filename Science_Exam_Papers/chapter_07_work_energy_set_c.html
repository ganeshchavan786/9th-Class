SECTION B: ASSERTION - REASON QUESTIONS (Q.16 TO Q.20) [5 MARKS]
Directions for Q16 to Q20: Select the correct option from the choices given below:
(A) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
(B) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is NOT the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
(C) Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
(D) Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true (or both are false).
Q.16 Assertion (A): Work done by gravity on an object sliding down a frictionless incline of height $h$ equals the work done dropping it vertically through height $h$.
Reason (R): Gravitational force is a conservative force, and work done between two vertical levels depends only on initial and final heights, not the path taken.
Q.17 Assertion (A): A heavier truck and a light car having identical kinetic energies have different momenta.
Reason (R): Momentum is proportional to $\sqrt{m}$ for a given constant kinetic energy ($p = \sqrt{2m E_k}$).
Q.18 Assertion (A): A machine cannot multiply both force and speed simultaneously.
Reason (R): Work output cannot exceed work input ($Load \times d_L \le Effort \times d_E$); if force is multiplied ($MA > 1$), distance and speed must be compromised ($VR < 1$).
Q.19 Assertion (A): When a gas expands isothermally against atmospheric pressure, it does positive work.
Reason (R): The force exerted by the expanding gas acts in the same direction as the displacement of the piston.
Q.20 Assertion (A): A stationary satellite in orbit consumes rocket fuel continuously to do gravitational work.
Reason (R): The gravitational pull of the Earth continuously accelerates the satellite inward.

OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & EXEMPLAR EXPLANATIONS (SET C)
Q. No. Correct Scientific Rationale & NCERT Exemplar Analysis
Q.1 (C) At the top, minimum speed is $v_{top} = \sqrt{gR}$. By energy conservation: $\frac{1}{2}mv_{bottom}^2 = \frac{1}{2}mv_{top}^2 + mg(2R) \Rightarrow v_{bottom}^2 = gR + 4gR = 5gR \Rightarrow v_{bottom} = \sqrt{5gR}$.
Q.2 (B) $W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{s} = (3 \times 5) + (4 \times 2) = 15 + 8 = 23$ Joules.
Q.3 (B) $E_k = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 = \frac{1}{2}(1000)(20^2) = 500 \times 400 = 200,000$ J $= 200$ kJ.
Q.4 (A) Hooke's law: $F = k x = 500\text{ N/m} \times 0.04\text{ m} = 20$ N.
Q.5 (B) Total energy $= P \times t = 2000\text{ W} \times 600\text{ s} = 1,200,000$ J. $W = mgh \Rightarrow 1,200,000 = m(10)(10) \Rightarrow 100m = 1,200,000 \Rightarrow m = 12,000$ kg $= 12,000$ liters.
Q.6 (C) Friction is a non-conservative, dissipative force; the work done against friction over a closed path is non-zero and dissipates mechanical energy as heat.
Q.7 (B) Total loss of potential energy $= mg(h + d) = 2 \times 10 \times (5 + 0.10) = 20 \times 5.10 = 102$ J. Work done by sand $= F \times d \Rightarrow F \times 0.10 = 102 \Rightarrow F = 1020$ N.
Q.8 (A) $MA_{ideal} = \frac{\text{Radius of wheel}}{\text{Radius of axle}} = \frac{40}{8} = 5$.
Q.9 (B) $p = mv \Rightarrow v = p/m$. $E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}m(p/m)^2 = \frac{p^2}{2m}$.
Q.10 (A) Instantaneous power is the scalar product of force and instantaneous velocity: $P = F v$.
Q.11 (B) $\text{Average power} = \frac{\text{Work}}{\text{Time}} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}mv^2}{t} = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{mv}{t}\right) v = \frac{1}{2} F v$.
Q.12 (A) Input hydraulic power $= mgh/t = 10,000 \times 10 \times 100 = 10,000,000$ W $= 10$ MW. Output power $= 0.85 \times 10 = 8.5$ Megawatts.
Q.13 (A) $W_{out} = 400 \times 2 = 800$ J. $W_{in} = 100 \times 10 = 1000$ J. $\eta = \frac{800}{1000} \times 100 = 80\%$.
Q.14 (B) At that height, $E_p = 50$ J. $mgh = 50 \Rightarrow 1 \times 10 \times h = 50 \Rightarrow h = 5.0$ m.
Q.15 (C) In a Class 3 lever (e.g. human forearm holding weight, fishing rod, tweezers), effort is applied between fulcrum and load, making effort arm shorter than load arm ($MA < 1$).
Q.16 (A) Because gravity is conservative, $W = mgh$ independent of path curvature or inclination.
Q.17 (A) Since $p = \sqrt{2m E_k}$, the heavier vehicle ($m_{truck} > m_{car}$) carries substantially greater momentum.
Q.18 (A) Energy conservation dictates that a machine can be a force multiplier ($MA > 1$) or a speed multiplier ($VR < 1$), but never both.
Q.19 (A) $W = \int P dV$. When volume expands ($dV > 0$), pressure force and boundary displacement align, performing positive work.
Q.20 (D) Assertion is completely false: In circular orbit, displacement is perpendicular to gravitational force, so zero work is done ($W=0$) and no fuel is consumed to maintain orbit. Reason is true.
Q.21 (B) $E_p = mgh_A = 500 \times 10 \times 45 = 225,000$ J $= 225$ kJ.
Q.22 (B) $\frac{1}{2}mv^2 = mgh_A \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{2gh_A} = \sqrt{2 \times 10 \times 45} = \sqrt{900} = 30$ m/s (108 km/h).
Q.23 (A) $v = \sqrt{2g(h_A - h_B)} = \sqrt{2 \times 10 \times (45 - 25)} = \sqrt{20 \times 20} = 20$ m/s.
Q.24 (A) $E_k = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 = \frac{1}{2} (500) (20^2) = 250 \times 400 = 100,000$ J $= 100$ kJ.
Q.25 (B) $\text{Available } E_k = 100\text{ kJ} - 25\text{ kJ (friction loss)} = 75$ kJ.
