SECTION B: ASSERTION - REASON QUESTIONS (Q.16 TO Q.20) [5 MARKS]
Directions for Q16 to Q20: Select the correct option from the choices given below:
(A) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
(B) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is NOT the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
(C) Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
(D) Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true (or both are false).
Q.16 Assertion (A): The least count of a measuring instrument represents the smallest measurement that can be accurately resolved by it.
Reason (R): A smaller least count generally leads to higher precision in measurement.
Q.17 Assertion (A): Random errors cannot be completely avoided even in the most carefully designed experiment.
Reason (R): Random errors arise from unpredictable microscopic variations in environmental conditions and minor observer judgment fluctuations.
Q.18 Assertion (A): A physical equation can be dimensionally correct yet physically incorrect.
Reason (R): Dimensionless constants (such as $\frac{1}{2}$, $\pi$) in an equation cannot be verified by dimensional analysis.
Q.19 Assertion (A): Flammable liquids like kerosene or alcohol can be extinguished using water.
Reason (R): Water has a high specific heat capacity that cools burning liquids.
Q.20 Assertion (A): In a scientific controlled experiment, only one independent variable is altered at a time.
Reason (R): Altering multiple variables simultaneously makes it impossible to isolate which specific factor produced the observed change.

OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & EXEMPLAR EXPLANATIONS (SET C)
Q. No. Correct Scientific Rationale & NCERT Exemplar Analysis
Q.1 (B) Since $T = 2\pi \sqrt{L/g}$, squaring both sides gives $T^2 = \left(\frac{4\pi^2}{g}\right) L$. This is of the form $y = mx$, representing a straight line passing through the origin.
Q.2 (C) Relative error in density $\frac{\Delta \rho}{\rho} = \frac{\Delta m}{m} + \frac{\Delta V}{V} = \frac{0.5}{50.0} + \frac{0.5}{25.0} = 0.01 + 0.02 = 0.03 = 3.0\%$.
Q.3 (B) Trailing zeros after a decimal point are significant. '2.500' has four significant figures (2, 5, 0, 0). Leading zeros in 0.0025 and 0.0250 are not significant.
Q.4 (C) In addition and subtraction, the final result can have no more decimal places than the measurement with the fewest decimal places (1.2 cm has 1 decimal place; rounded to 14.1 cm).
Q.5 (A) $\text{Least count} = \frac{\text{Pitch}}{\text{Number of circular divisions}} = \frac{0.5\text{ mm}}{50} = 0.01\text{ mm}$.
Q.6 (B) The SI system is globally standardized, coherent, and uses decimal powers of ten, minimizing unit translation errors and simplifying scientific calculations.
Q.7 (B) Flushing with large volumes of running water rapidly dilutes and washes away the acid. Never apply strong bases, as neutralization is highly exothermic and can cause thermal burns.
Q.8 (B) Relative density is the ratio of density of a substance to density of water at 4°C. Being a ratio of two identical physical units, it is pure dimensionless.
Q.9 (B) $V = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Density}} = \frac{6\text{ g}}{0.25\text{ g/cm}^3} = 24\text{ cm}^3$.
Q.10 (B) Accuracy is how close a measured value is to the true or accepted reference value; precision is how close repeated measurements are to one another.
Q.11 (A) $\text{Slope} = \frac{4\pi^2}{g} \Rightarrow g = \frac{4\pi^2}{\text{Slope}} = \frac{4(3.1416)^2}{4.02} = \frac{39.478}{4.02} \approx 9.82\text{ m/s}^2$.
Q.12 (C) Candela (cd) is the SI base unit of luminous intensity. Joule, Watt, and Pascal are derived units.
Q.13 (A) Thickness per sheet $= \frac{5.0\text{ cm}}{500} = 0.01\text{ cm} = 0.1\text{ mm}$.
Q.14 (B) The wire gauze (often with a ceramic center) spreads the Bunsen flame heat uniformly, preventing localized hot spots that shatter glassware.
Q.15 (B) $300,000,000\text{ m/s} = 3.0 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$ in standard normalized scientific notation.
Q.16 (B) Both statements are correct facts, but the reason states an advantage of small least count rather than explaining the operational definition of least count.
Q.17 (A) Random errors follow a normal statistical distribution due to uncontrollable thermal, mechanical, or human limits, making them inevitable in empirical testing.
Q.18 (A) For instance, $s = 2 u t + a t^2$ is dimensionally consistent ($[L] = [L]$) but physically wrong because the constant factor is erroneous.
Q.19 (D) Assertion is completely false: Kerosene and alcohol are less dense than water; pouring water causes the burning liquid to float on top, spreading the fire! (Use sand or foam extinguisher).
Q.20 (A) Fair testing requires holding all controlled variables fixed while systematically varying only the single factor under study.
Q.21 (B) Since $\Delta L = L_0 \alpha \Delta T$, with identical initial length and temperature rise, Aluminum expanded the most ($1.92$ mm), indicating the highest $\alpha$.
Q.22 (A) $\alpha = \frac{\Delta L}{L_0 \Delta T} = \frac{0.00096\text{ m}}{1.000\text{ m} \times 80^\circ\text{C}} = 1.2 \times 10^{-5}\text{ /}^\circ\text{C}$.
Q.23 (B) Copper expands more than Iron, forcing Copper to form the longer outer curve while Iron forms the shorter inner curve. Hence it bends towards Iron.
Q.24 (B) Without expansion gaps, high summer temperatures cause thermal expansion stress that severely warps and buckles railway tracks, risking derailment.
Q.25 (B) Thermostats use bending bimetallic strips to mechanically make and break electrical contacts as temperature fluctuates.
