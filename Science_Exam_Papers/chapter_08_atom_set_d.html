SECTION A: CORE THEORETICAL PRINCIPLES (Q.1 TO Q.10) [10 MARKS]
Q.1

Assertion (A): Dalton's atomic theory postulate that atoms are indivisible particles was disproved by late 19th-century physics discoveries.

Reason (R): The experimental discovery of subatomic particles (electrons, protons, and neutrons) proved that atoms are composed of smaller constituent entities.

Q.2

Assertion (A): Cathode rays discovered by J.J. Thomson consist of streams of negatively charged particles called electrons.

Reason (R): Cathode rays are deflected towards the positive plate in an external electric field and possess an identical charge-to-mass ratio ($e/m$) regardless of the gas used in the discharge tube.

Q.3

Assertion (A): An atom as a whole is electrically neutral.

Reason (R): The total number of negatively charged electrons orbiting outside the nucleus equals the total number of positively charged protons located inside the nucleus.

Q.4

Assertion (A): J.J. Thomson proposed the 'plum pudding' model of the atom.

Reason (R): Thomson visualized the atom as a uniform positively charged sphere in which electrons are embedded like dry raisins in a pudding.

Q.5

Assertion (A): In Rutherford's alpha particle scattering experiment, a thin foil of gold was selected as the target.

Reason (R): Gold is the most malleable metal, allowing fabrication of extremely thin foils approximately 1,000 atoms thick.

Q.6

Assertion (A): Most of the alpha particles passed straight through the gold foil without any deflection.

Reason (R): Most of the space inside an atom is completely empty.

Q.7

Assertion (A): Rutherford deduced that the entire positive charge and nearly all the mass of an atom are concentrated in a tiny central region called the nucleus.

Reason (R): A small fraction of alpha particles (about 1 in 12,000) suffered massive deflections and rebounded backward at 180°.

Q.8

Assertion (A): Rutherford's planetary model of the atom could not explain the atomic stability of matter.

Reason (R): According to classical electromagnetic theory, an accelerating charged electron orbiting a nucleus should continuously radiate electromagnetic energy, lose speed, and spiral into the nucleus in $10^{-8}$ s.

Q.9

Assertion (A): Neils Bohr proposed that electrons revolve around the nucleus only in certain discrete, non-radiating orbits called stationary energy levels.

Reason (R): While revolving in these discrete permissible orbits, electrons do not radiate energy.

Q.10

Assertion (A): The maximum number of electrons that can be accommodated in the $n$-th energy shell is given by $2n^2$.

Reason (R): For the M-shell ($n=3$), the maximum theoretical capacity according to the Bohr-Bury scheme is 18 electrons.

SECTION B: EXPERIMENTAL OBSERVATIONS & INFERENCES (Q.11 TO Q.18) [8 MARKS]
Q.11

Assertion (A): The outermost valence shell of an atom cannot accommodate more than 8 electrons, even if its theoretical capacity is larger.

Reason (R): An octet of valence electrons confers exceptional chemical stability (octet rule), as observed in noble gas electronic configurations.

Q.12

Assertion (A): James Chadwick discovered the neutron by bombarding thin beryllium targets with alpha particles in 1932.

Reason (R): Neutrons are electrically neutral subatomic particles with a mass approximately equal to that of a proton, located within atomic nuclei.

Q.13

Assertion (A): The mass number ($A$) of an atom is defined as the total number of protons and neutrons (nucleons) present in its nucleus.

Reason (R): Electrons have negligible mass ($\approx 1/1836$ of a proton mass), so the entire mass of an atom is concentrated in its nucleus.

Q.14

Assertion (A): Valency of magnesium (atomic number 12) is 2, while valency of chlorine (atomic number 17) is 1.

Reason (R): Magnesium readily loses 2 valence electrons ($2, 8, 2$) to achieve an octet, while chlorine gains 1 electron ($2, 8, 7$) to complete its octet ($8 - 7 = 1$).

Q.15

Assertion (A): Hydrogen is the only element whose most abundant atom ($^1_1H$, protium) contains no neutrons in its nucleus.

Reason (R): Protium has an atomic number of 1 and mass number of 1, yielding $A - Z = 1 - 1 = 0$ neutrons.

Q.16

Assertion (A): Isotopes of an element exhibit identical chemical properties.

Reason (R): Isotopes have the exact same atomic number and electronic configuration, and chemical reactivity is determined exclusively by valence electrons.

Q.17

Assertion (A): Isotopes of an element exhibit slightly different physical properties such as density and boiling points.

Reason (R): Isotopes possess different numbers of neutrons and consequently different atomic masses.

Q.18

Assertion (A): The average atomic mass of chlorine is recorded as 35.5 u.

Reason (R): Natural chlorine occurs as a mixture of two isotopes, $^{35}Cl$ and $^{37}Cl$, in a relative abundance ratio of $3:1$ ($75\%$ to $25\%$).

SECTION C: ADVANCED NUANCES & MISCONCEPTIONS (Q.19 TO Q.25) [7 MARKS]
Q.19

Assertion (A): Cobalt-60 ($^{60}Co$) isotope is widely used in the medical treatment of cancer (radiotherapy).

Reason (R): Cobalt-60 emits high-energy penetrating gamma rays that destroy rapidly dividing malignant tumor cells.

Q.20

Assertion (A): Uranium-235 ($^{235}U$) isotope is utilized as fuel in nuclear fission reactors to generate electrical power.

Reason (R): The fission of Uranium-235 nuclei induced by thermal neutrons releases enormous quantities of controlled nuclear energy.

Q.21

Assertion (A): Iodine-131 ($^{131}I$) isotope is used in the clinical diagnosis and treatment of goitre and thyroid disorders.

Reason (R): The thyroid gland selectively absorbs and concentrates iodine from the bloodstream to synthesize thyroxine hormone.

Q.22

Assertion (A): $^{40}_{18}Ar$ (Argon) and $^{40}_{20}Ca$ (Calcium) are classified as isobars.

Reason (R): Isobars are atoms of different chemical elements having different atomic numbers but identical mass numbers ($A=40$).

Q.23

Assertion (A): An alpha particle is essentially a doubly charged helium ion ($He^{2+}$).

Reason (R): An alpha particle consists of two protons and two neutrons, with mass 4 u and charge $+2e$, identical to a helium nucleus stripped of both electrons.

Q.24

Assertion (A): Noble gases like helium, neon, and argon have zero chemical valency.

Reason (R): Noble gases possess completely filled valence electron shells (duplet in helium, octets in neon and argon), rendering them chemically inert under standard conditions.

Q.25

Assertion (A): The radius of the atomic nucleus is approximately $10^5$ times smaller than the radius of the whole atom.

Reason (R): Nuclear radius is on the order of $10^{-15}$ m (fermi), whereas atomic radius is on the order of $10^{-10}$ m (angstrom).
