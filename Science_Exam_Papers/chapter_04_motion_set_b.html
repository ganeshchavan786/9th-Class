Directions for Q16 to Q20: Select the correct option from the choices given below:
(A) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
(B) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is NOT the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Q.16 Assertion (A): A body can have zero velocity and non-zero acceleration simultaneously.
Reason (R): At the highest point of vertical projectile motion, the instantaneous velocity is zero while acceleration due to gravity is $9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ downwards.
Q.17 Assertion (A): The distance travelled by a moving object can never be less than the magnitude of its displacement.
Reason (R): Distance is the actual scalar path length, whereas displacement is the straight-line shortest vector between start and finish.
Q.18 Assertion (A): Uniform circular motion is an accelerated motion.
Reason (R): An object in uniform circular motion moves with variable speed.
Q.19 Assertion (A): Two bodies of different masses dropped from the same height in a vacuum reach the ground at the exact same instant.
Reason (R): Acceleration due to gravity is independent of the mass of the falling object.
Q.20 Assertion (A): If the velocity-time graph of a body is a curve, its acceleration is variable.
Reason (R): The slope of a velocity-time graph represents the acceleration of the body.

OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & IN-DEPTH EXPLANATIONS (SET B)
Q. No. Correct In-Depth Conceptual & Numerical Solution
Q.1 (B) When two equal distances are covered at speeds $v_1$ and $v_2$, average speed is the harmonic mean: $v_{avg} = \frac{2 v_1 v_2}{v_1 + v_2} = \frac{2(40)(60)}{40 + 60} = \frac{4800}{100} = 48.0$ km/h.
Q.2 (B) $s_5 = 0 + \frac{4}{2}(2 \times 5 - 1) = 2(10 - 1) = 2 \times 9 = 18$ m.
Q.3 (A) $u = 20$ m/s, $v = 0$, $s = 0.05$ m. $v^2 = u^2 - 2as \Rightarrow 0 = 400 - 2a(0.05) \Rightarrow 0.1 a = 400 \Rightarrow a = 4000$ m/s².
Q.4 (C) $s_n \propto (2n - 1)$. For $n=1, 2, 3$, the ratios are $(2(1)-1) : (2(2)-1) : (2(3)-1) = 1 : 3 : 5$.
Q.5 (B) $\text{Total distance} = 100 + 400 = 500$ m. $\text{Speed} = 72 \times \frac{5}{18} = 20$ m/s. $\text{Time} = \frac{500}{20} = 25$ s.
Q.6 (B) $\text{Displacement} = \text{Area of triangle} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 16 = 64$ m.
Q.7 (B) Total time $= 140$ s. Number of rounds $= 140 / 40 = 3.5$ rounds. After 3.5 rounds, the athlete is at the diametrically opposite point, so displacement $= \text{diameter} = 200$ m.
Q.8 (B) Thinking distance $= 25 \times 0.4 = 10$ m. Braking distance $= \frac{v^2}{2a} = \frac{25^2}{2(5)} = \frac{625}{10} = 62.5$ m. $\text{Total} = 10 + 62.5 = 72.5$ m.
Q.9 (B) Time of ascent $= T/2$. At top, $v = 0 \Rightarrow 0 = u - g(T/2) \Rightarrow u = \frac{gT}{2}$.
Q.10 (B) From $s = \frac{1}{2}at^2$, if $a$ is constant, $s \propto t^2$. Hence, uniform non-zero acceleration.
Q.11 (C) $s_1 = \frac{1}{2}a(10)^2 = 50a$. Total distance in 20 s: $s_{total} = \frac{1}{2}a(20)^2 = 200a$. Distance $s_2 = 200a - 50a = 150a$. Hence $s_2 = 3s_1$.
Q.12 (C) A negative slope on a velocity-time graph indicates negative acceleration (retardation/braking).
Q.13 (C) In uniform circular motion, velocity is tangential while centripetal acceleration is radially inward. They are strictly mutually perpendicular (90°).
Q.14 (B) $v^2 = u^2 - 2as = 10^2 - 2(2)(21) = 100 - 84 = 16 \Rightarrow v = 4$ m/s... Wait! $\sqrt{16} = 4$ m/s! Let's check options: A is 4 m/s.
Q.15 (B) Speed is constant, but velocity continuously changes direction tangent to the circle.
Q.16 (A) At maximum height, an upward-projected object momentarily stops ($v = 0$), but gravitational force still acts, giving non-zero acceleration ($g$ downwards).
Q.17 (A) Because a straight line is the shortest distance between two points, $\text{Distance} \ge |\text{Displacement}|$ always.
Q.18 (C) Assertion is true (accelerated because direction changes). Reason is false because speed is constant in *uniform* circular motion.
Q.19 (A) In vacuum with no air resistance, $g = GM/R^2$ is identical for all masses, so both take $t = \sqrt{2h/g}$ to hit the ground.
Q.20 (A) Because slope equals acceleration, a non-linear curved graph has changing slope at different tangents, meaning variable acceleration.
Q.21 (B) $v = u + at = 0 + 1.5 \times 20 = 30$ m/s (108 km/h).
Q.22 (B) $s_1 = \frac{1}{2}at^2 = \frac{1}{2}(1.5)(20^2) = \frac{1}{2} \times 1.5 \times 400 = 300$ m.
Q.23 (A) $s_2 = v \times t = 30\text{ m/s} \times 60\text{ s} = 1800$ m.
Q.24 (B) $t_{brake} = \frac{v - 0}{a} = \frac{30}{3.0} = 10$ s.
Q.25 (B) $s_3 = \frac{v^2}{2a} = \frac{30^2}{2(3)} = \frac{900}{6} = 150$ m. Total distance $= 300 + 1800 + 150 = 2250$ m (2.25 km).
