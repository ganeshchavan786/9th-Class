OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & IN-DEPTH EXPLANATIONS (SET B)
Q. No. Correct In-Depth Conceptual & Numerical Solution
Q.1 (A) Least Count $= 1\text{ MSD} - 1\text{ VSD} = 1\text{ mm} - 0.9\text{ mm} = 0.1\text{ mm} = 0.01\text{ cm}$.
Q.2 (A) When the zero of the circular scale lies below the reference line, the gauge reads positive without load. Hence it is positive zero error and must be subtracted from the final reading.
Q.3 (B) Both 25.0 g and 10.0 cm³ have 3 significant figures. The quotient $25.0 / 10.0 = 2.50\text{ g/cm}^3$ must also have 3 significant figures.
Q.4 (B) $1\text{ kg/m}^3 = \frac{1000\text{ g}}{(100\text{ cm})^3} = \frac{10^3}{10^6}\text{ g/cm}^3 = 10^{-3}\text{ g/cm}^3$ (or $0.001\text{ g/cm}^3$).
Q.5 (A) $\text{Mean} = \frac{2.4 + 2.5 + 2.4 + 2.7}{4} = \frac{10.0}{4} = 2.5\text{ mm}$.
Q.6 (B) Ethanol is highly inflammable (boils at 78°C). Its vapors can ignite easily; hence it must be heated inside a boiling water bath, never directly over an open flame.
Q.7 (B) $\text{Displaced volume} = 55 - 45 = 10\text{ mL} = 10\text{ cm}^3$. $\text{Density} = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Volume}} = \frac{78\text{ g}}{10\text{ cm}^3} = 7.8\text{ g/cm}^3$.
Q.8 (A) $1\text{ km} = 10^6\text{ mm}$. Therefore, $3.5 \times 10^{-2} \times 10^6 = 3.5 \times 10^4 = 35,000\text{ mm}$.
Q.9 (B) A line or curve of best fit minimizes random observational errors by showing the overall mathematical trend rather than connecting random fluctuations.
Q.10 (C) $V_{Cu} = 89 / 8.9 = 10\text{ cm}^3$. $V_{metal} = 178 / 8.9 = 20\text{ cm}^3$. $\text{Total volume} = 10 + 20 = 30\text{ cm}^3$.
Q.11 (C) Under the revised SI definitions (2019), the kilogram (kg) is defined based on the fundamental Planck constant $h$.
Q.12 (B) Mercury does not wet glass (cohesive forces exceed adhesive forces), forming a convex meniscus. Hence, the reading is taken at the top/crest at eye level.
Q.13 (A) $\text{Percentage uncertainty} = \frac{\text{Least count}}{\text{Measured value}} \times 100 = \frac{0.2}{12.4} \times 100 \approx 1.61\%$.
Q.14 (B) A valid scientific test changes only the independent variable (light intensity) while keeping all other confounding variables strictly controlled.
Q.15 (B) The Globally Harmonized System (GHS) skull and crossbones pictogram denotes acute severe toxicity (fatal or poisonous upon exposure).
Q.16 (A) Averaging reduces random Gaussian errors, but systematic errors (like zero error or faulty calibration) always shift readings unidirectionally, so averaging cannot eliminate them.
Q.17 (A) According to the scientific method (Karl Popper's criterion of falsifiability), scientific hypotheses must make testable predictions that could potentially fail in experiments.
Q.18 (A) Intensive properties do not depend on system size or amount of material. Since doubling mass also doubles volume, the ratio (density) remains constant.
Q.19 (D) Both statements are completely false and dangerous! Never inhale directly; gently waft vapors towards the nose using your hand from a safe distance.
Q.20 (A) The metric SI system uses base 10 prefixes (kilo, mega, centi, milli, micro), making mathematical conversion straightforward without arbitrary conversion fractions.
Q.21 (A) $\text{Radius } r = D / 2 = 2.00 / 2 = 1.00\text{ cm}$.
Q.22 (A) $V = \pi r^2 H = 3.14 \times (1.00)^2 \times 5.00 = 15.70\text{ cm}^3$.
Q.23 (A) $\text{Density} = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Volume}} = \frac{125.60\text{ g}}{15.70\text{ cm}^3} = 8.00\text{ g/cm}^3$.
Q.24 (B) To convert from $\text{g/cm}^3$ to $\text{kg/m}^3$, multiply by 1000: $8.00 \times 1000 = 8,000\text{ kg/m}^3$.
Q.25 (B) $\text{True mass} = 125.60 - 0.60 = 125.00\text{ g}$. $\text{True density} = 125.00 / 15.70 \approx 7.96\text{ g/cm}^3$.
