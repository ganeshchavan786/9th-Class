OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET G)
Q. No. Correct Rigorous Scientific Solution & Formula Derivation
Q.1 (A) Distance along semicircle $= \pi R$. Displacement across diameter $= 2R$. Ratio $= \frac{\pi R}{2R} = \frac{\pi}{2}$.
Q.2 (B) Distance fallen in $t/2$: $s = \frac{1}{2}g(t/2)^2 = \frac{1}{4}(\frac{1}{2}gt^2) = \frac{1}{4}H$. Height remaining above ground $= H - \frac{1}{4}H = \frac{3}{4}H$.
Q.3 (B) $v_{max} = 2 \times 10 = 20\text{ m/s}$. Phase 1: $s_1 = \frac{1}{2}(2)(10)^2 = 100\text{ m}$. Phase 2: $s_2 = 20 \times 20 = 400\text{ m}$. Phase 3: $s_3 = \frac{1}{2}(20)(5) = 50\text{ m}$. Total $= 100 + 400 + 50 = 550\text{ m}$.
Q.4 (C) Distance in $n$-th second $s_n = u + \frac{1}{2}a(2n - 1)$. For $u = 0$, $s_n \propto (2n - 1)$, giving ratios $1 : 3 : 5 : 7...$
Q.5 (B) Since $s = \frac{1}{2}at^2$, $s \propto t^2$ implies that acceleration $a$ is strictly constant.
Q.6 (A) $v^2 = u^2 + 2as \Rightarrow 0 = (200)^2 + 2a(0.10) \Rightarrow 0.20 a = -40000 \Rightarrow a = -200,000\text{ m/s}^2 = -2 \times 10^5\text{ m/s}^2$.
Q.7 (B) Frequency $f = 120/60 = 2\text{ rev/s}$. Speed $v = 2\pi r f = 2\pi(0.5)(2) = 2\pi \approx 6.28\text{ m/s}$.
Q.8 (A) Relative speed $= 36 + 54 = 90\text{ km/h} = 90 \times (5/18) = 25\text{ m/s}$. Total distance to cross $= 150 + 150 = 300\text{ m}$. Time $= 300/25 = 12\text{ s}$.
Q.9 (B) $v^2 = u^2 - 2g(H/2) = u^2 - gH$. Since $H = \frac{u^2}{2g}$, $gH = \frac{u^2}{2}$. Thus $v^2 = u^2 - \frac{u^2}{2} = \frac{u^2}{2} \Rightarrow v = \frac{u}{\sqrt{2}}$.
Q.10 (A) In uniform circular motion, speed is constant while the direction of motion continuously turns, continuously altering velocity.
Q.11 (B) A negative slope $\Delta v / \Delta t < 0$ means velocity is decreasing with time, representing retardation.
Q.12 (A) Comparing with $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$: $u = 3\text{ m/s}$, and $\frac{1}{2}a = 2 \Rightarrow a = 4\text{ m/s}^2$.
Q.13 (C) $v = \frac{dx}{dt} = 3at^2$, and $a_{acc} = \frac{dv}{dt} = 6at \propto t$. Acceleration increases linearly with time.
Q.14 (B) Rebound height $h' = e^2 h$. Here $h/4 = e^2 h \Rightarrow e^2 = 1/4 \Rightarrow e = 0.50$.
Q.15 (A) $v = u + at \Rightarrow 15 = u + (2 \times 5) \Rightarrow u = 15 - 10 = 5\text{ m/s}$.
Q.16 (A) Average velocity equals average speed only when total distance equals displacement magnitude (unidirectional straight-line path).
Q.17 (A) $a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \frac{(8000)^2}{6.4 \times 10^6} = \frac{64 \times 10^6}{6.4 \times 10^6} = 10.0\text{ m/s}^2$.
Q.18 (C) Stopping distance $d \propto v^2$. If speed triples ($3v$), braking distance increases by $3^2 = 9$ times ($9d$).
Q.19 (B) By conservation of mechanical energy, impact speed equals projection speed, directed downward ($-u$).
Q.20 (C) Net displacement is zero; average velocity is 0. Dividing non-zero average speed by 0 is mathematically undefined.
Q.21 (B) $s_n = u + a(n - 0.5)$. Difference $s_3 - s_2 = a \Rightarrow 30 - 20 = 10\text{ m/s}^2$.
Q.22 (B) At the peak apex, the object momentarily halts ($v=0$) before reversing its vertical trajectory.
Q.23 (B) Even though instantaneous velocity is zero, gravitational acceleration $g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ downward acts continuously.
Q.24 (A) From $v^2 = 2as$, plotting $v^2$ on y-axis against $s$ on x-axis produces a straight line $y = mx$ with slope $m = 2a$.
Q.25 (D) Rest, motion, displacement, and velocity are all defined relative to a specified frame of reference.
