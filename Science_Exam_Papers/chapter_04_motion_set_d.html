SECTION A: CORE THEORETICAL PRINCIPLES (Q.1 TO Q.10) [10 MARKS]
Q.1

Assertion (A): The displacement of an object can be zero even when the total distance travelled by it is non-zero.

Reason (R): Displacement is a vector quantity representing the shortest straight-line path between initial and final positions; returning to the start yields zero displacement.

Q.2

Assertion (A): The magnitude of displacement can never exceed the total distance covered by a moving body.

Reason (R): Distance is the actual path length, and the straight line connecting two endpoints is always the shortest possible distance between them.

Q.3

Assertion (A): An object moving with uniform speed can still possess acceleration.

Reason (R): Velocity is a vector quantity having both magnitude and direction; continuously changing the direction of motion causes acceleration even if speed is constant.

Q.4

Assertion (A): The slope of a distance-time graph represents the speed of the moving object.

Reason (R): Slope is calculated as $\frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}$, which is the rate of change of distance with respect to time.

Q.5

Assertion (A): The area enclosed beneath a velocity-time graph and the time axis gives the displacement of the object.

Reason (R): Displacement equals velocity multiplied by time ($s = v \times t$); the product of the dimensions of the axes ($[\text{m/s}] \times [\text{s}] = [\text{m}]$) represents area.

Q.6

Assertion (A): A car moving on a crowded city street undergoes non-uniform motion.

Reason (R): The car covers unequal distances in equal intervals of time due to traffic signals, pedestrian crossings, and turns.

Q.7

Assertion (A): A body can have zero velocity and non-zero acceleration simultaneously.

Reason (R): At the highest point of its vertical trajectory, an object thrown vertically upward stops momentarily ($v=0$) while gravity $g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ acts downward.

Q.8

Assertion (A): Negative acceleration is commonly referred to as retardation or deceleration.

Reason (R): Retardation occurs when the direction of the acceleration vector is opposite to the direction of the velocity vector, causing speed to decrease.

Q.9

Assertion (A): The motion of artificial satellites revolving around the Earth in circular orbits is an accelerated motion.

Reason (R): Satellites continuously change their direction of velocity towards the Earth due to centripetal gravitational acceleration.

Q.10

Assertion (A): Average speed can be zero for a moving body.

Reason (R): Average speed is defined as total distance travelled divided by total time taken.

SECTION B: EXPERIMENTAL OBSERVATIONS & INFERENCES (Q.11 TO Q.18) [8 MARKS]
Q.11

Assertion (A): If the distance-time graph of a body is a straight line parallel to the time axis, the body is at rest.

Reason (R): The position of the body does not change with the passage of time, meaning its velocity (slope) is zero.

Q.12

Assertion (A): An athlete running once around a circular track of radius $r$ completes the lap in time $t$. His average velocity is zero.

Reason (R): The initial and final positions of the athlete coincide after one full lap, making his net displacement vector zero.

Q.13

Assertion (A): The equations of motion ($v = u + at$, $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$, $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$) are valid only for motion with uniform acceleration.

Reason (R): These three kinematic equations are derived on the strict mathematical condition that acceleration $a$ remains constant throughout.

Q.14

Assertion (A): The velocity-time graph of a freely falling body dropped from rest is a straight line passing through the origin with a positive slope.

Reason (R): A freely falling body experiences constant downward gravitational acceleration ($g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$).

Q.15

Assertion (A): An odometer installed in an automobile measures its instantaneous speed.

Reason (R): A speedometer measures instantaneous speed in km/h, whereas an odometer records total cumulative distance travelled.

Q.16

Assertion (A): In uniform circular motion, the direction of linear velocity at any instant is along the tangent to the circular path.

Reason (R): If a stone tied to a string being whirled in a circle breaks loose, it flies off tangentially along a straight line.

Q.17

Assertion (A): A train moving at 90 km/h is moving faster than a car moving at 25 m/s.

Reason (R): To convert km/h to m/s, multiply by $5/18$; $90 \times (5/18) = 25\text{ m/s}$.

Q.18

Assertion (A): The slope of a velocity-time graph represents the acceleration of the moving body.

Reason (R): Slope is given by $\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$, which is the rate of change of velocity per unit time.

SECTION C: ADVANCED NUANCES & MISCONCEPTIONS (Q.19 TO Q.25) [7 MARKS]
Q.19

Assertion (A): A particle travelling with constant acceleration can never reverse its direction of motion.

Reason (R): A constant force always acts in a fixed spatial direction.

Q.20

Assertion (A): During uniform circular motion of radius $R$ and period $T$, the magnitude of acceleration is $a = \frac{v^2}{R} = \frac{4\pi^2 R}{T^2}$.

Reason (R): The acceleration in uniform circular motion is directed radially inwards towards the center of curvature (centripetal acceleration).

Q.21

Assertion (A): If the displacement-time graph of a body is a curve curving upwards with increasing slope, the body is accelerating.

Reason (R): The instantaneous slope of a position-time graph represents velocity, so an increasing slope indicates increasing velocity with time.

Q.22

Assertion (A): A person sitting inside a train moving with uniform velocity throws an apple vertically upwards; the apple lands directly back into his hands.

Reason (R): Due to inertia of motion, the apple maintains the same horizontal forward velocity as the train and the passenger throughout its flight.

Q.23

Assertion (A): When a ball bounces off a hard wall with the same speed, its velocity does not change.

Reason (R): Velocity is a vector quantity that depends on both speed and direction.

Q.24

Assertion (A): Two stones of masses 1 kg and 5 kg dropped simultaneously from the same height in a vacuum reach the ground at the exact same instant.

Reason (R): In a vacuum, the acceleration due to gravity $g$ is independent of the mass of the falling object ($a = g$).

Q.25

Assertion (A): An object can have a northward velocity while simultaneously experiencing a southward acceleration.

Reason (R): When brakes are applied to a car heading north, the retarding acceleration vector points in the opposite direction (south).
