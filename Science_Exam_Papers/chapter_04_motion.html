Directions for Q16 to Q20: Select the correct option from the choices given below:
(A) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
(B) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is NOT the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
(C) Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
(D) Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.
Q.16 Assertion (A): The displacement of a moving body can be zero even if the distance travelled is non-zero.
Reason (R): Displacement is the vector representing the shortest path from initial to final position.
Q.17 Assertion (A): Motion in a circular track with constant speed is an accelerated motion.
Reason (R): Velocity is a vector, and in circular motion, the direction of motion changes continuously at every instant.
Q.18 Assertion (A): The slope of a velocity-time graph gives the acceleration of the body.
Reason (R): Acceleration is defined as the time rate of change of displacement.
Q.19 Assertion (A): An object can have constant speed and varying velocity.
Reason (R): Speed is a scalar quantity while velocity depends on both magnitude and direction.
Q.20 Assertion (A): When a body moves with uniform velocity, its acceleration is non-zero.
Reason (R): Uniform velocity means velocity is changing at a uniform rate.

OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & DETAILED EXPLANATIONS
Q. No. Correct Conceptual Explanation & Syllabus Rationale
Q.1 (B) Distance $= 2\pi r = 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 7 = 44$ m. Returning to the starting point gives displacement $= 0$ m.
Q.2 (B) Along a straight line without reversing direction, distance equals displacement magnitude, so average speed equals average velocity.
Q.3 (B) $u = 0$, $v = 72 \times \frac{5}{18} = 20$ m/s, $t = 10$ s. $a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{20 - 0}{10} = 2.0$ m/s².
Q.4 (B) Slope of distance-time graph $= \frac{\Delta \text{distance}}{\Delta \text{time}} = \text{Speed}$.
Q.5 (B) Area under a velocity-time graph $= \text{velocity} \times \text{time} = \text{displacement}$ (or distance for uni-directional motion).
Q.6 (B) $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 = 0 + \frac{1}{2}(3)(4^2) = \frac{1}{2} \times 3 \times 16 = 24$ m.
Q.7 (C) $v^2 - u^2 = 2as$ relates final velocity ($v$), initial velocity ($u$), acceleration ($a$), and displacement ($s$).
Q.8 (B) In uniform circular motion, direction changes continuously; the resulting centripetal acceleration is directed radially inward.
Q.9 (B) $u = 20$ m/s, $v = 0$, $a = -2$ m/s². $v = u + at \Rightarrow 0 = 20 - 2t \Rightarrow 2t = 20 \Rightarrow t = 10$ s.
Q.10 (C) An odometer measures total distance covered in kilometers, whereas a speedometer measures instantaneous speed.
Q.11 (B) At max height $v = 0$. $v^2 = u^2 - 2gh \Rightarrow 0 = 20^2 - 2(10)h \Rightarrow 20h = 400 \Rightarrow h = 20$ m.
Q.12 (C) A horizontal line on a displacement-time graph means displacement does not change as time advances, so the object is stationary.
Q.13 (B) $v^2 = u^2 + 2as \Rightarrow 30^2 = 10^2 + 2a(80) \Rightarrow 900 - 100 = 160a \Rightarrow 800 = 160a \Rightarrow a = 5$ m/s².
Q.14 (C) Displacement has both magnitude and direction, making it a vector quantity.
Q.15 (A) $\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}} = \frac{220\text{ m}}{44\text{ s}} = 5$ m/s.
Q.16 (A) If an object returns to its starting point, initial and final coordinates coincide, making displacement zero while distance is $>0$.
Q.17 (A) Even though magnitude of velocity (speed) is fixed, change in direction means velocity changes, causing continuous centripetal acceleration.
Q.18 (C) Assertion is true. Reason is false because acceleration is the rate of change of *velocity*, not displacement (rate of change of displacement is velocity).
Q.19 (A) In uniform circular motion, speed is constant while direction changes, resulting in varying velocity.
Q.20 (D) Both statements are false. Uniform velocity means velocity remains constant ($v - u = 0$), so acceleration is strictly zero.
Q.21 (B) $a = \frac{20 - 0}{10} = 2$ m/s².
Q.22 (B) $\text{Distance} = v \times t = 20\text{ m/s} \times 30\text{ s} = 600$ m.
Q.23 (B) Retardation is the magnitude of negative acceleration: $a = \frac{0 - 20}{10} = -2$ m/s², so retardation $= 2$ m/s².
Q.24 (C) Total distance is the area of trapezoid: $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times (\text{sum of parallel sides}) \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \times (50 + 30) \times 20 = 40 \times 20 = 800$ m.
Q.25 (A) $\text{Average speed} = \frac{\text{Total distance}}{\text{Total time}} = \frac{800\text{ m}}{50\text{ s}} = 16$ m/s.
