SECTION A: CORE THEORETICAL PRINCIPLES (Q.1 TO Q.10) [10 MARKS]
Q.1

Assertion (A): The plasma membrane is referred to as a selectively permeable membrane.

Reason (R): It permits the entry and exit of certain specific substances while preventing the passage of other materials across the cell.

Q.2

Assertion (A): Plant cells can withstand much greater changes in the surrounding hypotonic medium than animal cells without bursting.

Reason (R): Plant cells possess a rigid, tensile cell wall made of cellulose that exerts counter wall pressure against incoming turgor pressure.

Q.3

Assertion (A): Mitochondria are often designated as the 'powerhouses of the cell'.

Reason (R): Mitochondria synthesize adenosine triphosphate (ATP), the universal chemical energy currency required for cellular metabolic activities.

Q.4

Assertion (A): Lysosomes are commonly known as the 'suicide bags' of the cell.

Reason (R): When a cell suffers irreparable metabolic damage or dies, its lysosomes rupture, releasing powerful hydrolytic enzymes that digest the host cell.

Q.5

Assertion (A): Ribosomes are found in both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.

Reason (R): Ribosomes are membrane-bound organelles containing hydrolytic digestive enzymes.

Q.6

Assertion (A): Mitochondria and chloroplasts are considered semi-autonomous organelles.

Reason (R): They contain their own circular DNA and 70S ribosomes, enabling them to synthesize some of their own structural proteins and replicate independently.

Q.7

Assertion (A): Chromosomes are visible as distinct rod-shaped structures only when a cell is actively preparing to divide.

Reason (R): In a non-dividing cell, genetic material exists in the form of an uncoiled, entangled thread-like network called chromatin.

Q.8

Assertion (A): The inner mitochondrial membrane is extensively folded into finger-like projections called cristae.

Reason (R): Cristae substantially increase the total surface area available for ATP-generating respiratory enzyme complexes.

Q.9

Assertion (A): Prokaryotic cells lack a defined, membrane-bound nuclear region.

Reason (R): The undefined nuclear region containing naked nucleic acids in prokaryotes is designated as a nucleoid.

Q.10

Assertion (A): Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum (SER) plays a crucial role in detoxifying many poisons and drugs in liver cells of vertebrates.

Reason (R): SER contains enzyme complexes that chemically convert lipid-soluble toxins into water-soluble compounds for excretion.

SECTION B: EXPERIMENTAL OBSERVATIONS & INFERENCES (Q.11 TO Q.18) [8 MARKS]
Q.11

Assertion (A): When dry raisins are soaked in pure water, they swell up after several hours.

Reason (R): Water enters the raisin cells via endosmosis because the external pure water is hypotonic relative to the concentrated cell sap.

Q.12

Assertion (A): When swollen raisins are subsequently transferred into a concentrated salt solution, they shrink.

Reason (R): Water molecules move out of the raisin cells into the hypertonic external medium via exosmosis.

Q.13

Assertion (A): A peel of Rhoeo leaf mounted in strong sugar solution displays plasmolysis under a microscope.

Reason (R): Severe loss of water via exosmosis causes the plant protoplast to shrink and pull away from the rigid cellulose cell wall.

Q.14

Assertion (A): Boiled Rhoeo leaf cells do not undergo plasmolysis when placed in a concentrated sugar solution.

Reason (R): Boiling kills the plant cells, destroying the semi-permeable properties of the cell membrane and denaturing transport proteins.

Q.15

Assertion (A): Red blood cells (RBCs) swell and burst (lyse) when placed in a hypotonic salt solution.

Reason (R): Animal cells lack a rigid cell wall to withstand increasing hydrostatic internal turgor pressure.

Q.16

Assertion (A): Amoeba acquires its food from the external aquatic environment through the process of endocytosis.

Reason (R): The plasma membrane of Amoeba is flexible and dynamic, allowing it to engulf external food particles by forming pseudopodia.

Q.17

Assertion (A): The Golgi apparatus is directly involved in the synthesis of structural proteins from amino acids.

Reason (R): Golgi apparatus consists of membrane-bound parallel stacks of flattened cisternae.

Q.18

Assertion (A): Chloroplasts are known as the kitchen of plant cells.

Reason (R): Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll pigments and photosynthetic machinery that synthesize glucose using sunlight, water, and $CO_2$.

SECTION C: ADVANCED NUANCES & MISCONCEPTIONS (Q.19 TO Q.25) [7 MARKS]
Q.19

Assertion (A): Endoplasmic Reticulum and Golgi apparatus operate in functional coordination within the eukaryotic endomembrane system.

Reason (R): Proteins and lipids synthesized in the ER are transported to the Golgi apparatus for chemical modification, packaging, and dispatch.

Q.20

Assertion (A): Leucoplasts are primary sites for photosynthesis in plant stems.

Reason (R): Leucoplasts are specialized colorless plastids that store starch, oils, and protein granules in non-photosynthetic plant tissues.

Q.21

Assertion (A): Large central vacuoles occupy 50% to 90% of the volume of mature plant cells.

Reason (R): The central vacuole maintains turgidity and rigidity in plant cells and serves as a reservoir for cellular waste and nutrients.

Q.22

Assertion (A): Diffusion is an active transport process that requires continuous breakdown of cellular ATP molecules.

Reason (R): In diffusion, molecules move spontaneously from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration along a gradient.

Q.23

Assertion (A): The cell is recognized as the structural and functional unit of all living organisms.

Reason (R): All basic metabolic processes of life (respiration, nutrition, excretion) can be independently performed by a single individual cell.

Q.24

Assertion (A): Nuclear membrane contains microscopic pores called nuclear pores.

Reason (R): Nuclear pores facilitate regulated bidirectional exchange of RNA and proteins between the nucleoplasm and cytoplasm.

Q.25

Assertion (A): Viruses do not show characteristics of life until they enter a living host cell.

Reason (R): Viruses lack cellular machinery and membranes of their own and utilize the host cell's metabolic machinery to multiply.
