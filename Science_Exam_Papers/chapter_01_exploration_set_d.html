SECTION A: CORE THEORETICAL PRINCIPLES (Q.1 TO Q.10) [10 MARKS]
Q.1

Assertion (A): The International System of Units (SI) is adopted universally across global scientific communities.

Reason (R): The SI system is built upon coherent, standardized base-10 decimal units that eliminate arbitrary regional conversion errors.

Q.2

Assertion (A): A simple pendulum of mass 50 g and another of mass 100 g having identical length exhibit the exact same time period of oscillation at a given place.

Reason (R): The time period of a simple pendulum is strictly independent of the mass and material of the oscillating bob.

Q.3

Assertion (A): Relative density of a solid substance has no physical unit.

Reason (R): Relative density is defined as the ratio of the density of the substance to the density of water at 4°C, causing all units to cancel out.

Q.4

Assertion (A): A measurement of 5.00 cm is more precise than a measurement of 5.0 cm.

Reason (R): Precision is determined by the least count of the measuring instrument; 5.00 cm indicates measurement up to two decimal places.

Q.5

Assertion (A): The period of oscillation of a simple pendulum increases when taken to the top of Mount Everest.

Reason (R): The acceleration due to gravity $g$ decreases with increasing altitude above the Earth's surface.

Q.6

Assertion (A): Parallax error occurs when an observer views the pointer or meniscus of a measuring instrument at an oblique angle.

Reason (R): To obtain an accurate reading, the observer's line of sight must always be positioned perpendicularly to the instrument scale.

Q.7

Assertion (A): The density of an iron nail is greater than the density of a massive wooden log.

Reason (R): Iron atoms possess greater atomic mass and are packed much more densely in a crystalline lattice than organic wood fibers.

Q.8

Assertion (A): A piece of cork floats on water, whereas an iron nail of the same volume sinks immediately.

Reason (R): Water exerts an upward buoyant force only on light objects like cork and exerts zero buoyant force on heavy objects like iron.

Q.9

Assertion (A): In scientific laboratory investigations, multiple trials of an experiment are recorded and averaged.

Reason (R): Averaging repeated measurements minimizes random observational errors and enhances experimental reliability.

Q.10

Assertion (A): When measuring the volume of water in a graduated cylinder, the reading should be taken at the upper convex meniscus.

Reason (R): Water wets the glass surface, forming a concave meniscus due to strong adhesive forces between water and glass molecules.

SECTION B: EXPERIMENTAL OBSERVATIONS & INFERENCES (Q.11 TO Q.18) [8 MARKS]
Q.11

Assertion (A): A graph plotted between $T^2$ (square of time period) and $L$ (length) for a simple pendulum is a straight line passing through the origin.

Reason (R): The square of the time period of a simple pendulum is directly proportional to its effective length ($T^2 \propto L$).

Q.12

Assertion (A): A vernier caliper can measure internal diameter, external diameter, and depth of a hollow cylinder.

Reason (R): A vernier caliper is equipped with upper jaws, lower jaws, and a metallic depth probe strip.

Q.13

Assertion (A): If a pendulum clock is transported from the North Pole to the Equator, it runs slower and loses time.

Reason (R): The value of $g$ is slightly smaller at the Equator than at the Poles due to the Earth's equatorial bulge and rotational centrifugal effect.

Q.14

Assertion (A): Zero error in a screw gauge is a systematic error that can be mathematically corrected by addition or subtraction.

Reason (R): Systematic errors consistently skew measurements in a single direction and arise from faulty calibration or zero offset.

Q.15

Assertion (A): A wire gauze with an asbestos or ceramic center is placed between a Bunsen burner flame and a glass beaker.

Reason (R): Direct exposure to concentrated flame causes localized uneven expansion of glass, leading to thermal fracture.

Q.16

Assertion (A): A body of mass 10 kg has a weight of 98 N on Earth and a weight of 0 N in deep interstellar space.

Reason (R): Mass is an invariant intrinsic property of matter, while weight is a gravitational force dependent on local acceleration due to gravity.

Q.17

Assertion (A): Water can never be used to extinguish a burning oil or petrol fire in the laboratory.

Reason (R): Water reacts violently with petrol to produce toxic chlorine gas.

Q.18

Assertion (A): A block of wood floats with 60% of its volume submerged in water. Its density is $0.6\text{ g/cm}^3$.

Reason (R): According to the principle of floatation, a floating body displaces a volume of liquid whose weight equals the total weight of the body.

SECTION C: ADVANCED NUANCES & MISCONCEPTIONS (Q.19 TO Q.25) [7 MARKS]
Q.19

Assertion (A): The amplitude of oscillation of a simple pendulum in a school lab must be kept small (under 10°).

Reason (R): The mathematical formula $T = 2\pi \sqrt{L/g}$ is strictly derived using the small-angle approximation $\sin\theta \approx \theta$.

Q.20

Assertion (A): While heating a test tube containing chemicals, its mouth should never be directed towards oneself or lab partners.

Reason (R): Boiling liquids in test tubes can superheat and violently eject hot reactive chemicals due to sudden vapor bubble formation (bumping).

Q.21

Assertion (A): If the length of a simple pendulum is increased by four times, its time period doubles.

Reason (R): The time period of a simple pendulum is inversely proportional to the square root of its length.

Q.22

Assertion (A): A hydrometer sinks deeper in kerosene than in pure water.

Reason (R): Kerosene has a lower density than water, requiring a larger volume of kerosene to be displaced to balance the hydrometer's weight.

Q.23

Assertion (A): The least count of an instrument can be decreased indefinitely without any practical physical limit.

Reason (R): Decreasing least count always increases the precision of any measurement.

Q.24

Assertion (A): During an experiment to determine the boiling point of water, pumice stones are added to the boiling flask.

Reason (R): Pumice stones introduce nucleation sites, promoting steady, smooth boiling and preventing explosive superheating.

Q.25

Assertion (A): In a controlled scientific experiment, only one independent variable should be altered at a time while holding all other variables constant.

Reason (R): If multiple variables are changed simultaneously, it becomes scientifically impossible to determine which specific factor caused the observed effect.
