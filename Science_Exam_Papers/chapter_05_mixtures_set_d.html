SECTION A: CORE THEORETICAL PRINCIPLES (Q.1 TO Q.10) [10 MARKS]
Q.1

Assertion (A): A true solution of sodium chloride in water is completely homogeneous and clear.

Reason (R): In a true solution, solute particles are broken down to individual ions or molecules having diameter less than 1 nm ($10^{-9}$ m).

Q.2

Assertion (A): A beam of light passed through a true copper sulfate solution does not show a visible illuminated path.

Reason (R): Particles in a true solution are too small ($< 1$ nm) to scatter visible light rays (no Tyndall effect).

Q.3

Assertion (A): Milk is classified as a colloid, specifically an emulsion.

Reason (R): In milk, tiny liquid fat droplets are dispersed uniformly throughout a continuous liquid aqueous medium.

Q.4

Assertion (A): A suspension is a heterogeneous mixture that is thermodynamically unstable over time.

Reason (R): The dispersed solute particles in a suspension are larger than 100 nm and gradually settle down under gravity upon standing.

Q.5

Assertion (A): Brass is considered a homogeneous mixture (solid solution) rather than a chemical compound.

Reason (R): Brass retains the individual chemical properties of copper and zinc, and its composition can vary within certain percentage ranges.

Q.6

Assertion (A): The solubility of most solid solutes like potassium nitrate ($KNO_3$) in water increases with an increase in temperature.

Reason (R): The dissolution of potassium nitrate in water is an endothermic process; according to Le Chatelier's principle, adding heat favors dissolution.

Q.7

Assertion (A): A saturated solution becomes supersaturated upon heating to a higher temperature without adding more solute.

Reason (R): Solubility increases with temperature, so a saturated solution becomes unsaturated when heated.

Q.8

Assertion (A): Burning of a candle involves both physical and chemical changes simultaneously.

Reason (R): Melting of candle wax and vaporizing are physical changes, while combustion of wax vapor into carbon dioxide and water vapor is a chemical change.

Q.9

Assertion (A): When iron filings and sulfur powder are heated strongly together, a compound iron sulfide (FeS) is formed.

Reason (R): The product FeS can be separated back into iron and sulfur using a simple bar magnet.

Q.10

Assertion (A): Colloidal particles do not settle down at the bottom of the container when left undisturbed.

Reason (R): Colloidal particles carry similar electrical charges that repel each other and undergo continuous random zigzag Brownian motion.

SECTION B: EXPERIMENTAL OBSERVATIONS & INFERENCES (Q.11 TO Q.18) [8 MARKS]
Q.11

Assertion (A): When sunlight enters a dark dusty room through a narrow chink, the path of the beam becomes brilliantly visible.

Reason (R): Suspended airborne dust and smoke particles scatter visible light rays in all directions, demonstrating the Tyndall effect.

Q.12

Assertion (A): Blood cannot be separated into its cellular components by simple ordinary paper filtration.

Reason (R): Blood cells and plasma proteins are too small and flexible, easily passing through ordinary filter paper pores along with water.

Q.13

Assertion (A): Centrifugation is used in diagnostic laboratories to separate blood cells from liquid blood plasma.

Reason (R): High-speed spinning creates intense centrifugal force that drives denser cellular components to the bottom while less dense plasma stays on top.

Q.14

Assertion (A): Fog and mist are classified as liquid aerosols.

Reason (R): In fog, liquid water droplets are dispersed in a gaseous dispersion medium (air).

Q.15

Assertion (A): Rusting of iron is a purely physical change.

Reason (R): During rusting, iron reacts with atmospheric oxygen and moisture to produce hydrated ferric oxide ($Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O$), a chemically distinct substance.

Q.16

Assertion (A): To prepare a 10% (by mass) aqueous solution of sodium chloride, 10 g of NaCl is dissolved in 90 g of water.

Reason (R): Mass percentage of solute is calculated as $\frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Mass of Solution}} \times 100$, where mass of solution is $10 + 90 = 100$ g.

Q.17

Assertion (A): A suspension scatters a beam of light as long as its particles remain suspended in the liquid.

Reason (R): When the suspension settles completely at the bottom of the beaker, the supernatant clear liquid ceases to scatter light.

Q.18

Assertion (A): Pure distilled water is classified as a homogeneous mixture.

Reason (R): Pure water consists of hydrogen and oxygen chemically combined in a fixed ratio of $1:8$ by mass and cannot be separated physically.

SECTION C: ADVANCED NUANCES & MISCONCEPTIONS (Q.19 TO Q.25) [7 MARKS]
Q.19

Assertion (A): Gases like oxygen and carbon dioxide dissolve to a measurable extent in water.

Reason (R): Dissolved oxygen in natural water bodies is essential for the aquatic respiration and survival of fish and aquatic plants.

Q.20

Assertion (A): Brownian motion provides direct macroscopic evidence for the kinetic nature and continuous random movement of fluid molecules.

Reason (R): Colloidal particles undergo erratic collisions with rapidly moving, invisible molecules of the dispersion medium.

Q.21

Assertion (A): Shaving cream is a colloid belonging to the category of foam.

Reason (R): In shaving cream, gas bubbles are dispersed within a liquid dispersion medium.

Q.22

Assertion (A): A mixture of kerosene and water can be separated using a separating funnel.

Reason (R): Kerosene and water are immiscible liquids that separate into two distinct layers based on their density difference.

Q.23

Assertion (A): During crystallization, pure crystals of copper sulfate are obtained from an impure solution.

Reason (R): Crystallization is superior to simple evaporation to dryness because evaporation can decompose heat-sensitive salts or leave charred residues.

Q.24

Assertion (A): Tincture of iodine is a liquid solution prepared by dissolving solid iodine in alcohol.

Reason (R): In tincture of iodine, iodine acts as the solute and ethyl alcohol acts as the solvent.

Q.25

Assertion (A): All mixtures exhibit sharp, fixed melting and boiling points.

Reason (R): Substances with fixed melting and boiling points are pure elements or chemical compounds.
