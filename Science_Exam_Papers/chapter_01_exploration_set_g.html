OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET G)
Q. No. Correct Rigorous Scientific Solution & Formula Derivation
Q.1 (C) Volume $V = x^3$. The relative percentage error is $\frac{\Delta V}{V} \times 100 = 3 \times \left(\frac{\Delta x}{x}\right) \times 100 = 3 \times \left(\frac{0.1}{10.0}\right) \times 100 = 3.0\%$.
Q.2 (A) $\text{Slope} = \frac{4\pi^2}{g} \Rightarrow g = \frac{4\pi^2}{\text{Slope}} = \frac{4(3.1416)^2}{4.00} = (3.1416)^2 \approx 9.87\text{ m/s}^2$.
Q.3 (A) Volume of metal in hollow sphere $= \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 - \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi(R^3 - r^3)$. Density $\rho = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Volume}} = \frac{3M}{4\pi(R^3 - r^3)}$.
Q.4 (D) Inside an orbiting satellite, effective gravity $g_{eff} = 0$ (weightlessness). $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/0} \rightarrow \infty$; the pendulum does not oscillate.
Q.5 (A) $\text{LC} = \frac{1\text{ MSD}}{n} = \frac{0.5\text{ mm}}{20} = 0.025\text{ mm}$.
Q.6 (A) Fraction submerged $= \frac{\rho_{ice}}{\rho_{seawater}} = \frac{0.92}{1.03} \approx 0.893 = 89.3\%$.
Q.7 (A) Volume $= 4 \times 5 \times 6 = 120\text{ cm}^3$. Density $= 120\text{ g} / 120\text{ cm}^3 = 1.00\text{ g/cm}^3$. Being equal to water density, it remains fully submerged in equilibrium (100%).
Q.8 (B) Fractional period change $\frac{\Delta T}{T} = -\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta g}{g} = +0.1\%$. Time lost per day $= 0.001 \times 86400\text{ s} = 86.4\text{ seconds}$.
Q.9 (B) When the circular scale zero is above the reference line, the error is negative ($-5 \times 0.01 = -0.05\text{ mm}$) and must be added to observed readings.
Q.10 (A) From $F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} \Rightarrow G = \frac{F r^2}{m_1 m_2}$, unit is $\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$.
Q.11 (A) All digits in the mantissa (9, 1, 0, 9) are significant; the power of ten does not affect significant figure count (4 significant figures).
Q.12 (B) Screw gauge (LC 0.01 mm) > Vernier (LC 0.1 mm) > Meter rule (LC 1 mm). Finer least count equals higher precision.
Q.13 (A) $\text{Density} = \text{Mass} / \text{Volume} = [\text{M}] / [\text{L}^3] = [\text{ML}^{-3}]$.
Q.14 (C) Volumetric expansion is 3 times linear expansion: $\frac{\Delta V}{V} \approx 3 \times \frac{\Delta L}{L} = 3 \times 0.1\% = 0.3\%$.
Q.15 (B) By Newton's third law, if water exerts an upward buoyant force $F_b$ on the ball, the ball exerts an equal downward force $F_b$ on the water, increasing the scale reading.
Q.16 (B) Since $T \propto g^{-1/2}$, fractional change $\frac{\Delta T}{T} \approx -\frac{1}{2} \frac{\Delta g}{g} = -\frac{1}{2}(+4\%) = -2\%$.
Q.17 (B) $\frac{1}{2} \rho_A = \frac{2}{3} \rho_B \Rightarrow \frac{\rho_A}{\rho_B} = \frac{2/3}{1/2} = \frac{4}{3}$.
Q.18 (C) '4.000 m' measures to the nearest millimeter (0.001 m), representing the smallest least count and highest precision.
Q.19 (C) $12.2 \times 3.4 = 41.48\text{ cm}^2$. The least number of significant figures in the data is 2 (in 3.4 cm), so the result is rounded to 2 significant figures: 41 cm².
Q.20 (C) Floating ice displaces water equal to its own mass. When melted, the resulting water volume exactly equals the submerged volume it previously displaced.
Q.21 (A) $T = 2\pi \sqrt{9.8 / 9.8} = 2\pi(1) = 2\pi \approx 6.28\text{ s}$.
Q.22 (A) $\text{Least count} = \frac{1\text{ mm}}{50} = 0.02\text{ mm}$.
Q.23 (C) $1\text{ g/cm}^3 = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3 \Rightarrow 19.3 \times 1000 = 19300\text{ kg/m}^3$.
Q.24 (B) In denser liquids, the hydrometer floats higher (sinks less), so lower numerical readings are at the top and higher readings at the bottom.
Q.25 (C) Counting discrete whole events (integers) has no instrumental scale error, unlike analog continuous scale instruments.
