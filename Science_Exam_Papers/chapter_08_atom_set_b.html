(B) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is NOT the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

Q.16 Assertion (A): An atom remains stable and its electrons do not spiral into the nucleus.
Reason (R): According to Neils Bohr, electrons revolve only in discrete stationary orbits without radiating electromagnetic energy.
Q.17 Assertion (A): Isotopes of chlorine, $^{35}_{17}\text{Cl}$ and $^{37}_{17}\text{Cl}$, have identical chemical properties.
Reason (R): Chemical properties are governed by valence electrons, and both isotopes have identical atomic number 17 and electronic configuration (2, 8, 7).
Q.18 Assertion (A): The atomic mass of an element is often a fractional decimal number (e.g., Chlorine is 35.5 u).
Reason (R): Naturally occurring elements often exist as mixtures of two or more isotopes with different natural abundance percentages.
Q.19 Assertion (A): Noble gases (Helium, Argon, Neon) have zero valency.
Reason (R): Their outermost shells are completely filled, leaving no tendency to gain, lose, or share electrons.
Q.20 Assertion (A): Thomson's model predicted that alpha particles would easily pass through gold atoms with only minimal deflection.
Reason (R): In Thomson's model, positive charge and mass were thought to be spread thinly throughout the entire volume of the atom.

OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & IN-DEPTH EXPLANATIONS (SET B)
Q. No. Correct In-Depth Conceptual & Numerical Solution
Q.1 (B) $V \propto r^3$. Ratio $= \left(\frac{10^{-10}}{10^{-15}}\right)^3 = (10^5)^3 = 10^{15}$. The atom is mostly empty space.
Q.2 (A) Electrons are identical in all matter, giving fixed $e/m$. Positive canal ray particles are stripped residual gas cations; heavier gases produce heavier ions with lower $e/m$.
Q.3 (A) $\text{Mass} = \frac{(24 \times 79) + (25 \times 10) + (26 \times 11)}{100} = \frac{1896 + 250 + 286}{100} = \frac{2432}{100} = 24.32$ u.
Q.4 (A) According to the Bohr-Bury octet rule, the outermost valence shell cannot hold more than 8 electrons; hence, the 19th electron starts the N-shell ($4s$ level).
Q.5 (B) $Z = 17$ (Chlorine). Mass number $A = 17 + 18 = 35$. With 18 electrons (one extra than 17 protons), it carries a net $-1$ charge ($^{35}_{17}\text{Cl}^-$).
Q.6 (B) Argon ($Z=18$) and Calcium ($Z=20$) both have mass number 40; they are isobars.
Q.7 (B) $\text{Neutrons} = A - Z = 3 - 1 = 2$.
Q.8 (B) James Chadwick observed penetrating neutral radiation consisting of uncharged particles with mass equal to a proton: neutrons ($^1_0 n$).
Q.9 (D) Element S has 6 valence electrons; to complete its octet, it needs $8 - 6 = 2$ electrons. Valency $= 2$ (Oxygen).
Q.10 (A) Uranium-235 undergoes controlled thermal neutron-induced nuclear fission inside commercial nuclear power reactors.
Q.11 (C) Maximum capacity $= 2 n^2 = 2(4^2) = 2(16) = 32$ electrons.
Q.12 (D) All three have 10 electrons ($11-1=10$, $12-2=10$, $8+2=10$), matching Neon's 2, 8 configuration.
Q.13 (B) Criss-cross valency method: $X^3$ and $Y^2$ yields $X_2 Y_3$.
Q.14 (A) $1.675 \times 10^{-24} \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} \approx 1.008$ g.
Q.15 (A) The thyroid gland absorbs iodine; radioactive Iodine-131 is used to image and treat goiter and thyroid carcinoma.
Q.16 (A) Bohr postulated quantized non-radiating orbits, overcoming the fatal flaw in Rutherford's model where accelerated charges radiate energy.
Q.17 (A) Chemical reactivity depends strictly on outer valence electron configuration, which is identical in both isotopes.
Q.18 (A) Average atomic mass is a weighted average of isotopic masses based on natural fractional abundances (e.g. 75% 35Cl + 25% 37Cl = 35.5 u).
Q.19 (A) A complete duplet (He) or octet (Ne, Ar) imparts exceptional thermodynamic stability, resulting in zero chemical valency.
Q.20 (A) Rutherford expected minor deflections because Thomson's diffuse positive sphere could not exert sufficient concentrated electric field to deflect fast alpha particles backward.
Q.21 (B) $A = \text{protons} + \text{neutrons} = 6 + 8 = 14$.
Q.22 (A) Species P and Q both have $Z = 6$ (protons), but different neutron counts (6 vs 8); they are isotopes of Carbon ($^{12}_6C$ and $^{14}_6C$).
Q.23 (B) Species Q ($A = 6+8=14$) and Species R ($A = 7+7=14$) have different atomic numbers (6 and 7) but the same mass number (14); they are isobars.
Q.24 (B) $Z = 8$ (Oxygen). It has 8 protons ($+8$) and 10 electrons ($-10$), giving a net charge of $-2$ ($O^{2-}$ anion).
Q.25 (A) Carbon-14 decays with a half-life of 5,730 years, utilized to date ancient organic artifacts up to 50,000 years old.
