READING PASSAGE & EXPERIMENTAL SETUP:

Case Study: Industrial Cryogenic Separation of Air
To produce pure liquid oxygen for medical cylinders, atmospheric air is passed through high-efficiency dust filters. It is then compressed under high pressure (200 atmospheres) and cooled by refrigeration pipes to remove water vapor and carbon dioxide (which would freeze and clog the machinery). The cold compressed air is expanded rapidly through a jet nozzle, causing Joule-Thomson cooling to -200°C where air liquefies into liquid air. The liquid air enters a tall fractional distillation column.

Q.21 Why must carbon dioxide and moisture be removed from the air before cooling below -78°C?
Q.22 What physical property difference allows the separation of nitrogen, argon, and oxygen in the column?
Q.23 Which gaseous component collects at the very bottom of the distillation column as a liquid?
Q.24 Is the production of liquid oxygen from atmospheric air a physical or chemical process?
Q.25 Which gas comprises approximately 0.9% of dry air and boils at -186°C between nitrogen and oxygen?

OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & IN-DEPTH EXPLANATIONS (SET B)
Q. No. Correct In-Depth Conceptual & Numerical Solution
Q.1 (B) For 100 g water, crystal mass $= 106 - 32 = 74$ g. For 50 g water, mass precipitated $= 74 / 2 = 37$ g.
Q.2 (B) Let mass of salt be $x$. $\frac{x}{x + 180} = 0.10 \Rightarrow x = 0.10x + 18 \Rightarrow 0.90x = 18 \Rightarrow x = 20$ g.
Q.3 (B) $R_f = \frac{\text{Distance travelled by solute}}{\text{Distance travelled by solvent}} = \frac{4.8}{8.0} = 0.60$.
Q.4 (B) 1. Sublimation vaporizes $NH_4Cl$ leaving sand + salt. 2. Adding water dissolves salt; filtration removes insoluble sand. 3. Evaporation/crystallization recovers solid NaCl.
Q.5 (B) Glass beads supply a huge cool surface area where rising vapors repeatedly condense and re-evaporate, creating a sharp temperature gradient and enriching the vapor in the more volatile component.
Q.6 (C) A gel is a colloid where a liquid is trapped in a solid matrix (dispersed phase: liquid, dispersion medium: solid, e.g. cheese, butter, jelly).
Q.7 (B) Thermal Brownian motion keeps tiny colloidal particles suspended, while mutual electrostatic repulsion of like surface charges prevents agglomeration.
Q.8 (B) Rusting converts iron into hydrated iron(III) oxide ($Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O$), forming new chemical bonds through irreversible oxidation.
Q.9 (B) Evaporating to dryness bakes impurities onto the crystals and can decompose heat-sensitive salts. Crystallization leaves impurities dissolved in the mother liquor.
Q.10 (B) Acetone has higher vapor pressure and lower boiling point (56°C), boiling first and condensing pure into the receiver while water remains in the flask.
Q.11 (B) Dissolution of most solid salts is endothermic (absorbs heat); according to Le Chatelier's principle, higher temperature drives higher solubility.
Q.12 (B) Nitrogen has the lowest boiling point (-196°C) and boils first, followed by Argon (-186°C), leaving liquid Oxygen (-183°C) at the base.
Q.13 (B) Suspension particles ($>100$ nm) are larger than filter paper pores, so they are trapped as residue on the paper.
Q.14 (B) Brass is a homogeneous solid solution alloy consisting of approximately 70% copper and 30% zinc. (Bronze is copper and tin).
Q.15 (B) $\text{Total volume} = 50 + 150 = 200$ mL. $\text{Volume \%} = \frac{50}{200} \times 100 = 25.0\%$.
Q.16 (A) Because dispersed fat and casein particles are 1-100 nm, they scatter incident photons, producing a bright visible beam path (Tyndall effect).
Q.17 (A) Elevation of boiling point is a colligative property: non-volatile solute molecules occupy surface sites, lowering vapor pressure and raising boiling point.
Q.18 (D) Assertion is false: Separating funnels only work for *immiscible* liquids (like oil and water). Reason is true (ethanol and water are completely miscible).
Q.19 (A) Wax melts (solid to liquid) and vaporizes physically, while flame combustion chemically oxidizes hydrocarbon wax into carbon dioxide, water vapor, and soot.
Q.20 (A) Centrifugal acceleration forces denser particles (erythrocytes, leukocytes) outward and down, separating them cleanly from blood plasma.
Q.21 (B) $CO_2$ sublimes at -78.5°C and water freezes at 0°C; solid ice and dry ice would choke cryogenic pipes.
Q.22 (B) Fractional distillation separates liquefied gases based on boiling point differences ($-196^\circ\text{C}, -186^\circ\text{C}, -183^\circ\text{C}$).
Q.23 (B) Oxygen has the highest boiling point (-183°C) among the three, so it remains liquid at the bottom while nitrogen vaporizes up.
Q.24 (B) Condensation, expansion, and fractional distillation are physical phase transitions; $O_2$ and $N_2$ molecules remain chemically intact.
Q.25 (C) Argon is a noble gas constituting $\approx 0.93\%$ of atmospheric air with a boiling point of -186°C.
