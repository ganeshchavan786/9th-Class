SECTION A: CORE THEORETICAL PRINCIPLES (Q.1 TO Q.10) [10 MARKS]
Q.1

Assertion (A): Scientifically, work is said to be done only when a force produces displacement in the direction of the force.

Reason (R): Work is mathematically defined as the scalar dot product of force and displacement vectors ($W = F \cdot s = F s \cos\theta$).

Q.2

Assertion (A): A coolie carrying a heavy 50 kg trunk on his head while walking horizontally across a railway platform does zero work against gravity.

Reason (R): The gravitational force acts vertically downward while the displacement is purely horizontal (angle $\theta = 90^\circ$; $\cos 90^\circ = 0$).

Q.3

Assertion (A): The work done by the gravitational force on an artificial satellite revolving in a circular orbit around the Earth is zero.

Reason (R): The gravitational centripetal force acts towards the center of the Earth, which is perpendicular to the satellite's instantaneous tangential displacement at every point.

Q.4

Assertion (A): Work done can be negative.

Reason (R): When a force acts in a direction opposite to the direction of motion (such as kinetic friction or gravity on an upward-moving stone), the angle $\theta = 180^\circ$ and $\cos 180^\circ = -1$.

Q.5

Assertion (A): The kinetic energy of an object quadruples if its speed is doubled.

Reason (R): Kinetic energy is directly proportional to the square of the speed ($E_k = \frac{1}{2}m v^2$).

Q.6

Assertion (A): Energy is a scalar quantity, and its SI unit is the Joule (J).

Reason (R): One Joule is defined as the amount of work done when a force of 1 Newton displaces an object through a distance of 1 meter along the line of action of the force.

Q.7

Assertion (A): The total mechanical energy of an oscillating simple pendulum remains conserved in the absence of air resistance.

Reason (R): As the pendulum oscillates, potential energy at extreme positions continuously converts into kinetic energy at the mean position and vice versa, keeping $E_k + E_p = \text{constant}$.

Q.8

Assertion (A): Power is defined as the rate of doing work or the rate of transfer of energy.

Reason (R): The SI unit of power is the Watt (W), where 1 Watt equals 1 Joule per second ($1\text{ W} = 1\text{ J/s}$).

Q.9

Assertion (A): Commercial electricity consumption is billed in kilowatt-hours (kWh) rather than Joules.

Reason (R): The Joule is an extremely small unit of energy; $1\text{ kWh} = 1000\text{ W} \times 3600\text{ s} = 3.6 \times 10^6\text{ J}$ (3.6 MegaJoules).

Q.10

Assertion (A): A compressed spring in a toy gun possesses gravitational potential energy.

Reason (R): Energy possessed by a body due to change in its physical shape or configuration is elastic potential energy.

SECTION B: EXPERIMENTAL OBSERVATIONS & INFERENCES (Q.11 TO Q.18) [8 MARKS]
Q.11

Assertion (A): When a ball is dropped from a height $h$, its velocity just before striking the ground is $v = \sqrt{2gh}$, independent of its mass.

Reason (R): According to conservation of mechanical energy, loss in gravitational potential energy equals gain in kinetic energy ($mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$), and mass cancels out.

Q.12

Assertion (A): A person pushing against a massive rigid concrete wall until exhausted performs zero scientific work on the wall.

Reason (R): Although the person exerts considerable muscular force and burns biochemical calories, the physical displacement of the wall is zero ($s = 0$).

Q.13

Assertion (A): A simple machine like a crowbar or lever can multiply energy.

Reason (R): A machine can multiply force or speed, but according to the law of conservation of energy, work output can never exceed work input.

Q.14

Assertion (A): In a class 1 lever (such as a crowbar or pair of scissors), the mechanical advantage can be greater than, equal to, or less than 1.

Reason (R): In a class 1 lever, the fulcrum lies between the effort and the load, allowing the effort arm to be longer than, equal to, or shorter than the load arm.

Q.15

Assertion (A): An electric crane of power 2 kW can lift a load twice as fast as a crane of power 1 kW.

Reason (R): Power is directly proportional to the speed at which a constant force is applied ($P = F \times v$).

Q.16

Assertion (A): The efficiency of any real practical machine is always strictly less than 100% (or less than 1).

Reason (R): Some fraction of the input energy is unavoidably dissipated as heat and sound to overcome friction between moving mechanical parts.

Q.17

Assertion (A): A heavy truck moving at 30 km/h requires four times the braking distance to stop compared to when it moves at 15 km/h under identical braking force.

Reason (R): According to the work-energy theorem, the work done by braking force ($F \times d$) must equal the initial kinetic energy ($\frac{1}{2}m v^2$), so stopping distance $d \propto v^2$.

Q.18

Assertion (A): When a stone is thrown vertically upward, the work done by gravity during the upward ascent is negative.

Reason (R): The displacement vector is upward while the gravitational force vector acts downward, making the angle between them 180°.

SECTION C: ADVANCED NUANCES & MISCONCEPTIONS (Q.19 TO Q.25) [7 MARKS]
Q.19

Assertion (A): A single movable pulley has an ideal mechanical advantage of 2.

Reason (R): In a single movable pulley, the load is supported by two parallel segments of the rope, halving the effort required to lift the load.

Q.20

Assertion (A): When a fast-moving bullet embeds itself into a stationary wooden block, total mechanical energy is conserved.

Reason (R): In an inelastic collision, total linear momentum is conserved, but mechanical energy is partially converted into internal thermal energy and sound.

Q.21

Assertion (A): A stretched rubber bow possesses elastic potential energy that can propel an arrow forward.

Reason (R): Work done in deforming the elastic bowstring against restoring intermolecular forces is stored as potential energy.

Q.22

Assertion (A): If a man walks up a flight of stairs in 10 seconds, he performs more mechanical work than if he walks up the same stairs in 30 seconds.

Reason (R): The mechanical work done against gravity depends solely on the vertical height and body mass ($W = mgh$) and is independent of time.

Q.23

Assertion (A): In a hydroelectric power station, potential energy of water stored at high elevation is converted into electrical energy.

Reason (R): Stored gravitational potential energy converts to kinetic energy as water falls, which spins turbines connected to electric generators.

Q.24

Assertion (A): The work done by kinetic friction on a sliding block is always independent of the path taken between two points.

Reason (R): Friction is a non-conservative force whose work depends directly on the total path length traversed.

Q.25

Assertion (A): A wheelbarrow is an example of a class 2 lever.

Reason (R): In a wheelbarrow, the load is situated between the fulcrum (wheel axle) and the effort (handles), resulting in a mechanical advantage always greater than 1.
