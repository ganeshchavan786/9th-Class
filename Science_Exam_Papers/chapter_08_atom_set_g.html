OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET G)
Q. No. Correct Rigorous Scientific Solution & Formula Derivation
Q.1 (A) Rutherford's classical scattering differential cross-section formula: $N(\theta) \propto \csc^4(\theta/2) = \frac{1}{\sin^4(\theta/2)}$.
Q.2 (A) At closest approach, kinetic energy converts to electrostatic potential energy: $E_k = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{(2e)(Ze)}{d} \Rightarrow d = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{2Ze^2}{E_k}$.
Q.3 (A) Bohr's quantum condition: Angular momentum is an integral multiple of $h/2\pi$ ($mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi}$).
Q.4 (A) $\Delta E = E_2 - E_1 = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \Rightarrow \lambda = \frac{hc}{E_2 - E_1}$.
Q.5 (B) Number of neutrons $N = A - Z = 232 - 90 = 142$.
Q.6 (A) $Z = 15$ identifies Phosphorus. It has 3 extra electrons ($18 - 15 = 3$), giving a trivalent anion $P^{3-}$.
Q.7 (A) Isobars have different atomic numbers but identical mass numbers ($A = 40$ in Argon and Calcium).
Q.8 (A) In $^{14}_6C$, $N = 14 - 6 = 8$. In $^{16}_8O$, $N = 16 - 8 = 8$. Identical neutron count defines isotones.
Q.9 (A) $10 x + 11(1 - x) = 10.8 \Rightarrow 10x + 11 - 11x = 10.8 \Rightarrow 11 - x = 10.8 \Rightarrow x = 0.20 = 20\%$.
Q.10 (B) Capacity $= 2n^2 = 2(4)^2 = 2 \times 16 = 32$ electrons.
Q.11 (A) Carbon-14 ($^{14}C$) is a beta-emitting radioactive isotope with a half-life of 5,730 years used in radiocarbon dating.
Q.12 (B) Different atoms in the macroscopic gas sample undergo electronic transitions across different energy levels, producing distinct spectral lines.
Q.13 (A) An electron has fundamental negative charge $e = -1.602 \times 10^{-19}$ C and rest mass $9.109 \times 10^{-31}$ kg.
Q.14 (A) Silicon shares 4 valence electrons to complete its octet, exhibiting a valency of 4 (tetravalent).
Q.15 (B) Criss-crossing valencies ($Al^{3+}$ and $SO_4^{2-}$) gives $Al_2(SO_4)_3$.
Q.16 (A) Criss-crossing valencies ($NH_4^{1+}$ and $CO_3^{2-}$) gives $(NH_4)_2CO_3$.
Q.17 (A) Neutrinos are neutral leptons with near-zero rest mass emitted during radioactive beta decay.
Q.18 (A) Nuclear radius $R = R_0 A^{1/3}$, where $R_0 \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-15}\text{ m}$ (1.2 fermi).
Q.19 (A) Mass defect $\Delta m$ represents binding energy released when protons and neutrons bind to form a nucleus.
Q.20 (A) Potassium ($K$, $Z = 19$) has 19 electrons arranged as $2, 8, 8, 1$.
Q.21 (B) Weighted averaging across multiple isotopic fractions yields fractional atomic weights (e.g., Cl = 35.5 u).
Q.22 (A) Tritium ($^3_1H$) has 1 proton and 2 neutrons ($N/Z = 2$), making it an unstable beta-emitter with half-life 12.3 years.
Q.23 (A) Heavy water is deuterium oxide ($D_2O$), where hydrogen is replaced by its heavier isotope deuterium ($^2_1H$).
Q.24 (A) Thomson's diffuse positive cloud could not exert the concentrated Coulomb repulsion required to deflect alpha particles at large angles.
Q.25 (A) The ground state ($n=1$, K-shell) is the lowest energy, thermodynamically most stable electronic state.
