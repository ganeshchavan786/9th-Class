Q.1 (A) Particles in a true solution are individual ions or molecules smaller than 1 nanometer ($< 10^{-9}$ m).

Q.3 (B) John Tyndall discovered that colloidal particles scatter light, illuminating the beam path (Tyndall effect).
Q.4 (B) In milk, liquid fat globules are dispersed in an aqueous liquid medium, forming an emulsion.
Q.5 (B) Brownian motion is the random zigzag movement of colloidal particles resulting from continuous collisions with solvent molecules.
Q.6 (B) Simple and fractional distillation separate miscible liquids that have distinct boiling points.
Q.7 (A) Paper chromatography separates soluble pigments based on differential solubility and adsorption rates.
Q.8 (A) Ammonium chloride, camphor, naphthalene, and iodine sublime directly from solid to vapor upon heating.
Q.9 (B) Tincture of iodine is an antiseptic solution of 2–7% elemental iodine in ethyl alcohol and water.
Q.10 (B) Rusting of iron forms a chemically new compound (hydrated ferric oxide), which is irreversible.
Q.11 (A) $\text{Mass \%} = \frac{40}{40 + 360} \times 100 = \frac{40}{400} \times 100 = 10\%$.
Q.12 (A) Centrifugation spins the milk rapidly, forcing denser skim milk to the outside while lighter fat cream stays at the center.
Q.13 (A) In fog and clouds, liquid water droplets are dispersed in a gaseous dispersion medium (air).
Q.14 (B) A saturated solution holds the maximum possible equilibrium amount of dissolved solute at a given temperature.
Q.15 (B) Dissolution of most solid salts is endothermic; increasing temperature increases solubility.
Q.16 (B) Dissolution of gases in liquids is exothermic; heating expels dissolved gas molecules, decreasing solubility.
Q.17 (A) Crystallization purifies heat-sensitive salts without thermal decomposition or trapping soluble contaminants.
Q.18 (A) A separating funnel allows the denser lower liquid to be drained via a stopcock from the lighter floating liquid.
Q.19 (A) Pure distilled water is a chemical compound with a fixed definite proportion of hydrogen and oxygen ($1:8$ by mass).
Q.20 (A) Shaving cream consists of air gas bubbles dispersed within a soapy liquid medium (foam).
Q.21 (A) In a gel, a liquid is trapped and dispersed inside a continuous solid network.
Q.22 (A) Fractional distillation of liquefied air separates nitrogen ($-196^\circ\text{C}$), argon ($-186^\circ\text{C}$), and oxygen ($-183^\circ\text{C}$).
Q.23 (C) Melting and vaporizing wax are physical changes; combustion of wax vapor into $CO_2$ and $H_2O$ is a chemical change.
Q.24 (B) Nitrogen makes up approximately 78.08% of dry atmospheric air by volume.
Q.25 (A) Solid sulfur dissolves in liquid non-polar carbon disulfide; sulfur is solute and $CS_2$ is solvent.
