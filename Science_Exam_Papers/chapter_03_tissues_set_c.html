Directions for Q16 to Q20: Select the correct option from the choices given below:
(A) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
(B) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is NOT the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
(C) Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
(D) Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true (or both are false).
Q.16 Assertion (A): Transpiration pull is the major driving force for the ascent of water in tall coniferous trees exceeding 100 meters.
Reason (R): Cohesion between water molecules and adhesion to xylem walls maintain an unbroken capillary water column under tension.
Q.17 Assertion (A): Involuntary smooth muscles are found in the walls of blood vessels and bronchi.
Reason (R): Smooth muscle contractions help regulate blood pressure and airflow without conscious voluntary intervention.
Q.18 Assertion (A): Intercellular matrix is absent in epithelial tissue.
Reason (R): Epithelial cells are compactly packed on a non-cellular basement membrane with specialized cell junctions.
Q.19 Assertion (A): Companion cells are present in gymnosperm phloem.
Reason (R): Gymnosperms possess sieve cells and albuminous cells instead of sieve tubes and companion cells.
Q.20 Assertion (A): Blood platelets (thrombocytes) are essential for blood coagulation.
Reason (R): Platelets aggregate at injury sites and release thromboplastin to initiate the clotting cascade.

READING PASSAGE & EXPERIMENTAL INVESTIGATION:

Case Study: Microscopic Examination of Plant Meristematic Zones
A biology student prepares a longitudinal section (LS) of an onion root tip stained with acetocarmine. Under the microscope, she identifies four distinct longitudinal zones from the tip upward:
1. Zone I (Apex): A thimble-like cap of loose parenchymatous cells covering the extreme apex.
2. Zone II (Meristematic zone): Densely packed small isodiametric cells with thin cellulose walls, dense granular cytoplasm, and large nuclei, with several cells showing mitotic chromosomes.
3. Zone III (Elongation zone): Cells rapidly increasing in length with large central vacuoles forming.
4. Zone IV (Maturation zone): Cells developing thickened walls and forming root hairs.

Q.21 What is the primary function of Zone I (Root cap)?
Q.22 Why were mitotic dividing figures observed exclusively in Zone II?
Q.23 Which zone is primarily responsible for the rapid linear growth and extension of the root into deep soil?
Q.24 Root hairs observed in Zone IV are cellular extensions of which tissue layer?
Q.25 In which zone do cells differentiate into specialized vascular tissues (xylem vessels and phloem sieve tubes)?

OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & EXEMPLAR EXPLANATIONS (SET C)
Q. No. Correct Scientific Rationale & NCERT Exemplar Analysis
Q.1 (A) $K^+$ influx lowers the water potential inside guard cells; water enters by endosmosis. Turgid guard cells bend outward along their thin outer walls, opening the pore.
Q.2 (B) Xylem is the conducting tissue for water and dissolved minerals; transpirational pull draws the red eosin dye upward through xylem conduits.
Q.3 (B) Mature human erythrocytes extrude their nuclei to maximize space for hemoglobin, maintaining a biconcave disc shape for efficient gas exchange over their 120-day life.
Q.4 (B) Action potentials cause synaptic vesicles to fuse with the presynaptic membrane, releasing neurotransmitters (like acetylcholine) to diffuse across the synaptic cleft.
Q.5 (A) Coordinated rhythmic beating of cilia on the luminal surface of ciliated columnar epithelial cells drives the ovum forward along the oviduct.
Q.6 (B) A sarcomere is the basic contractile unit of striated muscle bounded between adjacent Z-lines.
Q.7 (A) Reticular tissue consists of delicate networks of type III collagen reticular fibers and fibroblasts, supporting free immune cells in lymphoid organs.
Q.8 (B) Lenticels are lens-shaped porous regions in the periderm of woody stems that facilitate gaseous exchange across suberized cork layers.
Q.9 (C) Basal epithelial stem cells divide continuously by mitosis to replace sloughed-off superficial cells of skin and mucous membranes.
Q.10 (B) Phloem ray parenchyma cells conduct nutrients and water radially (laterally) across the stem cross-section.
Q.11 (B) Tendons are composed of parallel bundles of Type I collagen fibers, providing extraordinary tensile strength along the direction of muscular traction.
Q.12 (B) Incomplete C-shaped rings of hyaline cartilage keep the trachea permanently patent (open) and prevent collapse during negative pleural pressure breathing.
Q.13 (C) Lymphocytes (B cells producing antibodies, T cells performing cell-mediated lysis) are the key orchestrators of adaptive immune defense.
Q.14 (B) Commercial jute, hemp, and flax fibers are bast sclerenchyma fibers harvested from phloem and pericycle for textile and rope manufacture.
Q.15 (B) In the peripheral nervous system (PNS), Schwann cells wrap repeatedly around axons to form insulating myelin sheaths.
Q.16 (A) The cohesion-tension theory explains that transpirational evaporation pulls unbroken columns of water upward due to strong hydrogen bonding (cohesion) between water molecules.
Q.17 (A) Vascular and bronchial smooth muscle responds automatically to autonomic and hormonal signals (vasoconstriction/vasodilation) to sustain homeostatic perfusion.
Q.18 (A) Epithelial sheets form continuous protective barriers where adjacent cells are tightly bound by desmosomes and tight junctions with negligible extracellular matrix.
Q.19 (D) Assertion is false: Gymnosperms lack true companion cells and sieve tubes. Reason is true: Gymnosperms have sieve cells associated with Strasburger (albuminous) cells.
Q.20 (A) Platelets form a mechanical hemostatic plug and release clotting factors that convert prothrombin into thrombin, generating an insoluble fibrin clot.
Q.21 (B) The root cap protects the fragile actively dividing apical meristem behind it and secretes mucilage to lubricate root penetration into soil.
Q.22 (A) The apical meristem in Zone II consists of undifferentiated, perpetually dividing cells responsible for primary root growth.
Q.23 (C) Cellular elongation and vacuolation in Zone III exert mechanical pushing force that drives root lengthening deep into the earth.
Q.24 (C) Root hairs are unicellular tubular outgrowths of epidermal cells (epiblema/rhizodermis) that vastly amplify surface area for water and mineral absorption.
Q.25 (D) In the maturation zone, elongated cells undergo cytological differentiation into mature permanent tissues: xylem, phloem, cortex, and endodermis.
