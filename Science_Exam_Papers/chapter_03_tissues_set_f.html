Q. No. Correct Scientific Rationale & Formula Summary
Q.1 (B) Shoot and root apical meristems are located at terminal tips and drive primary longitudinal growth.
Q.2 (B) Lateral meristems (vascular and cork cambium) produce radial secondary growth, increasing trunk girth.
Q.3 (B) Collenchyma cells have corner pectin-cellulose thickenings that provide flexibility and support to growing organs.
Q.4 (C) Coir from coconut husk is composed of dead sclerenchyma fibers with heavily lignified, cemented walls.
Q.5 (A) Parenchyma is the most abundant unspecialized ground tissue, performing storage, photosynthesis, and secretion.
Q.6 (A) Aerenchyma is modified parenchyma with large interconnected intercellular air spaces that help water plants float.
Q.7 (A) Sieve tube elements connect longitudinally with perforated sieve plates to translocate photosynthesized sugars.
Q.8 (D) Tracheids, vessels, and fibers are dead at maturity; only xylem parenchyma remains living for lateral storage.
Q.9 (A) Simple squamous epithelium forms an ultra-thin, smooth sheet of pavement-like cells optimizing gaseous diffusion.
Q.10 (A) Ciliated columnar cells bear beating hair-like cilia that sweep mucus and trapped particulates up the respiratory tract.
Q.11 (B) Tendons connect muscle to bone; ligaments connect bone to bone across movable joints.
Q.12 (B) Ligaments are elastic fibrous cords containing elastin that stabilize joints by binding bones together.
Q.13 (A) Adipose tissue contains adipocytes packed with fat globules, providing energy storage and thermal insulation.
Q.14 (A) Bone matrix consists of collagen fibers mineralized with calcium phosphate (hydroxyapatite) crystals.
Q.15 (D) Kidney tubules are lined by cuboidal epithelium; ear pinna, nose tip, larynx, and trachea contain cartilage.
Q.16 (C) Cardiac muscle fibers are involuntary, cylindrical, branched, and joined by electrical intercalated discs.
Q.17 (B) Involuntary unstriated spindle-shaped smooth muscle lines internal visceral organs such as the gut and blood vessels.
Q.18 (B) Neurons are electrically excitable cells specialized to receive, conduct, and transmit nerve impulses.
Q.19 (B) The axon conducts electrical action potentials away from the neuronal soma (cell body) toward synaptic terminals.
Q.20 (B) The synapse is the microscopic gap across which neurotransmitters diffuse to propagate electrical signals between neurons.
Q.21 (B) Mature mammalian RBCs are enucleated biconcave discs optimized to carry oxygen bound to hemoglobin.
Q.22 (A) Platelets aggregate at vascular lesions and release thromboplastin to trigger the coagulation cascade.
Q.23 (B) Suberin is a hydrophobic waxy biopolymer that makes mature dead cork cells impermeable to water and gases.
Q.24 (A) Areolar tissue is loose connective tissue that fills spaces inside organs, supports internal organs, and helps tissue repair.
Q.25 (B) A thick waxy cuticle layer of cutin coats the epidermal surface of desert plants to drastically curtail water loss.
