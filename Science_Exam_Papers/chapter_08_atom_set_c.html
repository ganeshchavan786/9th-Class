SECTION B: ASSERTION - REASON QUESTIONS (Q.16 TO Q.20) [5 MARKS]
Directions for Q16 to Q20: Select the correct option from the choices given below:
(A) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
(B) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is NOT the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
(C) Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
(D) Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true (or both are false).
Q.16 Assertion (A): The chemical reactivity of an element is determined by its valence electrons.
Reason (R): In chemical reactions, only the outermost valence shell electrons are lost, gained, or shared to achieve stable octet configurations.
Q.17 Assertion (A): Rutherford's nuclear model could not explain the atomic emission spectra of hydrogen.
Reason (R): Classical electromagnetic theory predicted accelerating electrons would continuously radiate energy and collapse into the nucleus, leaving no explanation for discrete spectral lines.
Q.18 Assertion (A): The nucleus of an atom carries a positive electric charge.
Reason (R): The nucleus contains positively charged protons and electrically neutral neutrons.
Q.19 Assertion (A): Isotopes have identical physical properties such as boiling point and density.
Reason (R): Physical properties depend on mass, and isotopes of an element have different mass numbers.
Q.20 Assertion (A): Canal rays travel in straight lines towards the cathode.
Reason (R): Canal rays are produced by ionization of residual gas molecules in a discharge tube under high voltage.

READING PASSAGE & EXPERIMENTAL INVESTIGATION:

Case Study: Radioactive Isotope Tracers in Medicine & Industry
Radioactive isotopes (radioisotopes) emit ionizing radiation (alpha, beta, or gamma) as their unstable nuclei decay. In nuclear medicine and industry:
• Technetium-99m: Emits gamma rays with a 6-hour half-life used for imaging heart and bone perfusion.
• Iodine-131: Beta and gamma emitter with an 8-day half-life targeting the thyroid gland.
• Cobalt-60: Emits high-energy gamma rays (1.17 and 1.33 MeV) used in teletherapy cancer radiotherapy.
• Americium-241: Alpha emitter used in household smoke ionization detectors.

Q.21 Why is Technetium-99m ideal for diagnostic medical scans inside human patients?
Q.22 Why does radioactive Iodine-131 selectively accumulate in the human thyroid gland?
Q.23 In cancer radiotherapy, how do Cobalt-60 gamma rays destroy malignant cancer cells?
Q.24 In a household smoke detector, why are alpha particles from Americium-241 utilized instead of gamma rays?
Q.25 Which subatomic particle is identical to the alpha particle emitted by Americium-241?

OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & EXEMPLAR EXPLANATIONS (SET C)
Q. No. Correct Scientific Rationale & NCERT Exemplar Analysis
Q.1 (B) Rutherford's scattering formula shows that the number of deflected particles drops inversely with $\sin^4(\theta/2)$; only a tiny fraction (1 in 12,000) experiences wide-angle backscattering near 180°.
Q.2 (B) According to Bohr's model, when an electron jumps from a higher to lower stationary state, it emits a quantum photon of light whose energy equals the difference between the two orbital energy levels.
Q.3 (D) All six ions have identical electron configurations (2, 8). $Al^{3+}$ has the highest nuclear charge ($Z = 13$ protons), which exerts the strongest electrostatic pull on the 10 electrons, contracting the electron cloud to the smallest radius.
Q.4 (B) Uncharged neutrons do not experience electrostatic repulsion from positive nuclear protons, allowing them to penetrate deep into heavy nuclei at low thermal speeds.
Q.5 (B) Electrons in $M^{2+} = 2 + 8 + 14 = 24$. Since it lost 2 electrons, the neutral atom had $24 + 2 = 26$ electrons ($Z = 26$, Iron).
Q.6 (B) $2 n^2 = 2(3^2) = 2(9) = 18$ electrons.
Q.7 (B) Beta emitters like Strontium-90 are absorbed proportional to material thickness, allowing automated radiation sensors to control roller pressure.
Q.8 (A) $Z = A - N = 39 - 20 = 19$ (Potassium). Electronic configuration $= 2, 8, 8, 1$. Valency $= 1$.
Q.9 (B) Subatomic particle discoveries (J.J. Thomson, Goldstein, Chadwick) and nuclear transmutation proved that atoms are divisible complex structures.
Q.10 (B) Mass of proton $\approx 1.673 \times 10^{-27}$ kg; mass of electron $\approx 9.109 \times 10^{-31}$ kg. Ratio $\approx 1836 \approx 1840 : 1$.
Q.11 (A) $Z = 15$ is Phosphorus (2, 8, 5). It exhibits variable valency 3 (gaining/sharing 3 electrons) and 5 (utilizing all 5 valence electrons with vacant d-orbitals).
Q.12 (B) Heavy water is composed of deuterium ($^2_1 H$), having one proton and one neutron, utilized as a moderator in nuclear reactors.
Q.13 (A) In $^{14}_6 C$, neutrons $= 14 - 6 = 8$. In $^{16}_8 O$, neutrons $= 16 - 8 = 8$. Both have 8 neutrons (isotones).
Q.14 (C) The neutron was not discovered until 1932 by James Chadwick, decades after Thomson (1897) and Rutherford (1911).
Q.15 (B) An alpha particle consists of two protons and two neutrons, with a net charge of $+2$ ($+3.2 \times 10^{-19}$ C) and mass $\approx 4$ u.
Q.16 (A) Core inner electrons are strongly shielded and tightly bound; chemical bonding and valency involve exclusively outer valence electrons.
Q.17 (A) Continuous radiation would collapse the atom in $10^{-8}$ s and yield a continuous spectrum. Neils Bohr introduced discrete quantum orbits to solve this.
Q.18 (A) Protons have charge $+e$ and neutrons have charge $0$, so the net nuclear charge equals $+Ze$.
Q.19 (D) Assertion is false: Isotopes have *different* physical properties (density, boiling point, mass). Reason is true.
Q.20 (A) Positively charged residual gas ions are accelerated towards the perforated negative cathode, passing through the canals as luminous streams.
Q.21 (B) A 6-hour half-life gives sufficient time to complete diagnostic gamma scans while clearing quickly so the patient does not retain long-term radiation.
Q.22 (A) The thyroid gland has active sodium-iodide symporters that concentrate iodine; localized beta radiation from 131-I destroys overactive thyroid or carcinoma cells.
Q.23 (B) Ionizing radiation creates free radicals and direct DNA breaks; rapidly dividing cancer cells lack repair capacity and undergo mitotic death.
Q.24 (A) Alpha particles strongly ionize air molecules between electrodes. Smoke absorbs alpha rays, dropping current and triggering the alarm. Alphas cannot penetrate the outer casing, posing zero hazard.
Q.25 (B) An alpha particle consists of two protons and two neutrons tightly bound, identical to a helium-4 nucleus.
