SECTION B: ASSERTION - REASON QUESTIONS (Q.16 TO Q.20) [5 MARKS]
Directions for Q16 to Q20: Select the correct option from the choices given below:
(A) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
(B) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true but Reason (R) is NOT the correct explanation of Assertion (A).
(C) Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.
(D) Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true (or both are false).
Q.16 Assertion (A): A horse cannot pull a cart in deep outer space where there is no ground.
Reason (R): To accelerate forward, the horse's hooves must push backward against the ground so the ground pushes the horse-cart forward by reaction.
Q.17 Assertion (A): Modern car bumpers and crumple zones are designed to collapse easily upon severe collision.
Reason (R): Collapsing extends the duration of the impact, thereby reducing the peak retarding force experienced by passengers.
Q.18 Assertion (A): A person stepping out of a rowing boat onto the riverbank causes the boat to move backwards.
Reason (R): The foot pushes the boat backward to gain the forward momentum needed to step onto the bank.
Q.19 Assertion (A): Newton's first law of motion is often referred to as the law of inertia.
Reason (R): Inertia is the inherent resistance of an object to changes in its state of rest or uniform motion.
Q.20 Assertion (A): When a glass tumbler falls on a cement floor, it breaks, but when it falls on a carpet from the same height, it does not break.
Reason (R): The carpet exerts zero total impulse on the falling tumbler.

Case Study: Automobile Crash Test & Airbag Dynamics
In a national vehicle safety assessment, a test car of total mass 1500 kg travelling at 54 km/h (15 m/s) collides with a rigid concrete wall. An anthropomorphic test dummy of mass 70 kg is restrained in the driver's seat. Two test cases are examined:

• Case 2 (With Airbag): The deployed airbag cushions the dummy, compressing smoothly and bringing the dummy to rest over 0.15 seconds.

Q.21 What is the initial momentum of the 70 kg dummy before impact?
Q.22 In Case 1 (without airbag), what is the average retarding force exerted on the dummy's chest?
Q.23 In Case 2 (with airbag), what is the average force exerted on the dummy?
Q.24 By what factor does the airbag reduce the peak impact force in this crash scenario?
Q.25 What fundamental law of physics explains the primary protective mechanism of the airbag?

OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & IN-DEPTH EXPLANATIONS (SET B)
Q. No. Correct In-Depth Conceptual & Numerical Solution
Q.1 (B) Common acceleration $a = \frac{F}{m_1 + m_2} = \frac{20}{4 + 6} = 2\text{ m/s}^2$. Contact force pushing $m_2$ is $F_c = m_2 a = 6 \times 2 = 12$ N.
Q.2 (C) Apparent weight $N = m(g - a) = 60(10 - 2) = 60 \times 8 = 480$ N.
Q.3 (C) $N = m(g - g) = 0$. In free fall, contact normal force vanishes, producing apparent weightlessness.
Q.4 (A) $\text{Impulse} = \Delta p = m(v - u) = 5(8 - 2) = 5 \times 6 = 30\text{ N}\cdot\text{s}$.
Q.5 (B) $F = \frac{m \Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{1200 \times 25}{0.05} = \frac{30,000}{0.05} = 600,000$ N.
Q.6 (A) $\text{Thrust } F = v_{rel} \frac{\Delta m}{\Delta t} = 1000 \times 50 = 50,000$ N.
Q.7 (B) $m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 = 0 \Rightarrow 2(30) + 4(v_2) = 0 \Rightarrow 4 v_2 = -60 \Rightarrow v_2 = -15$ m/s. Speed $= 15$ m/s in opposite direction.
Q.8 (B) Mass per second $= 20 \times 0.035 = 0.70$ kg/s. $F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = 0.70 \times 400 = 280$ N.
Q.9 (A) $\Delta v = 20 - (-12) = 32$ m/s. $F = \frac{0.15 \times 32}{0.02} = \frac{4.8}{0.02} = 240$ N.
Q.10 (A) Force is the derivative of momentum: $F = \frac{dp}{dt} = 10 t$. At $t = 3$ s, $F = 10(3) = 30$ N.
Q.11 (B) Static friction is self-adjusting up to its maximum limiting value (30 N). When 20 N is applied, static friction exactly equals 20 N to keep the block stationary.
Q.12 (B) With identical velocity, the massive truck has much greater momentum ($p=mv$). For the same braking force, $\Delta t$ and stopping distance are much longer.
Q.13 (B) $\text{Centripetal force } F = \frac{m v^2}{r} = \frac{1 \times 4^2}{1} = 16$ N.
Q.14 (B) The bowstring pushes the arrow forward (action), while the arrow simultaneously exerts an equal and opposite backward force on the string (reaction).
Q.15 (B) $E_k = \frac{p^2}{2m}$. If momentum becomes $2p$, kinetic energy becomes $\frac{(2p)^2}{2m} = 4 \left(\frac{p^2}{2m}\right) = 4 E_k$.
Q.16 (A) Walking or pulling requires horizontal contact friction. Pushing backward on ground yields the forward reaction that accelerates horse and cart.
Q.17 (A) Crumple zones absorb collision energy by deforming over a longer time $\Delta t$, lowering impact acceleration ($F = \Delta p / \Delta t$) to survivable levels.
Q.18 (A) Newton's third law: pushing backward on the boat (action) produces the forward reaction pushing the person to the bank; momentum conservation moves the boat backward.
Q.19 (A) The first law formalizes Galileo's concept of inertia: an object maintains its velocity unless compelled to change by an external net force.
Q.20 (C) Assertion is true. Reason is false: The total impulse $\Delta p$ is identical in both cases, but the soft carpet increases impact time $\Delta t$, reducing force below the glass fracture threshold.
Q.21 (B) $p = m v = 70\text{ kg} \times 15\text{ m/s} = 1050\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$.
Q.22 (B) $F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \frac{1050}{0.01} = 105,000$ N (105 kN, fatal magnitude!).
Q.23 (A) $F = \frac{1050}{0.15} = 7,000$ N (7 kN, survivable with seatbelt load limiters).
Q.24 (C) $\frac{105,000}{7,000} = 15$ times lower force.
Q.25 (B) For a fixed change in momentum $\Delta p$, increasing impact duration $\Delta t$ proportionally reduces the net destructive force $F$.
