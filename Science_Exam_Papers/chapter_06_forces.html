OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & DETAILED EXPLANATIONS
Q. No. Correct Conceptual Explanation & Syllabus Rationale
Q.1 (C) Balanced forces have zero net force, so they cannot accelerate an object, but equal opposite forces can compress or distort its shape (e.g., squeezing a rubber ball).
Q.2 (B) Mass is the quantitative measure of inertia. A heavier body has greater resistance to changes in its state of motion.
Q.3 (C) Due to inertia of direction, passenger bodies tend to maintain their original straight-line path when the bus turns right.
Q.4 (D) Linear momentum is defined as the product of mass and velocity ($p = mv$), with SI unit $kg \cdot m/s$.
Q.5 (A) $a = \frac{10 - 4}{3} = 2$ m/s². $F = ma = 5 \times 2 = 10$ N.
Q.6 (B) From $F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$, increasing duration $\Delta t$ drastically reduces the impact force $F$ felt by the palms.
Q.7 (B) Action and reaction forces always act on two different interacting objects simultaneously with equal magnitude and opposite direction.
Q.8 (A) $m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 = 0 \Rightarrow 4 \times v_{gun} + 0.02 \times 400 = 0 \Rightarrow 4 v_{gun} = -8 \Rightarrow v_{gun} = -2$ m/s.
Q.9 (B) $F = \frac{m(v - u)}{t} = \frac{1000(0 - 20)}{0.1} = -200,000$ N.
Q.10 (B) Pushing water backwards (action) produces an equal and opposite forward push (reaction) from the water on the swimmer.
Q.11 (B) Galileo studied motion on double inclined planes and concluded that an object in motion continues moving unless resisted by friction.
Q.12 (B) By $F = ma$, if $F_{net} = 0$, then $a = 0$. The object maintains constant velocity or remains at rest.
Q.13 (A) The carpet moves forward when struck, but dust particles tend to remain in their state of rest due to inertia and fall away.
Q.14 (A) $m_1 = 5/10 = 0.5$ kg, $m_2 = 5/20 = 0.25$ kg. Combined mass $M = 0.75$ kg. $a = F/M = 5/0.75 = 6.67$ m/s².
Q.15 (B) Expulsion of exhaust gases at high speed downward imparts an equal upward thrust (momentum conservation / action-reaction).
Q.16 (A) Mass determines inertia. Because iron is much denser and more massive than a football, it offers greater resistance to acceleration.
Q.17 (A) Forces cancel only when acting on the same single body. Action and reaction act on different interacting bodies.
Q.18 (A) The formula $F = ma$ derived from Newton's second law provides the mathematical definition and metric value of force.
Q.19 (A) When a car stops abruptly, inertia carries unbelted occupants forward into windshield or steering wheel; seatbelts supply the decelerating force.
Q.20 (D) Reason is true (forces are equal and opposite). But Assertion is false because $a = F/m$; since the rifle is much more massive, its acceleration is far smaller than the bullet's.
Q.21 (C) The athlete has the same mass and reaches the ground with the same impact velocity, so the change in momentum $\Delta p = 0 - mv$ is identical in both cases.
Q.22 (B) Foam compresses over time $\Delta t$. Because $F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$, increasing impact time substantially lowers impact force.
Q.23 (B) $F = \frac{m \Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{60 \times 6}{0.3} = 1200$ N.
Q.24 (C) $F = \frac{60 \times 6}{0.01} = 36,000$ N (30 times higher, enough to fracture bones!).
Q.25 (D) Airbags, bubble wrap, and shock absorbers all increase collision contact duration to minimize peak impact force.
