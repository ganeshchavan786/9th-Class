Case Study 2: Automotive Braking Distance & Driver Reaction Dynamics

A traffic safety research institute analyzed the total stopping distance of a car travelling at different initial speeds ($u$) on a dry asphalt highway.
• Total Stopping Distance consists of two phases:
1. Thinking Distance ($d_{think}$): Distance covered during the driver's physiological reaction time ($t_r = 0.60\text{ s}$) before the brakes are engaged ($d_{think} = u \times t_r$).
2. Braking Distance ($d_{brake}$): Distance covered while the brakes apply a constant retarding deceleration of $a = -5.0\text{ m/s}^2$ until the car comes to rest ($v=0$).
• Data for two test speeds:
• Car A speed: $36\text{ km/h} = 10\text{ m/s}$
• Car B speed: $72\text{ km/h} = 20\text{ m/s}$
Q.6 What is the thinking distance covered by Car A ($u = 10\text{ m/s}$) before the driver presses the brake pedal?
Q.7 What is the actual braking distance ($d_{brake}$) required for Car A to come to a complete stop?
Q.8 What is the total stopping distance (Thinking + Braking) for Car A?
Q.9 For Car B travelling at double the speed ($72\text{ km/h} = 20\text{ m/s}$), what is its braking distance ($d_{brake}$)?
Q.10 Why does braking distance depend quadratically on initial speed ($d_{brake} \propto u^2$)?

Case Study 4: Multi-Stage Velocity-Time Graph Analysis of an Express Train

• Stage 2 ($20\text{ to }50\text{ s}$): The train maintains a constant cruise velocity of $30\text{ m/s}$.
• Stage 3 ($50\text{ to }60\text{ s}$): The brakes are applied uniformly, bringing the train to rest at $60\text{ s}$ ($v=0$).

Case Study 5: Centripetal Mechanics in Uniform Circular Motion

In a physics laboratory investigation of circular motion, students whirled a rubber bung of mass $m = 0.050\text{ kg}$ tied to a nylon string of radius $r = 0.80\text{ m}$ in a horizontal circular path.
• The string passed through a smooth glass tube, and its lower end supported a hanging counterweight of mass $M = 0.40\text{ kg}$.
• The students maintained steady horizontal rotation such that the hanging mass remained stationary at equilibrium ($T = M g = 0.40 \times 9.8 = 3.92\text{ N}$).
• The rubber bung completed 20 revolutions in 20.0 seconds (period $T_{rev} = 1.0\text{ s}$).
Q.21 What is the linear speed ($v$) of the rubber bung moving along the circular path?
Q.22 What is the magnitude of the centripetal acceleration ($a_c$) experienced by the rotating bung?
Q.23 What provides the necessary centripetal force to keep the rubber bung moving in a circle?
Q.24 In which direction does the rubber bung fly if the nylon string snaps suddenly?
Q.25 Why is uniform circular motion classified as an accelerated motion even when its speed is strictly constant?

Q. No. Correct Scientific Rationale & Experimental Data Analysis
Q.1 (B) $v_1 = \frac{\Delta s_1}{\Delta t} = \frac{2.0\text{ cm}}{0.10\text{ s}} = 20.0\text{ cm/s} = 0.20\text{ m/s}$.
Q.2 (B) $v_2 = \frac{6.0\text{ cm}}{0.10\text{ s}} = 60.0\text{ cm/s} = 0.60\text{ m/s}$.
Q.3 (B) $a = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{\Delta t} = \frac{0.60 - 0.20}{0.10} = \frac{0.40\text{ m/s}}{0.10\text{ s}} = 4.0\text{ m/s}^2$.
Q.4 (B) Equal increments in displacement during successive equal time intervals ($\Delta s = 4.0$ cm increase per interval) confirm constant acceleration.
Q.5 (B) For uniform acceleration, velocity increases linearly with time ($v = u + at$), yielding a straight line with gradient equal to acceleration.
Q.6 (B) $d_{think} = u \times t_r = 10\text{ m/s} \times 0.60\text{ s} = 6.0\text{ m}$.
Q.7 (B) Using $v^2 = u^2 + 2as \Rightarrow 0^2 = 10^2 + 2(-5.0)d_{brake} \Rightarrow 10 d_{brake} = 100 \Rightarrow d_{brake} = 10.0\text{ m}$.
Q.8 (B) Total Stopping Distance $= d_{think} + d_{brake} = 6.0\text{ m} + 10.0\text{ m} = 16.0\text{ m}$.
Q.9 (B) $d_{brake} = \frac{u^2}{2a} = \frac{20^2}{2 \times 5.0} = \frac{400}{10} = 40.0\text{ m}$. Notice that doubling speed quadruples braking distance ($10 \times 4 = 40$ m).
Q.10 (B) According to the work-energy theorem, braking work equals kinetic energy: $F_{brake} \times d = \frac{1}{2}m u^2 \Rightarrow d \propto u^2$.
Q.11 (A) Since the ball is released from rest ($u = 0$), $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ reduces directly to $h = \frac{1}{2}gt^2$.
Q.12 (A) $g = \frac{2h}{t^2} = \frac{2 \times 0.800}{(0.404)^2} = \frac{1.600}{0.1632} \approx 9.80\text{ m/s}^2$.
Q.13 (A) $v = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{2 \times 9.80 \times 1.250} = \sqrt{24.5} \approx 4.95\text{ m/s}$.
Q.14 (C) All bodies, regardless of mass, experience the identical gravitational acceleration ($a=g$) when aerodynamic drag is negligible.
Q.15 (B) Since $h = \left(\frac{1}{2}g\right) t^2$, plotting $h$ on y-axis against $t^2$ on x-axis produces a straight line with slope $m = \frac{1}{2}g$.
Q.16 (A) $a_1 = \frac{v - u}{\Delta t} = \frac{30 - 0}{20} = 1.5\text{ m/s}^2$.
Q.17 (B) Velocity is constant at 30 m/s; therefore, rate of change of velocity is zero ($a = 0$).
Q.18 (B) $a_3 = \frac{0 - 30}{10} = -3.0\text{ m/s}^2$. Retardation is the magnitude of deceleration, which is $3.0\text{ m/s}^2$.
Q.19 (B) Total displacement equals the area of the trapezium beneath the $v$-$t$ graph: $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \times (\text{sum of parallel sides}) \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \times (60 + 30) \times 30 = \frac{1}{2} \times 90 \times 30 = 1350\text{ m}$.
Q.20 (A) $\text{Average speed} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}} = \frac{1350\text{ m}}{60\text{ s}} = 22.5\text{ m/s}$.
Q.21 (B) $v = \frac{2\pi r}{T_{rev}} = \frac{2 \times 3.1416 \times 0.80\text{ m}}{1.0\text{ s}} = 5.026\text{ m/s} \approx 5.03\text{ m/s}$.
Q.22 (B) $a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \frac{(5.026)^2}{0.80} = \frac{25.27}{0.80} \approx 31.6\text{ m/s}^2$.
Q.23 (A) The inward radial tension in the string pulls the revolving bung toward the center of rotation, providing the centripetal force.
Q.24 (C) The moment centripetal tension disappears, the bung continues along its instantaneous tangential straight-line velocity due to inertia.
Q.25 (B) Velocity is a vector quantity. Even if its magnitude (speed) is constant, continuous turning of its direction produces continuous centripetal acceleration.
