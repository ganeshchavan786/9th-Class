SECTION A: CORE THEORETICAL PRINCIPLES (Q.1 TO Q.10) [10 MARKS]
Q.1

Assertion (A): Balanced forces acting on a body do not alter its state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line.

Reason (R): The vector sum (resultant) of all balanced forces acting on a body is exactly equal to zero ($F_{net} = 0$).

Q.2

Assertion (A): A heavier iron anvil of mass 50 kg has greater inertia than a wooden block of mass 5 kg.

Reason (R): The mass of an object is a quantitative measure of its inertia.

Q.3

Assertion (A): According to Newton's first law of motion, force is required to maintain a moving object at constant velocity in deep space.

Reason (R): A body continues in its state of rest or uniform straight-line motion unless acted upon by an external net unbalanced force.

Q.4

Assertion (A): The rate of change of linear momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied unbalanced force and takes place in the direction of the force.

Reason (R): Newton's second law of motion provides a quantitative mathematical relationship for calculating force ($F = ma$).

Q.5

Assertion (A): Action and reaction forces never cancel each other to produce equilibrium on a single body.

Reason (R): Action and reaction forces always act simultaneously on two different, distinct physical bodies.

Q.6

Assertion (A): The SI unit of linear momentum is kilogram meter per second ($\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$), which is equivalent to Newton second ($\text{N}\cdot\text{s}$).

Reason (R): Momentum is defined as the product of mass ($m$) and velocity ($v$), having dimensions $[\text{M}][\text{L}][\text{T}]^{-1}$.

Q.7

Assertion (A): A high-velocity bullet of mass 10 g can easily penetrate a thick wooden plank, whereas an identical bullet thrown gently by hand bounces off.

Reason (R): The penetrating power and impact damage of an object depend on its linear momentum ($p = mv$), not on mass alone.

Q.8

Assertion (A): Friction between the soles of shoes and the ground is an essential force that enables human walking.

Reason (R): When walking, a person pushes backward on the ground; according to Newton's third law, the ground pushes forward with equal force.

Q.9

Assertion (A): A body moving with constant speed along a circular track has constant momentum.

Reason (R): Linear momentum is a vector quantity having the same direction as the velocity vector, which changes continuously in circular motion.

Q.10

Assertion (A): Newton's first law of motion is also known as the Law of Inertia.

Reason (R): Galileo Galilei first deduced that objects in motion continue moving with constant velocity if no resistive forces act on them.

SECTION B: EXPERIMENTAL OBSERVATIONS & INFERENCES (Q.11 TO Q.18) [8 MARKS]
Q.11

Assertion (A): A cricket fielder pulls his hands backwards while catching a fast-flying ball.

Reason (R): Pulling hands back increases the duration of time over which momentum drops to zero, thereby reducing the impact force on his palms.

Q.12

Assertion (A): When a carpet is beaten vigorously with a stick, dust particles fly out.

Reason (R): Due to inertia of rest, dust particles tend to stay in their stationary positions while the carpet fabric is suddenly jerked away.

Q.13

Assertion (A): Passengers standing in a moving bus fall forward when the driver suddenly applies emergency brakes.

Reason (R): The lower part of the body in contact with the bus floor comes to rest abruptly, while the upper torso continues moving forward due to inertia of motion.

Q.14

Assertion (A): When a gun fires a bullet forward, the gun recoils backwards with a lower velocity than the bullet.

Reason (R): According to the law of conservation of momentum, the backward recoil momentum of the gun equals the forward momentum of the bullet; since the gun has a much larger mass, its recoil velocity is proportionally smaller.

Q.15

Assertion (A): Modern passenger cars are equipped with crumple zones and seatbelts.

Reason (R): Crumple zones prolong collision impact time, drastically reducing the deceleration and peak force transmitted to passengers.

Q.16

Assertion (A): Athletes in high jump events are made to land on deep foam mattresses or soft sand pits.

Reason (R): A soft landing increases the time taken for the athlete's vertical momentum to drop to zero, reducing the ground reaction force.

Q.17

Assertion (A): A rocket operates on the principle of Newton's third law of motion and linear momentum conservation.

Reason (R): A rocket pushes against the atmospheric air surrounding it to propel itself into outer space.

Q.18

Assertion (A): When a rotating stone tied to a string is released, it flies off tangentially in a straight line.

Reason (R): The moment the centripetal inward tension force ceases, the stone continues in the direction of its instantaneous tangential velocity due to inertia.

SECTION C: ADVANCED NUANCES & MISCONCEPTIONS (Q.19 TO Q.25) [7 MARKS]
Q.19

Assertion (A): In any isolated system of colliding bodies where no external net force acts, total linear momentum is conserved.

Reason (R): The internal collision forces between interacting bodies occur in equal and opposite action-reaction pairs according to Newton's third law.

Q.20

Assertion (A): A horse must exert a continuous forward pull to keep a heavy cart moving with uniform constant velocity on a rough dirt road.

Reason (R): The continuous forward pull of the horse is required to overcome the opposing resistive friction force exerted by the ground.

Q.21

Assertion (A): An astronaut of mass 80 kg floating freely outside the International Space Station throws a 2 kg metal wrench forward at 10 m/s; the astronaut drifts backwards.

Reason (R): The backward drift velocity of the astronaut is $0.25\text{ m/s}$ in accordance with the law of conservation of momentum.

Q.22

Assertion (A): Water droplets fly off radially when an umbrella is rotated rapidly.

Reason (R): The adhesive force between water and the umbrella fabric is insufficient to provide the necessary centripetal force for circular motion.

Q.23

Assertion (A): If the net force acting on a moving particle is zero, its kinetic energy must remain constant.

Reason (R): When net force is zero, acceleration is zero, meaning velocity (and hence speed) remains strictly constant.

Q.24

Assertion (A): A heavy truck and a lightweight bicycle moving with identical kinetic energy have identical linear momentum.

Reason (R): Linear momentum and kinetic energy are related by the formula $p = \sqrt{2m E_k}$; for equal $E_k$, momentum is directly proportional to $\sqrt{m}$.

Q.25

Assertion (A): A swimmer pushes the water backwards with his hands and feet to propel himself forward through the pool.

Reason (R): According to Newton's third law of motion, for every action force there is an equal and opposite reaction force.
