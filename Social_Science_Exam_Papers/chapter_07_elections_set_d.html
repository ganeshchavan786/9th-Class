SECTION A: CORE THEORETICAL PRINCIPLES (Q.1 TO Q.10) [10 MARKS]
Q.1

Assertion (A): Elections are essential for the functioning of a modern representative democracy.

Reason (R): Citizens cannot assemble daily to govern directly and therefore require regular elections to choose representatives who will legislate and form government policies.

Q.2

Assertion (A): In India, elections to the Lok Sabha follow the First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) electoral system.

Reason (R): In the FPTP system, the candidate who secures the highest number of votes in a single-member constituency is declared elected, even without an absolute majority.

Q.3

Assertion (A): The Delimitation Commission in India demarcates constituency boundaries based on the latest census population figures.

Reason (R): Constituencies are delineated so that each parliamentary seat represents approximately equal population numbers, upholding 'one person, one value'.

Q.4

Assertion (A): The Constitution of India reserves specific legislative constituencies exclusively for Scheduled Castes (SC) and Scheduled Tribes (ST).

Reason (R): Reserved seats ensure that historically marginalized communities receive fair political representation and are not perpetually outvoted in open contests.

Q.5

Assertion (A): In a reserved SC constituency, only voters belonging to the Scheduled Caste community are legally permitted to vote.

Reason (R): All eligible registered voters living in the reserved constituency cast their votes, but only candidates belonging to the designated SC category can contest.

Q.6

Assertion (A): Every Indian citizen who has attained the age of 18 years possesses the constitutional right to vote in general elections.

Reason (R): The 61st Constitutional Amendment Act of 1988 lowered the voting age in India from 21 years to 18 years.

Q.7

Assertion (A): A person convicted of serious criminal offences and sentenced to imprisonment for more than two years can still contest Lok Sabha elections.

Reason (R): Under Section 8 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, a person convicted and sentenced to two or more years of imprisonment is disqualified from contesting.

Q.8

Assertion (A): Candidates contesting elections in India are legally required to file a sworn affidavit disclosing their criminal antecedents, assets, and liabilities.

Reason (R): Mandatory affidavit disclosures empower voters to make informed choices based on the candidate's integrity and financial transparency.

Q.9

Assertion (A): The minimum eligible age to vote in India is identical to the minimum eligible age to contest a Lok Sabha parliamentary seat.

Reason (R): The minimum age to vote is 18 years, whereas a citizen must be at least 25 years of age to contest an election for the Lok Sabha.

Q.10

Assertion (A): The Voters' List (Electoral Roll) is revised and updated periodically by the Election Commission of India.

Reason (R): Periodic revision ensures that newly eligible 18-year-olds are registered and deceased or migrated citizens are removed from the roll.

SECTION B: EVIDENTIARY PROOFS & MECHANISMS (Q.11 TO Q.18) [8 MARKS]
Q.11

Assertion (A): The Election Commission of India (ECI) operates as an independent constitutional body free from executive government control.

Reason (R): Article 324 of the Constitution vests the superintendence, direction, and control of all elections in the Election Commission.

Q.12

Assertion (A): The ruling government can unilaterally dismiss the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) of India at any time without parliamentary involvement.

Reason (R): The CEC enjoys security of tenure and can only be removed from office through impeachment by both Houses of Parliament with a special majority on grounds of proved misbehavior or incapacity.

Q.13

Assertion (A): During general elections, the Model Code of Conduct (MCC) comes into force immediately upon the announcement of election dates by the ECI.

Reason (R): The MCC prevents the ruling party from misusing official government machinery, vehicles, and state advertisements to gain an unfair advantage.

Q.14

Assertion (A): Under election laws in India, political parties are permitted to appeal to voters in the name of religion and caste.

Reason (R): Section 123 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951 classifies appeals to religion, race, caste, community, or religious symbols as corrupt electoral practices.

Q.15

Assertion (A): During elections, civil servants and police personnel on election duty operate under the administrative control of the Election Commission rather than their state governments.

Reason (R): Statutory deputation to the ECI ensures that local government officials perform their polling duties impartially without fear of political retaliation by ruling ministers.

Q.16

Assertion (A): Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs) used in Indian elections are connected to the central internet network to transmit votes in real time.

Reason (R): Indian EVMs are standalone offline microcontrollers with write-once software that are never connected to the internet, Wi-Fi, or Bluetooth networks.

Q.17

Assertion (A): The Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT) unit provides an independent verification mechanism for EVM voting.

Reason (R): When a voter presses a candidate button, the VVPAT prints a paper slip showing the candidate's serial number, name, and symbol for 7 seconds behind a glass window.

Q.18

Assertion (A): The Election Commission of India has the authority to order a repoll in specific polling booths if booth capturing or electoral malpractice is detected.

Reason (R): The ECI is legally empowered to countermand elections and order fresh polling to ensure that the outcome reflects genuine popular will.

SECTION C: ADVANCED NUANCES & INSTITUTIONAL COMPLEXITIES (Q.19 TO Q.25) [7 MARKS]
Q.19

Assertion (A): In India, poor, illiterate, and underprivileged citizens vote in a higher proportion than affluent and educated elites.

Reason (R): In Western democracies like the USA, voter turnout is typically higher among wealthy and educated citizens, whereas Indian marginalized classes view voting as their most potent tool to extract welfare entitlements.

Q.20

Assertion (A): Incumbent political parties in India always win reelection comfortably across successive elections.

Reason (R): In India, ruling parties regularly lose elections at both the national and state levels, demonstrating the genuine competitive fluidity and power of the electorate.

Q.21

Assertion (A): Money power and criminal connections present severe challenges to free and fair elections in India.

Reason (R): Wealthy candidates and parties spend disproportionate resources on publicity and logistics, making it extremely difficult for honest, resource-poor candidates to compete on an equal footing.

Q.22

Assertion (A): Dynastic succession in political parties undermines internal party democracy.

Reason (R): When top party positions and election tickets are monopolized by a single family, talented ordinary party cadres are denied fair leadership opportunities.

Q.23

Assertion (A): The NOTA (None of the Above) option on EVMs was introduced following a Supreme Court directive in 2013.

Reason (R): NOTA provides voters with the constitutional freedom of negative voting to express disapproval of all candidates contesting in their constituency.

Q.24

Assertion (A): If NOTA receives the highest number of votes in an Indian constituency, the election is automatically nullified and reheld without those candidates.

Reason (R): Under current Indian electoral law, NOTA votes are counted as invalid votes, and the candidate with the highest number of valid votes among the remaining contestants is declared the winner.

Q.25

Assertion (A): Free and fair elections serve as the bedrock of constitutional democracy in India.

Reason (R): Elections provide a legitimate, peaceful mechanism for the transfer of sovereign state power according to the sovereign will of the people.
