Q.1 What is the primary archaeological significance of the discovery of 'steatite micro-beads' (some less than 1 mm in diameter) at Harappa and Chanhudaro?
Q.2 In Harappan town planning, why did the residential houses in the Lower Town have their entrance doors opening into narrow side lanes rather than the main streets?
Q.3 What unique architectural feature was discovered at Kalibangan and Banawali that was notably ABSENT in the coastal site of Lothal?
Q.4 The Harappan civilization utilized an elaborate system of standardized weights. What was the exact ratio sequence of the smaller binary denominations before switching to decimal multiples?
Q.5 What is the 'lost-wax' (cire perdue) process used by Harappan artisans to cast the famous bronze 'Dancing Girl'?
Q.6 Which distinctive pigment was predominantly used by Mesolithic hunters to paint the rock shelters of Bhimbetka, and where did they obtain it?
Q.7 Why do archaeologists consider the mature Harappan site of Shortughai in Badakhshan (northern Afghanistan) an extraordinary anomaly in Indus settlement geography?
Q.8 In the Indus Valley Civilisation, what was the primary purpose of the 'Citadel' (Upper Town) located on an elevated artificial mud-brick platform in the western part of cities?
Q.9 What does the discovery of etched carnelian beads in both Harappa and the Royal Cemetery of Ur in Mesopotamia reveal about ancient trade technology?
Q.10 Which Harappan city is uniquely characterized by the COMPLETE ABSENCE of a fortified Citadel, consisting exclusively of an open Lower Town?
Q.11 What material was used by Harappans to manufacture 'faience' bangles and cosmetic vessels, and why was it considered a luxury material?
Q.12 What was the function of the massive stone structures termed 'reservoirs' in Dholavira, which covered approximately 10% of the entire fortified city area?
Q.13 At the coastal site of Lothal, what evidence confirms that the large brick basin was an engineered tidal dockyard rather than a domestic water irrigation tank?
Q.14 What linguistic and contextual clue connects the Mesopotamian cuneiform name 'Meluhha' with the Harappan Civilisation?
Q.15 Why were the burials excavated in the Cemetery R-37 at Harappa characterized by simple wooden coffins and pottery vessels rather than massive gold hoards?
Q.16 What is the 'Dholavira Signboard' discovered in the western chamber of the northern gate of the Citadel?
Q.17 Which precious green gemstone was imported by the Harappans from the Pamir Mountains and Central Asia for carving ornamental beads?
Q.18 What is the primary technological evidence that the Harappans did NOT possess the horse (*Equus caballus*) as a dominant domestic animal during the mature urban phase?
Q.19 How did Harappan city engineers ensure that wastewater from domestic second-story bathrooms was transported safely to the street drains without splashing?
Q.20 What agricultural feature was discovered at the Harappan site of Shortughai in Afghanistan that demonstrates adaptation to arid Central Asian geography?
Q.21 What soft mineral paste was applied to the surface of steatite seals before heating them in specialized kilns to produce a durable white finish?
Q.22 What does the discovery of copper axes and chisels in mature Harappan sites containing trace percentages of arsenic indicate about ancient metallurgy?
Q.23 Why is the Mesolithic rock art site of Bhimbetka considered an invaluable record of prehistoric human-animal relationships?
Q.24 What does the term 'Palaeolithic' literally mean from its Greek linguistic roots?
Q.25 In summary, why did the urban civilization of the Indus Valley stand out as uniquely advanced among all contemporary Bronze Age civilizations of Egypt and Mesopotamia?

OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET C)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & In-Depth Conceptual Solution
Q.1 (A) Manufacturing 1-mm micro-beads of talc/steatite required grinding paste, extruding tiny cylinders, microscopic drilling, and heating to $>1000^\circ\text{C}$ to induce enstatite hardening.
Q.2 (A) Courtyard-centered architecture prioritized privacy: blank outer walls, offset entryways from narrow side alleys, and ground-floor rooms without street-facing windows kept interiors quiet.
Q.3 (A) Kalibangan and Banawali feature series of ritual fire altars with terracotta cakes and charcoal, whereas Lothal is characterized by its tidal brick dockyard and bead factories.
Q.4 (A) Harappan metrology: binary denominations (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 up to 64, where unit 16 weighed ~13.7 g), followed by decimal multiples (160, 200, 320, 640, 1600, 3200, etc.).
Q.5 (A) Lost-wax casting: a wax effigy is coated with refractory clay; heating drains the wax; molten bronze ($Cu+Sn$) fills the hollow cavity; upon cooling, the clay shell is broken away.
Q.6 (A) Bhimbetka rock art used natural mineral pigments: red from pulverized hematite/geru ($Fe_2O_3$), white from limestone/kaolin, and green from copper minerals, bound with water or fat.
Q.7 (A) Shortughai was an imperial trading enclave along the Oxus River, featuring classic Indus seals, pottery, and weights positioned strategically to control Badakhshan lapis lazuli.
Q.8 (A) The western Citadel sat on massive mud-brick terraces 7–12 meters high, segregating elite public civic, ritual, and storage facilities from the residential Lower Town.
Q.9 (A) Alkali-etched carnelian was a signature Indus technological monopoly: painting potash/soda patterns and firing to create indelible white lines on red carnelian (*guglu*).
Q.10 (A) Chanhudaro (in modern Sindh) is the only excavated major Indus urban center that lacked a fortified Citadel mound, functioning primarily as a craft manufacturing town.
Q.11 (A) Faience is an artificial glassy composite of crushed quartz sand, mineral colorants, and flux fused at high temperatures ($>950^\circ\text{C}$); its rarity made it a high-status prestige good.
Q.12 (A) Dholavira's 16 colossal rock-cut reservoirs interconnected by drains and sluices stored over 2.5 lakh cubic meters of water, sustaining an island city in the arid Rann of Kutch.
Q.13 (A) Lothal's basin ($214 \times 36\text{ m}$) features burnt brick walls, a 7-meter inlet lock-gate, grooved sluice spillways, perforated stone mooring anchors, and marine micro-organisms.
Q.14 (A) King Sargon of Akkad (c. 2334 BCE) boasted that ships from Meluhha moored at his quays, laden with carnelian, lapis lazuli, ivory, and *haja* (peacock, *mayura*).
Q.15 (A) Unlike Egyptian Pharaohs whose tombs hoarded national bullion, Harappans interred modest ceramic vessels, shell bangles, and copper mirrors, recycling precious metals in the living economy.
Q.16 (A) The Dholavira Signboard (c. 2500 BCE) is one of the world's earliest public monumental signboards: 10 large crystalline gypsum glyphs once mounted over a civic gateway.
Q.17 (A) Jade and jadeite ornamental stones were procured through high-altitude Central Asian exchange networks across the Pamirs.
Q.18 (A) The zebu bull, unicorn, elephant, tiger, and rhinoceros appear prolifically on seals and terracotta models, while true domesticated horse remains are conspicuously absent in mature strata.
Q.19 (A) Upper-floor latrines and bathrooms emptied via vertical terracotta interlocking drainpipes concealed inside brick wall cavities, feeding into ground-level covered chutes.
Q.20 (A) Unlike the seasonal flood-inundation farming of the Indus plain, Shortughai features direct archaeological evidence of stone-and-earth diversion canals for irrigation.
Q.21 (A) Steatite seals were carved from soft soapstone, coated with a finely ground talc/alkali chemical paste, and kiln-fired to $>900^\circ\text{C}$ to form an enstatite glazed white surface.
Q.22 (A) Arsenical copper alloys (1–4% As) were intentional early Bronze Age metallurgical techniques: arsenic deoxidized molten copper, improved tensile hardness, and reduced casting bubbles.
Q.23 (A) Bhimbetka's rock paintings span the Upper Palaeolithic through Mesolithic, chronicling animal vitality, communal drive hunts, ritual dances, and animal-mask ceremonies.
Q.24 (A) Coined by Sir John Lubbock in 1865, 'Palaeolithic' combines Greek *palaios* (ancient/old) and *lithos* (stone), contrasting with 'Neolithic' (new stone).
Q.25 (A) While Egypt built royal tombs and Mesopotamia built royal ziggurats, Harappans directed civic surplus into public sanitation, standard weights, and equitable urban hygiene.
