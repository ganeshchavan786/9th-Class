OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET A)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & Conceptual Rationale
Q.1 (B) Endogenic forces are internal forces originating deep within the Earth, responsible for mountain building (orogeny), volcanism, and earthquakes.
Q.2 (A) Plate Tectonics explains the structural features of the Earth's crust in terms of the interaction of rigid lithospheric plates moving over the asthenosphere.
Q.3 (B) At a convergent boundary, two plates collide. One plate is forced beneath the other (subduction) or crust crumples upward into fold mountains (e.g., Himalayas).
Q.4 (B) At a divergent boundary, tectonic plates pull apart and molten magma rises to form new oceanic crust, as seen along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge.
Q.5 (B) The focus (hypocentre) is the actual underground point of origin of an earthquake. The point on the Earth's surface directly above the focus is the epicentre.
Q.6 (A) The epicentre is the location on the Earth's surface directly above the focus where earthquake vibrations are typically felt first and most intensely.
Q.7 (B) A seismograph (or seismometer) records the vibrations and seismic waves generated by earthquakes.
Q.8 (B) The Richter scale measures earthquake magnitude. Each whole-number increase represents roughly a 32-fold increase in released energy.
Q.9 (A) A volcanic crater is the bowl-shaped depression or vent around the volcanic mouth through which eruptive materials are ejected.
Q.10 (A) Weathering is the in-situ physical breakdown and chemical alteration of rocks at or near the Earth's surface without being transported away.
Q.11 (B) Erosion involves the wearing away and dynamic transportation of rock particles by mobile natural agents like rivers, glaciers, wind, and sea waves.
Q.12 (A) A gorge is a deep, narrow river valley with very steep rock walls formed by intensive vertical down-cutting in young mountainous stages.
Q.13 (B) In plains, a river flows sluggishly and develops S-shaped looping bends called meanders due to lateral erosion on outer banks and deposition on inner banks.
Q.14 (A) When a meander loop is cut off from the main river channel during floods, it leaves a curved, crescent-shaped lake known as an oxbow lake.
Q.15 (A) A delta is a triangular alluvial deposit formed at the mouth of a river as velocity drops upon meeting a standing body of water (e.g., Sundarbans delta).
Q.16 (A) Glaciers widen, deepen, and straighten pre-existing river valleys, carving steep-sided, flat-bottomed U-shaped valleys (glacial troughs).
Q.17 (A) Moraines are accumulations of glacial till (unsorted rock debris) deposited at the margins, sides (lateral), or terminus (terminal) of a glacier.
Q.18 (A) Wind carries sand particles predominantly near the ground (within 1 meter), eroding the lower trunk of rocks faster and leaving mushroom-shaped rocks.
Q.19 (A) Barchans are crescent-shaped shifting sand dunes with horns pointing in the direction of the prevailing wind.
Q.20 (A) Loess is an extensive unstratified deposit of windblown, silt-sized dust particles, creating rich, easily cultivated agricultural soils.
Q.21 (A) Sea cliffs are steep vertical rock faces formed along coastlines where relentless wave action undermines and notches the base of coastal rocks.
Q.22 (A) When sea caves in a headland unite from both sides or erode completely through, they form a natural bridge or sea arch.
Q.23 (A) A sea stack is a steep, isolated vertical pillar of rock in the sea, left standing after the collapse of a sea arch roof.
Q.24 (A) Beaches are coastal depositional landforms created when constructive waves wash sand and pebbles onto the shore.
Q.25 (A) The Himalayas were formed by the continent-continent collision of the Indian Plate moving northwards into the Eurasian Plate at a convergent boundary.
