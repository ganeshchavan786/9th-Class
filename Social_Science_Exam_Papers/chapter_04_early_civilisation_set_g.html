Q1. What advanced Stone Age lithic technology involved shaping a flint core before detaching a single pre-determined, razor-sharp flake tool with a prepared striking platform?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Levallois technique (Middle Palaeolithic) demonstrated cognitive planning by pre-shaping cores to strike off standardized flakes.
Q2. Excavations of human skeletal remains from the mature Harappan cemetery at Rakhigarhi, Haryana, with successful ancient DNA (aDNA) sequencing revealed:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Rakhigarhi aDNA (Shinde et al., 2019) disproved the Aryan invasion timeline, showing genetic continuity with indigenous South Asian populations.
Q3. What mathematical relationship governed the Harappan binary weight unit scale, where the standard unit 16 weighed approximately 13.7 grams?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Harappan metrology combined an exact lower binary scale (1 to 64) with upper decimal multiples, standardizing transactions across 1,000 km.
Q4. What specialized, extremely hard metamorphic lithic material was manufactured into specialized micro-drills to perforate carnelian beads at Chanhudaro and Lothal?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Harappan bead artisans developed specialized constricted micro-drills made of 'Ernestite', a rare metamorphic rock harder than carnelian.
Q5. How did Harappan pyrotechnologists manufacture the diagnostic etched red-and-white carnelian beads exported to Mesopotamian royal courts?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Harappan pyrotechnology bleached intricate white patterns onto iron-rich carnelian by applying alkali paste and refiring the gemstone.
Q6. In the famous 'Pashupati' seal from Mohenjo-daro, which four wild animals surround the central horned seated figure?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Pashupati seal depicts an elephant and tiger on the right, a rhinoceros and buffalo on the left, and two antelopes beneath the seat.
Q7. What distinct physical feature characterizes the famous steatite bust of the 'Priest-King' excavated at Mohenjo-daro?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Priest-King statue wears an embroidered trefoil-patterned shawl over the left shoulder, a forehead fillet, and a groomed beard.
Q8. What dental pathology observed on human teeth from Mehrgarh provides the world's earliest archaeological evidence of proto-dentistry (in vivo tooth drilling)?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Microscopic dental analysis on Mehrgarh skeletons revealed 11 molars precisely drilled with flint micro-drills to relieve tooth decay.
Q9. Which site in the Kachi plain documented the botanical transition from two-row wild hulled barley to six-row naked domesticated barley alongside zebu cattle domestication?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Mehrgarh's deep stratigraphy provided the botanical evidence of cereal domestication (six-row naked barley) in South Asia.
Q10. What is the archaeological term for the artificial raised platforms of sun-dried mud bricks upon which Harappan Citadels were constructed?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Harappan Citadels were erected on massive elevated mud-brick podiums to protect elite civic structures from perennial monsoon inundations.
Q11. What unique sanitary feature was discovered inside the residential houses of Mohenjo-daro and Lothal connected directly to exterior drain pipes?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Houses featured paved brick bathing stalls and latrines with sloping discharge chutes feeding covered street sewer mains.
Q12. Why is the Harappan script believed to have been written in the 'Boustrophedon' style in multi-line inscriptions?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Boustrophedon ('as the ox turns') describes inscriptions where text alternates direction in successive lines.
Q13. What raw material was used by Harappans to manufacture synthetic, vitrified, glassy blue-green paste beads and figurines?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Harappan faience was a synthetic pyrotechnological material made by fusing crushed quartz silica with alkali fluxes and copper glazes.
Q14. What archaeological evidence found at Kalibangan and Banawali demonstrates that Harappans possessed wheeled transportation?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Terracotta models of bullock carts and preserved cart ruts matching the gauge of modern bullock carts prove wheeled transport.
Q15. The discovery of a double burial (a male and a female skeleton interred together in a single brick-lined grave) was unearthed at which Harappan site?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Lothal's cemetery yielded unique joint/double burials where two individuals were buried together in a single grave pit.
Q16. Which Harappan city featured monumental fortification walls built of solid limestone blocks up to 7 meters thick, punctuated with grand entry gates?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Dholavira's fortifications were constructed of massive dressed stone blocks, unique among Harappan cities.
Q17. What was the purpose of the perforated cylindrical terracotta jars found abundantly in Harappan excavations?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Perforated jars were utilized as functional sieves/strainers, likely in the fermentation of beverages or pressing curds.
Q18. What paleoclimatic event around 4200 BP (c. 2200 BCE) caused global megadroughts that contributed to the collapse of the Old Kingdom in Egypt, the Akkadian Empire in Mesopotamia, and the Harappan Civilisation?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The 4.2k BP aridification event triggered multi-century droughts across Afro-Eurasia, undermining the agricultural surplus of Bronze Age empires.
Q19. Which Harappan site in Haryana is recognized as the largest Harappan city in geographic area (spanning over 350 hectares), surpassing Mohenjo-daro?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Rakhigarhi in Hisar district, Haryana, is now confirmed as the largest Harappan urban site by total surface extent.
Q20. What unique architectural feature was discovered at Banawali in Haryana regarding Harappan street planning?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Banawali exhibited a radial street network diverging outward from the center, departing from the typical strict orthogonal gridiron.
Q21. What diagnostic ceramic ware characterized by a lustrous, glassy black surface and exceptional hardness emerged in the 1st millennium BCE across North India?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW) was a high-status, mirror-like ceramic associated with the Second Urbanisation and the rise of Mahajanapadas.
Q22. Which ceramic culture, associated with early iron metallurgy in the Gangetic plains, preceded Northern Black Polished Ware (c. 1200–600 BCE)?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Painted Grey Ware (PGW), discovered at Hastinapur and Kurukshetra, represents the material culture of the Later Vedic era and early iron usage.
Q23. What is 'Cire Perdue'?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Cire perdue (lost-wax casting) is the metallurgical technique used to cast the Harappan Dancing Girl and Chola bronzes.
Q24. In the Mesolithic rock art of Bhimbetka, which artistic convention was used to depict animals with their internal organs visible inside their bodies?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Prehistoric hunters rendered animals in the 'X-ray style', painting internal organs, gastrointestinal tracts, and unborn young.
Q25. What is the primary conclusion drawn by modern archaeologists regarding the political structure of the Mature Harappan Civilisation?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Remarkable standardization across a million square kilometers points to centralized civic institutions and merchant oligarchies without military despotism.
