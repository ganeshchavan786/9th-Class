Q1. Using Stefan-Boltzmann's law of radiation ($E = \sigma T^4$), what would Earth's mean surface equilibrium temperature be in the COMPLETE ABSENCE of atmospheric greenhouse gases?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Without the natural greenhouse effect, radiative equilibrium with solar insolation yields an effective emission temperature of 255 K (-18°C).
Q2. When an unsaturated air parcel ascends adiabatically, its temperature drops at the Dry Adiabatic Lapse Rate (DALR) of:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The dry adiabatic lapse rate ($g/c_p$) is approximately 9.8°C per 1,000 meters of elevation change.
Q3. Under what condition is an atmospheric layer classified as 'Absolutely Unstable' for convective cloud development?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: When the environmental lapse rate exceeds the DALR, an ascending air parcel remains warmer and less dense than surrounding air, accelerating convection.
Q4. What is the 'Geostrophic Wind' in dynamic meteorology?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Geostrophic balance occurs above the friction layer when Coriolis force balances the pressure gradient force, flowing parallel to isobars.
Q5. In the Southern Oscillation Index (SOI), what atmospheric pressure difference is measured to assess the El Niño / La Niña state?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Southern Oscillation Index (SOI) measures normalized pressure anomalies between Tahiti and Darwin; sustained negative SOI indicates El Niño.
Q6. What is the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD), and how does a 'Positive IOD' phase influence the Indian monsoon?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: A positive IOD warms the western Indian Ocean relative to the east, reinforcing convective cells and boosting Indian monsoon rainfall.
Q7. What is the Madden-Julian Oscillation (MJO)?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The MJO is an eastward-propagating tropical wave of enhanced rainfall that modulates monsoon active-break cycles on 30–60 day timeframes.
Q8. Why does a mature tropical cyclone require low vertical wind shear (< 10 knots) between the lower and upper troposphere to survive?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Strong vertical wind shear tilts and disperses the warm latent heat core, disrupting the chimney effect and tearing the vortex apart.
Q9. What is the 100-year Global Warming Potential (GWP) of Methane ($CH_4$) relative to Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$)?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Over a 100-year time horizon, methane traps approximately 28 to 36 times more infrared radiation per kilogram than $CO_2$.
Q10. What gas has the highest known Global Warming Potential (GWP), exceeding 23,500 over a 100-year timeframe, used as an electrical insulator in transformers?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Sulfur hexafluoride ($SF_6$) is an extraordinarily potent greenhouse gas with a GWP of $\approx 23,500$ and an atmospheric lifetime of 3,200 years.
Q11. What is the 'Ice-Albedo Positive Feedback' in global climate dynamics?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Ice loss replaces high-albedo ice with low-albedo ocean water, driving greater absorption of insolation and compounding warming.
Q12. What thermodynamic parameter determines the maximum moisture-holding capacity of an air parcel as expressed by the Clausius-Clapeyron relation?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Clausius-Clapeyron equation dictates that the atmosphere's saturation vapor pressure expands exponentially by ~7% per °C of warming.
Q13. What is the primary thermodynamic cause of the 'October Heat' phenomenon experienced in peninsular India following the Southwest Monsoon?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The transition period in October features clear sunny skies over soil still saturated with monsoon moisture, producing sultry 'October heat'.
Q14. The 'Rossby Waves' (planetary waves) in the upper troposphere develop primarily due to:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Rossby waves arise from conservation of potential vorticity and the meridional gradient of the Coriolis force ($eta$-effect).
Q15. Why does the Subtropical Westerly Jet stream withdraw to the north of the Himalayas before the onset of the Indian summer monsoon?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The northward shift of the subtropical westerly jet beyond Tibet is a mandatory dynamic precursor to the onset of the Indian southwest monsoon.
Q16. What is 'Rayleigh Scattering' in atmospheric optics, and what everyday optical phenomenon does it explain?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Rayleigh scattering intensity is proportional to $\lambda^{-4}$; short blue wavelengths scatter much more than longer red wavelengths, coloring the sky blue.
Q17. What optical scattering mechanism causes clouds and fog droplets (which are larger than light wavelengths) to appear white?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Mie scattering occurs when particle sizes equal or exceed light wavelengths, scattering all visible wavelengths equally to appear white.
Q18. What is the primary component of Photochemical Smog formed during sunny afternoons in polluted urban centers?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Photochemical smog is driven by solar UV light acting on vehicular $NO_x$ and hydrocarbons, generating secondary toxic ozone and PAN.
Q19. Why is stratospheric ozone considered 'good ozone', while tropospheric ground-level ozone is considered 'bad ozone'?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Ozone ($O_3$) in the stratosphere protects against ultraviolet rays; in the troposphere, it is a hazardous oxidant damaging human lungs and crops.
Q20. Which atmospheric layer reflects high-frequency skywave radio communications back to Earth due to free electrons and ions?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Ionosphere (D, E, and F layers) contains solar-ionized plasma that refracts and reflects HF radio waves for long-distance communication.
Q21. What is the 'Thermal Equator'?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The thermal equator is the isotherm of maximum surface temperature, migrating seasonally into the Northern Hemisphere in July.
Q22. The 'Föhn' and 'Chinook' winds are examples of:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Chinook and Föhn winds are warm, dry winds on leeward mountain slopes heated by adiabatic compression during descent.
Q23. What is the 'Walker Circulation' in equatorial meteorology?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Walker circulation is the zonal atmospheric cell across the equatorial Pacific; its breakdown or displacement triggers El Niño.
Q24. How does aerosol pollution (such as sulfate particles and black carbon) exert a 'direct radiative forcing' on climate?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Light-colored sulfate aerosols increase planetary albedo (cooling), whereas dark soot particles absorb radiation, warming atmospheric layers.
Q25. Which international agreement under the Montreal Protocol, adopted in 2016, mandates the global phase-down of hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs)?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Kigali Amendment (2016) to the Montreal Protocol mandates the phase-down of potent greenhouse-warming HFCs.
