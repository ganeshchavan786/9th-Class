OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET A)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & Conceptual Rationale
Q.1 (A) The Rigveda is the earliest Vedic text, composed around 1500–1000 BCE, consisting of 1,028 hymns organized into 10 Mandalas.
Q.2 (A) The Sabha (an assembly of village elders/elites) and the Samiti (a broader general folk assembly) checked royal authority in Early Vedic times.
Q.3 (A) By the 6th century BCE, 16 powerful territorial states called Mahajanapadas (such as Magadha, Kosala, Vatsa, and Avanti) emerged in northern India.
Q.4 (A) Magadha (modern Bihar) became pre-eminent due to fertile Ganga-Son agricultural tracts, rich iron ore deposits in Rajgir/Chota Nagpur, and timber/elephants.
Q.5 (A) Vajji was governed as a Gana or Sangha, where power was shared among a council of rajas rather than a single hereditary monarch.
Q.6 (A) Chandragupta Maurya founded the Mauryan Empire, uniting vast territories of the subcontinent under centralized administration.
Q.7 (A) Kautilya's Arthashastra is a comprehensive ancient Sanskrit treatise on statecraft, military strategy, fiscal administration, and governance.
Q.8 (A) Megasthenes was the Seleucid envoy whose book 'Indica' recorded invaluable observations of Mauryan administration, military, and society.
Q.9 (A) The horrific carnage of the Kalinga War deeply moved Ashoka, leading him to embrace Buddhism and preach Dhamma (moral living and non-violence).
Q.10 (A) Ashoka's edicts intended for ordinary citizens were composed in Prakrit (the vernacular tongue) and carved in Brahmi script.
Q.11 (A) Ashoka instituted the office of Dhamma Mahamattas, senior officials dedicated to propagating dharma, charity, and social welfare.
Q.12 (A) Emperor Kanishka (c. 78–144 CE) was a great patron of Buddhism, presided over the 4th Buddhist Council, and issued exquisite gold coins.
Q.13 (A) The Gupta era saw remarkable intellectual and cultural flourishing: Kalidasa's poetry, Aryabhata's astronomy, Ajanta cave murals, and temple architecture.
Q.14 (A) Harishena composed the Allahabad Stone Pillar Inscription (Prayag Prashasti) in refined Sanskrit praising the campaigns of Samudragupta.
Q.15 (A) Aryabhata, in his landmark work 'Aryabhatiya' (499 CE), expounded the earth's rotation, the nature of solar/lunar eclipses, and trigonometric concepts.
Q.16 (A) Fa-Hien traveled through northern India during the early 5th century CE, leaving detailed descriptions of peace, prosperity, and charitable hospitals.
Q.17 (A) The Mehrauli Iron Pillar inscription commemorates King 'Chandra' (identified with Chandragupta II), showcasing phenomenal Gupta-era metallurgical mastery.
Q.18 (A) Banabhatta wrote 'Harshacharita', the first formal historical biography in Sanskrit celebrating the deeds of King Harshavardhana.
Q.19 (A) Nalanda was a world-famous center of higher learning attracting thousands of scholar-monks from China, Korea, Tibet, and Central Asia.
Q.20 (A) The Sangam literature (Ettuthokai, Pattupattu) represents ancient Tamil poetry composed at royal gatherings in Madurai under Pandya patronage.
Q.21 (A) The Pallavas (particularly Narasimhavarman I) created the iconic rock-cut rathas and structural Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram.
Q.22 (A) Under the Cholas, villages enjoyed remarkable autonomy; the 'Ur' was a general village assembly, while the 'Sabha' was an assembly of learned Brahmins.
Q.23 (A) The Uttaramerur inscription of Chola king Parantaka I (c. 921 CE) gives comprehensive rules for conducting village elections using palm-leaf pots.
Q.24 (A) Rajaraja Chola I constructed the magnificent Brihadisvara (Big) Temple at Thanjavur, a pinnacle of Dravidian architecture.
Q.25 (A) Pulakeshin II defeated northern emperor Harsha when the latter attempted to expand south across the Narmada River, as recorded at Aihole.
