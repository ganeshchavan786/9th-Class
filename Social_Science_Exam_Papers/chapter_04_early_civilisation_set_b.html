OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET B)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & Analytical Rationale
Q.1 (A) V. Gordon Childe coined the 'Neolithic Revolution' because food production created economic surpluses that freed segments of society to become artisans, administrators, and builders.
Q.2 (A) Standardized covered drains connecting private washrooms to street sumps reflect sophisticated municipal planning and public health standards unmatched in antiquity.
Q.3 (A) Most Indus cities had a dual layout (Citadel and Lower Town) built of fired brick; Dholavira exhibited a unique tripartite design constructed of dressed stone masonry.
Q.4 (A) Located in arid Kutch with seasonal monsoon rivulets (Mansar and Manhar), Dholavira engineered massive rock-cut reservoirs to harvest and conserve rainwater.
Q.5 (A) Lothal's basin, linked to the Bhogavo river, regulated water levels via sluices to berth maritime vessels, confirmed by Persian Gulf seals and terracotta ship models.
Q.6 (A) Uniform 1:2:4 brick proportions from Shortughai in Afghanistan to Lothal in Gujarat confirm centralized civic regulations and widespread standard metrology.
Q.7 (A) Clay sealings from Lothal showing knot and fabric impressions on the reverse prove seals were pressed into damp clay over bundled goods to authenticate ownership.
Q.8 (A) Chanhudaro and Lothal had specialized bead workshops where carnelian was baked to produce deep red colors, drilled with specialized chert drills, and polished for export.
Q.9 (A) Unlike Egypt's Pharaohs or Mesopotamia's Lugals, the Indus civilization lacks dynastic megaliths or militaristic trophies, pointing toward a merchant/civic oligopoly.
Q.10 (A) The Dancing Girl is a hollow-cast bronze masterpiece made via the lost-wax method, demonstrating sophisticated metallurgy and naturalistic aesthetic posture.
Q.11 (A) Indus seals, cubed chert weights, and carnelian beads in Mesopotamian royal tombs match Akkadian texts recording trade with Meluhha, Magan, and Dilmun.
Q.12 (A) The terracotta plough model from Banawali (alongside ploughed furrow grids at Kalibangan) confirms that Harappans utilized ox-drawn wooden ploughs.
Q.13 (A) Gypsum mortar bound the fired bricks, and a 3-cm lining of natural bitumen (asphalt) sealed the pool, demonstrating waterproofing mastery.
Q.14 (A) Nageshwar (Gujarat) and Balakot (Pakistan) were coastal shell-working industrial centers situated near rich marine conch beds (*Turbinella pyrum*).
Q.15 (A) Two sets of furrows crossing at right angles (narrow furrows for horsegram, wide furrows for mustard) demonstrate sophisticated mixed-cropping techniques.
Q.16 (A) The introspective, slit-eyed steatite bust of the 'Priest-King' with a trefoil-patterned robe resembles elite Mesopotamian religious and civic portraiture.
Q.17 (A) Shortughai was established along the Oxus River specifically to control the mining and trade of rare, highly prized Lapis Lazuli from Badakhshan.
Q.18 (A) Lacking bilingual parallel texts (like the Greek-Egyptian Rosetta Stone) and dealing with short inscriptions, epigraphists cannot definitively map phonemes.
Q.19 (A) Cosmetic artifacts in inhumation burials reflect vanity, refined body care, specialized metal polishing, and religious conceptions of post-mortem existence.
Q.20 (A) Excavations at Rakhigarhi in Haryana show a vast cluster of mounds spanning over 350 hectares, confirming it as the largest known Indus metropolis.
Q.21 (A) Paleoclimate studies show weakened summer monsoons after 2000 BCE and drying of the Ghaggar-Hakra system, causing urban de-densification and eastward rural migration.
Q.22 (A) The Late Harappan phase saw the collapse of centralized civic institutions, long-distance trade, and writing, giving way to rural squatter settlements.
Q.23 (A) The Pashupati seal from Mohenjo-daro depicts a three-faced horned deity in yogic posture flanked by four wild beasts, interpreted by Sir John Marshall as proto-Shiva.
Q.24 (A) Control of fire expanded the human niche: cooked food softened fibers and denatured toxins, drastically improving hominid energy intake and survival.
Q.25 (A) The post-glacial Holocene Mesolithic saw smaller geometric microliths hafted as arrowheads and sickles, alongside early faunal domestication.
