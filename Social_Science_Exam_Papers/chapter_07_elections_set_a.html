OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET A)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & Conceptual Rationale
Q.1 (A) Elections provide a peaceful mechanism for citizens to select their representatives, endorse policies, and replace non-performing leaders.
Q.2 (A) India is divided into 543 Lok Sabha constituencies, each electing one Member of Parliament (MP) to the lower house.
Q.3 (A) Voters in state assembly constituencies elect a Member of Legislative Assembly (MLA) to represent them in the Vidhan Sabha.
Q.4 (A) Reserved constituencies ensure that SC and ST communities, who might otherwise lack financial or social resources to win against elites, have voice in legislatures.
Q.5 (A) Under the 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments, not less than one-third (33%) of all seats and chairperson positions are reserved for women in local bodies.
Q.6 (A) The Electoral Roll, commonly known as the Voters' List, is the official register of eligible voters prepared ahead of elections.
Q.7 (A) The 61st Constitutional Amendment Act (1988) reduced the voting age from 21 years to 18 years for all Indian citizens.
Q.8 (A) To contest Lok Sabha or Vidhan Sabha elections, a candidate must be an Indian citizen of at least 25 years of age.
Q.9 (A) Candidates must disclose criminal antecedents, assets/liabilities, and education in sworn affidavits so voters can make informed choices.
Q.10 (A) The Congress led by Indira Gandhi swept the 1971 elections with the iconic rallying slogan 'Garibi Hatao' (Remove Poverty).
Q.11 (A) The Janata Party under Jayaprakash Narayan fought the post-Emergency 1977 elections on the banner of 'Save Democracy'.
Q.12 (A) The Representation of the People Act prohibits bribery, religious/caste appeals, use of places of worship, and misuse of government resources.
Q.13 (A) The spending ceiling for a Lok Sabha parliamentary constituency was enhanced to ₹95 lakhs (in larger states) to check corrupt money power.
Q.14 (A) The Model Code of Conduct (MCC) comes into force immediately upon election announcement, barring ministers from laying foundation stones or using state machinery.
Q.15 (A) Ministers cannot announce new welfare schemes or foundation stones to unfairly influence voters once the election schedule is gazetted.
Q.16 (A) Article 324 of the Indian Constitution vests the superintendence, direction, and control of elections in the independent Election Commission of India.
Q.17 (A) The CEC enjoys security of tenure like a Supreme Court judge and cannot be arbitrarily dismissed by the government.
Q.18 (A) During the election period, government officers and police work under the deputation and disciplinary control of the Election Commission, not the state government.
Q.19 (A) Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs) record votes securely and prevent booth capturing and fraudulent ballot stuffing.
Q.20 (A) VVPAT generates an instant paper slip visible behind glass for 7 seconds, providing an independent physical audit trail to verify EVM integrity.
Q.21 (A) Indelible ink containing silver nitrate is applied to the voter's finger, leaving a stain that cannot be washed off for several weeks.
Q.22 (A) Voter turnout measures citizen participation, calculated as the proportion of registered voters who actually vote on election day.
Q.23 (A) In India, poor, rural, and marginalized voters turn out in high numbers because they recognize that voting forces parties to address their livelihood needs.
Q.24 (A) NOTA allows voters to exercise their democratic right to reject all candidates in a constituency if they find none suitable.
Q.25 (A) Major challenges in Indian elections include the disproportionate influence of black money, candidates with criminal backgrounds, and dynastic tickets.
