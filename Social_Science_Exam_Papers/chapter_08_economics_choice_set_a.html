OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET A)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & Conceptual Rationale
Q.1 (A) Economics arises because resources (land, labour, capital) are limited (scarce) relative to unlimited human wants, forcing societies to make choices.
Q.2 (A) Scarcity means that available productive resources are insufficient to produce all the goods and services people desire to consume.
Q.3 (A) Opportunity cost is the foregone benefit of the next best alternative choice. Choosing to study an hour means giving up an hour of leisure.
Q.4 (A) By choosing wheat, the farmer foregoes the harvest and earnings from mustard; that sacrificed value is the opportunity cost.
Q.5 (A) The four fundamental factors of production are Land (natural resources), Labour (human effort), Capital (tools/machinery), and Human Capital (knowledge/enterprise).
Q.6 (A) In economics, 'Land' encompasses all free gifts of nature: agricultural land, minerals, rivers, forests, and fossil fuels.
Q.7 (A) Fixed capital includes durable physical assets (tractors, turbines, factory sheds) that survive multiple production cycles over years.
Q.8 (A) Working capital consists of raw materials and immediate cash required to pay wages and day-to-day operating expenses in production.
Q.9 (A) Human capital refers to the stock of skills, knowledge, health, and entrepreneurial capability embodied in the workforce.
Q.10 (A) In market systems, entrepreneurs are motivated by the prospect of earning economic profit (revenue exceeding production costs).
Q.11 (A) Every economic system must decide: (1) What goods to produce and in what quantities, (2) How to produce them (technology/mix), and (3) For whom to produce (distribution).
Q.12 (A) 'How to produce' addresses production technique: whether to employ more human labor (labour-intensive) or more machinery (capital-intensive).
Q.13 (A) 'For whom to produce' concerns how national output and income are shared among different citizens and social groups.
Q.14 (A) In a command economy, the state owns productive assets and central planners make decisions on production, allocation, and prices.
Q.15 (A) A market economy relies on price signals: prices rise when demand is high, prompting producers to allocate resources to profitable goods.
Q.16 (A) India operates a mixed economy where both private enterprise and government coexist, with state regulation protecting public welfare.
Q.17 (A) Education and health enhance human capabilities, enabling workers to innovate and generate higher economic productivity.
Q.18 (A) Economic activities involve the production, distribution, or consumption of goods and services performed to earn a monetary livelihood.
Q.19 (A) Households face budget constraints, requiring them to rank preferences rationally to fulfill basic needs before luxuries.
Q.20 (A) The payment made for the use of land is Rent; for Labour it is Wages; for Capital it is Interest; and for Entrepreneurship it is Profit.
Q.21 (A) Labour is remunerated through wages or salaries paid for services rendered in the production process.
Q.22 (A) Profit is the residual reward earned by the entrepreneur after paying rent for land, wages to labour, and interest on capital.
Q.23 (A) The Primary sector involves activities that directly harvest or extract raw materials from nature (farming, fishing, mining).
Q.24 (A) The Secondary sector transforms raw materials produced by the primary sector into finished industrial goods through manufacturing.
Q.25 (A) The Tertiary sector provides support services to the primary and secondary sectors, facilitating trade, finance, education, and health.
