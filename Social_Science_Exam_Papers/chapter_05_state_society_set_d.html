SECTION A: CORE THEORETICAL PRINCIPLES (Q.1 TO Q.10) [10 MARKS]
Q.1

Assertion (A): In the Early Vedic period, the political authority of the Rajan was checked by popular assemblies.

Reason (R): The Sabha and Samiti deliberated on military campaigns, distributed booty, and selected tribal leaders.

Q.2

Assertion (A): Magadha emerged as the most preeminent and powerful Mahajanapada in ancient North India.

Reason (R): Magadha possessed rich iron ore deposits in its vicinity, fertile Gangetic alluvium for high crop yields, and dense forests supplying timber and war elephants.

Q.3

Assertion (A): The Vajji confederacy was governed as an absolute hereditary monarchy.

Reason (R): Vajji was a Gana-Sangha (oligarchic republic) where power was shared among a council of elected clan chiefs called Rajas.

Q.4

Assertion (A): Kautilya's Arthashastra articulates the Saptanga theory of the state comprising seven essential elements.

Reason (R): According to Kautilya, a viable state requires Swami (King), Amatya (Ministers), Janapada (Territory & People), Durga (Fort), Kosha (Treasury), Danda (Army), and Mitra (Ally).

Q.5

Assertion (A): Emperor Ashoka abandoned the policy of Bherighosha (conquest by war) in favor of Dhammaghosha (conquest by righteousness).

Reason (R): Ashoka was deeply remorseful after witnessing the massive slaughter, captivity, and human misery caused by the Kalinga War.

Q.6

Assertion (A): Ashoka used Sanskrit exclusively in all of his royal pillar and rock edicts across the Mauryan Empire.

Reason (R): To reach ordinary subjects directly, Ashoka utilized local vernacular Prakrit scripts (Brahmi and Kharosthi) in India, as well as Greek and Aramaic in the northwest.

Q.7

Assertion (A): The Gupta period is often described as the classical golden age of ancient Indian cultural and scientific achievements.

Reason (R): During the Gupta era, major advancements were achieved in astronomy, mathematics, metallurgy, sculpture, and classical Sanskrit literature.

Q.8

Assertion (A): Aryabhata stated that the Earth is stationary and located at the geometric center of the universe.

Reason (R): Aryabhata proposed that the spherical Earth rotates on its own axis, causing the apparent daily rising and setting of the stars and sun.

Q.9

Assertion (A): The Chola village administration at Uttaramerur selected committee members through a democratic secret ballot system using electronic machines.

Reason (R): Uttaramerur inscriptions document the Kudavolai system where palm-leaf tickets containing names of eligible candidates were drawn from a pot by a young boy.

Q.10

Assertion (A): The Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayag Prashasti) composed by Harishena extols the military conquests of Chandragupta Maurya.

Reason (R): The Prayag Prashasti was composed in praise of the Gupta emperor Samudragupta, detailing his extensive military campaigns across northern and southern India.

SECTION B: EVIDENTIARY PROOFS & MECHANISMS (Q.11 TO Q.18) [8 MARKS]
Q.11

Assertion (A): The Mauryan Empire maintained an elaborate centralized espionage and surveillance system.

Reason (R): Kautilya believed secret informants (Gudhapurushas) were vital to prevent administrative corruption, detect treason, and monitor provincial governors.

Q.12

Assertion (A): Land grants (Agraharas and Brahmadeyas) in the post-Gupta era contributed to political decentralization and feudal tendencies.

Reason (R): Rulers transferred not only revenue collection rights but also judicial and administrative authority over villages to religious beneficiaries and feudatories.

Q.13

Assertion (A): In ancient India, merchant guilds were known as 'Shrenis'.

Reason (R): Shrenis trained apprentices, established price and quality standards, maintained private militias, and served as banking institutions for public deposits.

Q.14

Assertion (A): The Mehrauli Iron Pillar in Delhi exhibits exceptional metallurgical expertise due to its total lack of rusting over 1,600 years.

Reason (R): Ancient Indian metallurgists created wrought iron with high phosphorus content and low sulfur, forming a protective passive film of misawite on its surface.

Q.15

Assertion (A): Emperor Harshavardhana successfully conquered the Deccan by defeating Pulakeshin II on the banks of the Narmada.

Reason (R): The Aihole inscription composed by Ravikirti records that the Chalukya king Pulakeshin II decisively checked Harsha's southward military advance.

Q.16

Assertion (A): Ashoka's 12th Major Rock Edict advocates for the promotion of mutual tolerance and respect among diverse religious sects.

Reason (R): Ashoka asserted that praising one's own religious sect while condemning others damages one's own faith more severely.

Q.17

Assertion (A): The Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang visited India during the reign of Chandragupta Maurya.

Reason (R): Xuanzang studied Buddhist scriptures at Nalanda University during the reign of King Harshavardhana in the 7th century CE.

Q.18

Assertion (A): The Chola Navy under Rajendra I conducted naval expeditions across the Bay of Bengal against the Srivijaya Kingdom in Southeast Asia.

Reason (R): Chola naval campaigns were launched to protect maritime trade routes and eliminate piratical trade blockades between South India and Song dynasty China.

SECTION C: ADVANCED NUANCES & INSTITUTIONAL COMPLEXITIES (Q.19 TO Q.25) [7 MARKS]
Q.19

Assertion (A): The transition from the Early Vedic pastoral economy to the Later Vedic sedentary agrarian economy was driven by iron technology.

Reason (R): Iron axes and iron-tipped ploughshares enabled the clearing of dense tropical forests and cultivation of hard alluvial soils in the middle Gangetic basin.

Q.20

Assertion (A): The caste (Jati) hierarchy in ancient India was completely static and prohibited any form of occupational or social mobility.

Reason (R): Inscriptions, such as the Mandasor inscription of silk weavers, show that craft guilds could migrate, adopt alternative professions, and enhance their social status.

Q.21

Assertion (A): Coins issued by the Indo-Greek rulers introduced portraiture and bilingual inscriptions in ancient Indian numismatics.

Reason (R): Indo-Greek coins carried realistic portraits of reigning monarchs on the obverse and Greek legends alongside Kharosthi/Prakrit translations on the reverse.

Q.22

Assertion (A): The monolithic rock-cut Kailash Temple at Ellora was constructed by building stone blocks from the bottom ground up.

Reason (R): Rashtrakuta sculptors excavated the temple out of a single basalt cliff, carving downward from the top summit to the base without scaffolding.

Q.23

Assertion (A): Kautilya's Arthashastra advises that the king should prioritize personal luxury and amusement over administrative justice.

Reason (R): The Arthashastra famously states: 'In the happiness of his subjects lies the king's happiness; in their welfare his welfare.'

Q.24

Assertion (A): Sangam literature provides invaluable contemporary insights into the socioeconomic and political life of early Tamilakam.

Reason (R): Sangam poetry classifies landscapes into five ecological eco-zones (Tinais), each associated with distinct subsistence activities, social customs, and moods.

Q.25

Assertion (A): The Brihadisvara Temple at Thanjavur was built by the Pallava king Narasimhavarman I.

Reason (R): The Brihadisvara Temple, dedicated to Lord Shiva, was commissioned by the Chola emperor Rajaraja I in 1010 CE as a monumental architectural masterpiece.
