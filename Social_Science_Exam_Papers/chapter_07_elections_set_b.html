Q.1 In India's 'First-Past-The-Post' (FPTP) electoral system for Lok Sabha elections, how is the winning candidate in a single-member constituency determined?
Q.2 What major distortion can occur between national 'vote share' and 'seat share' in an FPTP electoral system?
Q.3 Why does the Indian Constitution provide for 'Reserved Constituencies' (SC and ST) rather than 'Separate Electorates' as was practiced during British colonial rule?
Q.4 What is the constitutional process of 'Delimitation' conducted by the Delimitation Commission of India?
Q.5 Why is the Delimitation Commission's final order legally protected from being challenged in any court of law under Article 329 of the Constitution?
Q.6 How does the Election Commission of India (ECI) maintain constitutional autonomy from the ruling executive government?
Q.7 When the Model Code of Conduct (MCC) takes effect upon the announcement of an election schedule, which of the following executive actions is strictly BANNED?
Q.8 Under landmark Supreme Court directives (*Association for Democratic Reforms*, 2002), what sworn declarations must candidates file in their nomination affidavits?
Q.9 How does the Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT) system attached to an Electronic Voting Machine (EVM) enhance electoral integrity?
Q.10 What legal sanction does the Representation of the People Act, 1951 impose for practicing 'corrupt practices' such as bribing or intimidating voters or appealing to religion/caste?
Q.11 Why does India experience a distinctive 'participatory upsurge' where marginalized, rural, and lower-income citizens vote in higher percentages than wealthy urban elites?
Q.12 What is the function of the 'None of the Above' (NOTA) button on an Indian Electronic Voting Machine?
Q.13 What is the primary rationale behind capping election campaign expenditure limits for candidates in parliamentary and assembly elections?
Q.14 During the actual conduct of elections, who holds disciplinary and administrative authority over state civil servants, district magistrates, and police officers?
Q.15 What is 'booth capturing', and how did the introduction of Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs) systematically curb this practice?
Q.16 What is the primary role of the 'General Observer' and 'Expenditure Observer' appointed by the Election Commission during an election?
Q.17 Why is the practice of 'paid news' (disguising promotional political propaganda as independent journalistic reporting) considered a grave threat to free elections?
Q.18 What constitutional protection does the 'Secret Ballot' ensure for every citizen in a democracy?
Q.19 How does the 'cVIGIL' mobile application launched by the Election Commission empower ordinary Indian citizens to enforce election integrity?
Q.20 Why is the continuous summary revision of the Electoral Roll (Voters' List) conducted annually by the Election Commission?
Q.21 What is the legal purpose of applying indelible ink (containing silver nitrate) to a voter's left index finger at the polling booth?
Q.22 How does dynasty politics (family control over party candidacies) undermine the democratic quality of Indian elections?
Q.23 What is a 'mid-term election' versus a 'by-election' in Indian parliamentary democracy?
Q.24 Why does the Law Commission of India advocate for state funding of elections or stringent transparency in political party contributions?
Q.25 What democratic purpose is served by the 'silence period' (the ban on public campaigning 48 hours prior to the close of voting)?

OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET B)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & Analytical Rationale
Q.1 (A) Under the FPTP system, plurality wins: the candidate with more votes than any single rival is elected, frequently winning with 30–40% in multi-cornered contests.
Q.2 (A) FPTP exaggerates seat share for the leading party across divided constituencies, meaning popular vote percentages rarely match legislative seat proportions.
Q.3 (A) Dr. Ambedkar and Mahatma Gandhi's Poona Pact (1932) established joint electorates with reserved seats: only SC/ST candidates contest, but all citizens in the constituency vote.
Q.4 (A) The independent Delimitation Commission redraws constituency boundaries after decennial censuses to preserve the 'one vote, one value' population parity principle.
Q.5 (A) Article 329 bars judicial interference in delimitation orders to prevent legal petitions from freezing or paralyzing scheduled election cycles.
Q.6 (A) Article 324 guarantees institutional insulation: the CEC cannot be removed by executive whim and their conditions of service cannot be altered to their disadvantage.
Q.7 (A) The MCC prevents ruling incumbents from using state exchequer resources, official vehicles, or last-minute project launches to unfairly bias voters.
Q.8 (A) Voters' Right to Information mandates sworn affidavits disclosing criminal records, wealth, liabilities, and education so voters can make informed, enlightened choices.
Q.9 (A) VVPAT provides physical verification: voters see their ballot printed on paper before it drops into a sealed box, allowing manual verification of electronic tallies.
Q.10 (A) Section 123 of the RPA criminalizes bribery, caste appeals, and booth capturing; High Courts can set aside election results and disqualify violators.
Q.11 (A) In Indian democracy, voting empowers marginalized groups to exert political leverage, demand welfare benefits, and challenge entrenched social hierarchies.
Q.12 (A) Introduced following the Supreme Court's *PUCL* ruling (2013), NOTA provides a confidential option to register disapproval of all candidates.
Q.13 (A) Statutory expenditure ceilings curb black money and exorbitant spending, preventing wealthy elites from monopolizing electoral visibility.
Q.14 (A) Officers on election duty are on deemed deputation to the ECI under Article 324; the Commission can transfer, suspend, or reprimand officials who exhibit bias.
Q.15 (A) EVMs enforce a 5-vote-per-minute rate limiter, preventing instant ballot stuffing; presiding officers can press the 'Close' button to freeze balloting instantly during attacks.
Q.16 (A) Independent Central Observers (IAS/IRS officers deployed outside their home cadres) oversee operations, shadow candidate expenditure, and ensure free and fair administration.
Q.17 (A) Paid news is deceptive corrupt expenditure: it launders partisan campaign propaganda as neutral journalism, misleading voters and evading statutory spending ceilings.
Q.18 (A) Secret balloting protects individual conscience: voters can vote against powerful landlords, employers, or party bosses without fear of retaliation.
Q.19 (A) cVIGIL crowdsources electoral vigilance: citizens upload geo-tagged photos/videos of MCC violations, triggering field flying squads within 100 minutes.
Q.20 (A) Annual summary revisions update the electoral roll, ensuring young adults enroll at 18 while removing deceased or permanently migrated electors to prevent impersonation.
Q.21 (A) Manufactured by Mysore Paints, silver nitrate ink bonds with skin keratin and darkens on exposure to light, preventing multiple voting across booths.
Q.22 (A) Dynastic succession concentrates ticket distribution within family networks, shutting out qualified grassroots leaders and reducing democratic accountability.
Q.23 (A) A mid-term election renews the entire legislative chamber following premature dissolution; a by-election fills an isolated constituency vacancy.
Q.24 (A) Transparent party financing and limited public funding decouple electoral politics from covert corporate quid-pro-quo financing.
Q.25 (A) Section 126 of the RPA imposes a 48-hour campaign blackout, offering electors calm reflection away from shrill rallies, disinformation, and canvassing.
