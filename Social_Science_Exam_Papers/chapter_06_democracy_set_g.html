Q1. In political philosophy, how does Montesquieu's doctrine of 'Separation of Powers' (Trias Politica) serve as an indispensable cornerstone of constitutional democracy?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Montesquieu formulated that political liberty exists only when legislative, executive, and judicial powers are separated, preventing any single body from exercising tyrannical tyranny over citizens.
Q2. What is the critical distinction between 'Procedural Democracy' and 'Substantive Democracy' in contemporary political theory?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Procedural democracy satisfies formal constitutional rules (elections, rule of law), while substantive democracy realizes the core values of justice, equality of opportunity, and socio-economic empowerment.
Q3. Under the landmark 'Basic Structure Doctrine' formulated by the Supreme Court of India in Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973), which principle protects the core tenets of democracy from parliamentary amendment?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Kesavananda Bharati ruling held that while Parliament has broad amending powers under Article 368, it cannot alter the 'Basic Structure' of the Constitution, which includes democracy and republican form of government.
Q4. Which historical case exemplifies how an elected democratic leader systematically dismantled constitutional checks to establish an authoritarian regime through legal decrees?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Robert Mugabe's ZANU-PF government in Zimbabwe used formal constitutional amendments, press censorship, and harassment of opposition activists to entrench authoritarian rule behind a veneer of regular elections.
Q5. Why is the Pakistani military dictator General Pervez Musharraf's issuance of the 'Legal Framework Order' (LFO) in August 2002 considered antithetical to democratic rule?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The 2002 Legal Framework Order granted General Musharraf autocratic powers to dismiss elected parliaments, appoint military chiefs, and install military oversight over civilian governance, subverting true democracy.
Q6. How did Mexico's Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) maintain unbroken political dominance for 71 years (1929–2000) despite conducting regular six-year presidential elections?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The PRI weaponized government patronage, coerced civil servants and unions, monopolized media coverage, and manipulated election day infrastructure, creating an unfair playing field.
Q7. What fundamental democratic principle is violated when Fiji's electoral system accords greater mathematical weight to the vote of an indigenous Fijian than to that of an Indo-Fijian citizen?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Democratic equality mandates that every adult citizen possesses one vote and each vote carries equal mathematical value. Weighted voting based on ethnicity violates Universal Political Equality.
Q8. In A.V. Dicey's classical formulation, what are the three core components of the 'Rule of Law' that distinguish a democracy from an autocracy?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Dicey defined the Rule of Law by three tenets: absence of arbitrary power (supremacy of regular law), equal subjection of all classes to ordinary law, and the primacy of individual rights enforced by courts.
Q9. Which criterion differentiates a 'Majoritarian Tyranny' from a genuine constitutional democracy?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Democracy is not merely 51% subjugating 49%. Constitutional democracy protects fundamental rights of minorities against majoritarian overreach through institutional checks and judicial review.
Q10. What argument did Nobel laureate Amartya Sen advance regarding democracy and the prevention of catastrophic famines?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Sen demonstrated that democracy, through opposition criticism, free journalism, and periodic elections, forces governments to take prompt preventive action against famines, contrasting democratic India with Mao's China.
Q11. In ancient Athens, what was the function of the democratic institution of 'Ostracism'?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Athenian citizens inscribed names on pottery fragments (ostraka); if 6,000 votes were cast, the individual was exiled for 10 years without loss of property, neutralising potential tyrants.
Q12. Why does a genuine democracy require an 'Autonomous and Independent Judiciary'?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: An independent judiciary acts as the sentinel qui vive of the constitution, possessing the power of judicial review to strike down unconstitutional legislation and protect individual freedoms.
Q13. In the Polish struggle for democracy leading up to 1989, what was 'Solidarity' (Solidarność)?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Solidarity arose in the Gdansk shipyards under Lech Wałęsa, becoming a mass social movement that shattered single-party communist monopoly and ushered in democratic transitions in Eastern Europe.
Q14. Why is freedom of speech and expression (protected under Article 19(1)(a) of the Indian Constitution) considered the 'lifeblood' of a democratic polity?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Freedom of speech enables vibrant public deliberation, oversight of governmental power, and peaceful dissent, without which periodic voting becomes an empty formality.
Q15. What distinguishes direct democracy from representative indirect democracy?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: In direct democracy, eligible citizens vote directly on legislation (referendums/initiatives), while representative democracy delegates legislative authority to elected parliamentary representatives.
Q16. Which institutional mechanism provides 'Horizontal Accountability' within a democratic constitutional framework?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Horizontal accountability refers to internal state oversight where separate constitutional organs (judiciary, legislative committees, CAG, ombudsman) monitor and check executive transgressions.
Q17. Why is the Chinese system of government considered non-democratic despite holding periodic elections to the National People's Congress?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: China's single-party system lacks true political contestation: no candidate can contest without CCP clearance, denying voters an authentic choice to change the governing regime.
Q18. What is the defining purpose of a 'Constitutional Bill of Rights' in modern democracies?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: A Bill of Rights enshrines inviolable human liberties (speech, faith, equality, due process) as supreme constitutional law, insulating citizens against arbitrary executive or legislative infringement.
Q19. In Alexis de Tocqueville's classic analysis 'Democracy in America' (1835), what did he identify as a major peril inherent to egalitarian democratic societies?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Tocqueville warned that in egalitarian democracies, overwhelming majoritarian consensus could tyrannize minority viewpoints, producing cultural conformism and intellectual docility.
Q20. Why does democratic governance lead to better policy decisions compared to autocratic rule?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Deliberative democracy requires extensive consultation and consensus-building, allowing errors to be detected and corrected before implementation, unlike hasty autocratic decrees.
Q21. What role does the 'Loyal Opposition' play in a functioning parliamentary democracy?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The parliamentary Opposition acts as a constructive critic: it questions ministers, debates policy flaws in standing committees, and represents dissenting citizen interests within constitutional bounds.
Q22. Which criterion constitutes the most reliable test of whether a nation is a genuine democracy?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Adam Przeworski famously encapsulated democracy as 'a system in which parties lose elections.' The real test is the incumbents' willingness to accept electoral defeat and transfer power peacefully.
Q23. What is the danger of 'Democratic Backsliding' (or Autocratic Legalism) observed in contemporary global politics?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Democratic backsliding involves elected incumbents systematically subverting checks and balances, weakening courts, and capturing public media under a facade of legality.
Q24. Under the concept of 'Deliberative Democracy', the legitimacy of a state law derives primarily from:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Deliberative democracy (developed by Habermas and Rawls) holds that legitimate law emerges from authentic, reasoned dialogue and inclusive justification among equal citizens, not just raw headcounts.
Q25. Why is the right to peacefully assemble and protest protected as an essential element of democratic citizenship?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Peaceful assembly provides a vital conduit for civic participation between elections, allowing marginalized communities to highlight grievances and advocate reform within constitutional parameters.
