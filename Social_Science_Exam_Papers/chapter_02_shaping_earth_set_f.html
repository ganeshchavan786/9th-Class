OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & RAPID RECALL EXPLANATIONS (SET F)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Concept & Rapid Recall Analysis
Q.1 (B) Endogenic forces originate from thermal and radioactive energy within the Earth, driving diastrophism and volcanism.
Q.2 (B) The epicentre is the surface point situated directly perpendicular to the underground focus.
Q.3 (B) P-waves are longitudinal compressional body waves that travel fastest and penetrate all phases of matter.
Q.4 (B) S-waves require shear rigidity and cannot propagate through liquid media, creating a broad shadow zone beyond 105°.
Q.5 (B) The Himalayas are young fold mountains formed by the ongoing collision between the Indian and Eurasian tectonic plates.
Q.6 (B) Divergent boundaries occur where plates move apart, creating mid-ocean ridges and continental rift valleys.
Q.7 (B) The San Andreas Fault is a transform plate boundary where the Pacific and North American plates slide horizontally.
Q.8 (B) Weathering is static breakdown in situ; erosion involves detachment and active transport by moving agents.
Q.9 (B) Vertical hydraulic erosion dominates the youthful course of a mountain river, carving narrow, steep V-shaped valleys.
Q.10 (A) Oxbow lakes are cutoff meander loops stranded on mature floodplains during high-discharge flood breaches.
Q.11 (B) Simultaneous basal abrasion and lateral plucking by moving glacial ice carves broad U-shaped troughs.
Q.12 (B) Cirques are steep-walled, bowl-shaped hollows excavated near mountain summits by freeze-thaw and glacial plucking.
Q.13 (B) Moraines are linear ridges of unsorted boulders, gravel, and clay deposited by advancing or melting glaciers.
Q.14 (B) Barchans are crescentic sand dunes whose curved horns extend downwind in regions with unidirectional wind.
Q.15 (A) Wind sand abrasion concentrates within the bottom 1-2 meters above ground, undercutting rocks into mushroom shapes.
Q.16 (A) Loess consists of wind-blown, yellowish silt-sized dust deposited over vast areas downwind of arid deserts.
Q.17 (B) Sea stacks are isolated rocky columns left standing in the ocean after wave collapse of coastal sea arches.
Q.18 (A) Sinkholes are dissolution depressions formed when groundwater dissolves subterranean limestone roofs.
Q.19 (B) Stalagmites grow upward from the cave floor; stalactites hang downward from the cave ceiling.
Q.20 (A) Alluvial fans form when sudden slope deceleration forces mountain streams to dump coarse bedload at plain junctions.
Q.21 (B) Deltas are wetland depositional landforms built by sediment discharge at river mouths.
Q.22 (B) Volumetric ice expansion exerts immense tensile stress within fractures, shattering jointed rock faces.
Q.23 (B) Oceanic trenches are created where a dense oceanic lithospheric slab subducts beneath another plate.
Q.24 (B) The 'Ring of Fire' is a horseshoe-shaped belt of subduction zones circling the Pacific basin.
Q.25 (B) Liquefaction transforms solid saturated sandy sediments into liquid slurry during cyclic seismic shaking.
