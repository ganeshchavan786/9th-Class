Q.4 In his concluding address to the Constituent Assembly on 25 November 1949, what fundamental contradiction in India's emerging democratic life did Dr. B.R. Ambedkar articulate?

Q.6 How does the principle of 'Majoritarianism' differ from and potentially threaten 'Constitutional Democracy'?

Q.9 How does democratic deliberation improve the quality of legislative policymaking compared to authoritarian decision-making?

OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET B)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & Analytical Rationale
Q.1 (A) Procedural elections can mask authoritarian control (as in Mugabe's Zimbabwe or PRI's Mexico); substantive democracy requires free competition, civil liberties, and institutional checks.
Q.2 (A) Procedural democracy satisfies ballot-box rituals; substantive democracy achieves real democratic equality, safeguarding minorities, social justice, and citizen voice.
Q.3 (A) Judicial review ensures that no state organ transcends constitutional limits, protecting citizen rights from majoritarian or executive tyranny.
Q.4 (A) Ambedkar warned that political democracy (formal one-vote equality) would collapse if social and economic inequalities (caste, wealth concentration) were not dismantled.
Q.5 (A) Sen's entitlement theory proves that famines stem from distribution failures; democratic accountability and free media force governments to mobilize relief before mass starvation occurs.
Q.6 (A) Majoritarian tyranny tramples minority civil liberties; constitutional democracy bounds majority rule by entrenched fundamental rights and the rule of law.
Q.7 (A) A regime cannot be democratic if elected representatives lack ultimate decision-making power and remain subordinate to an unelected military executive.
Q.8 (A) Free speech enables public deliberation, peaceful protest, investigative journalism, and democratic accountability, preventing authoritarian information suppression.
Q.9 (A) While democratic consultation takes time, multi-perspective debate surfaces unintended consequences, improves compliance, and prevents catastrophic unilateral blunders.
Q.10 (A) The PRI's systemic abuse of state apparatus, control over media, coercion of public employees, and electoral engineering eliminated fair competition, denying genuine choice.
Q.11 (A) Unregulated plutocratic campaign spending distorts the democratic norm of political equality, transforming formal democracy into an elite oligarchy.
Q.12 (A) Democratic federalism (Seventh Schedule Union/State/Concurrent lists, linguistic states reorganization) accommodates regional aspirations while preserving national integrity.
Q.13 (A) Without a viable, protected opposition, electoral competition becomes illusory, enabling ruling incumbents to entrench authoritarian power without checks.
Q.14 (A) When political victory is viewed as absolute and defeat as catastrophic, political tolerance erodes, leading to constitutional backsliding and hyper-polarization.
Q.15 (A) RTI empowers sovereign citizens to breach colonial administrative secrecy, holding civil servants accountable for public fund expenditures and welfare delivery.
Q.16 (A) Coalition vulnerabilities may foster short-term opportunistic bargaining and policy instability, though they also promote consensual broad-based representation.
Q.17 (A) The Basic Structure doctrine bars Parliament from using Article 368 to amend core pillars of the Constitution, including democracy, secularism, judicial review, and fundamental rights.
Q.18 (A) Democratic education cultivates critical inquiry, mutual respect, empathetic dialogue, and collaborative reasoning rather than authoritarian indoctrination.
Q.19 (A) Universal adult suffrage grants radical dignity: at the polling booth, feudal hierarchies dissolve as every adult citizen wields an identical franchise.
Q.20 (A) Democratic politics is an ongoing dialogic process; peaceful public protest channels citizen feedback and checks state arbitrary power between election cycles.
Q.21 (A) Authoritarian plebiscites use coercive intimidation and rigged choices to rubber-stamp dictators; democratic referendums guarantee free debate and secret balloting.
Q.22 (A) Civil society associations mobilize citizen concerns, provide disaster relief, advocate for marginalized communities, and check state and market abuses.
Q.23 (A) Grassroots devolution institutionalizes direct participatory democracy, enabling rural citizens to prioritize drinking water, sanitation, and school investments locally.
Q.24 (A) While technical expertise is essential, public policy choices involve moral values, equity trade-offs, and democratic legitimacy that only elected representatives can balance.
Q.25 (A) Churchill noted that despite human shortcomings, procedural squabbles, and slow deliberations, democracy alone secures human dignity and self-repairing liberties.
