SECTION A: CORE THEORETICAL PRINCIPLES (Q.1 TO Q.10) [10 MARKS]
Q.1

Assertion (A): Scarcity is the fundamental economic problem facing all human societies.

Reason (R): Human wants are virtually unlimited, while the productive economic resources available to satisfy them are finite and limited.

Q.2

Assertion (A): If resources were infinite and abundant, there would be no need for economic study or decision-making.

Reason (R): Economics is the study of how society allocates scarce resources with alternative uses to produce and distribute goods and services.

Q.3

Assertion (A): The concept of Opportunity Cost refers to the total monetary cash expenditure incurred in buying a commodity.

Reason (R): Opportunity cost is defined as the value of the next best alternative sacrifice or foregone opportunity when a choice is made.

Q.4

Assertion (A): The central economic problem of 'What to produce' involves choosing between capital goods and consumer goods.

Reason (R): An economy cannot produce maximum quantities of both consumer goods and capital goods simultaneously due to resource constraints.

Q.5

Assertion (A): The economic question of 'How to produce' deals with the choice of production technique.

Reason (R): Producers must choose between labour-intensive techniques and capital-intensive techniques based on relative resource availability and cost.

Q.6

Assertion (A): The question of 'For whom to produce' is concerned with the distribution of the national product among members of society.

Reason (R): How the national output is shared depends directly on the functional distribution of factor income (wages, rent, interest, and profit).

Q.7

Assertion (A): Land as a factor of production includes only agricultural farm soil.

Reason (R): In economics, land encompasses all natural gifts provided by nature free of human effort, including minerals, rivers, forests, and climate.

Q.8

Assertion (A): Labour as a factor of production is completely separable from the human labourer.

Reason (R): Labour power represents human physical and mental exertion and cannot be bought or stored independently of the worker who embodies it.

Q.9

Assertion (A): Tools, machinery, and factory buildings are classified as Working Capital.

Reason (R): Working capital consists of raw materials and liquid cash in hand used up in a single production cycle, while machinery is Fixed Capital.

Q.10

Assertion (A): Human Capital represents the accumulated knowledge, technical skills, health, and productive capacity of a population.

Reason (R): Investments in education, vocational training, and healthcare transform raw human labor into high-productivity human capital.

SECTION B: EVIDENTIARY PROOFS & MECHANISMS (Q.11 TO Q.18) [8 MARKS]
Q.11

Assertion (A): A Production Possibility Curve (PPC) slopes downwards from left to right.

Reason (R): To produce additional units of one commodity, an economy must transfer scarce resources away from and reduce production of another commodity.

Q.12

Assertion (A): A typical Production Possibility Curve is concave to the origin.

Reason (R): The Marginal Opportunity Cost (Marginal Rate of Transformation) increases as more resources are diverted from one good to another because resources are not equally specialized for all goods.

Q.13

Assertion (A): Any production point situated inside the Production Possibility Curve represents full and efficient resource utilization.

Reason (R): Points inside the PPC indicate that economic resources are unemployed, underemployed, or utilized inefficiently.

Q.14

Assertion (A): A point situated outside the existing Production Possibility Curve is currently unattainable for the economy.

Reason (R): The economy does not possess sufficient productive resources or advanced technology to produce combinations beyond its existing production frontier.

Q.15

Assertion (A): Technological advancements and discovery of new natural resources cause the PPC to shift outward to the right.

Reason (R): An outward shift of the PPC signifies economic growth and an expansion in the economy's maximum production potential.

Q.16

Assertion (A): Widespread destruction of factory infrastructure by an earthquake causes the PPC to shift outward.

Reason (R): A natural disaster destroys physical capital and productive assets, reducing the economy's productive capacity and shifting the PPC inward to the left.

Q.17

Assertion (A): The reward or factor payment received by the entrepreneur for bearing risk and organizing production is 'Profit'.

Reason (R): Profit is the residual revenue remaining after all contractual factor costs (wages, rent, and interest) have been settled.

Q.18

Assertion (A): The Primary sector involves activities that directly extract and utilize natural resources.

Reason (R): Agriculture, forestry, animal husbandry, fishing, and mining are classified under the Primary economic sector.

SECTION C: ADVANCED NUANCES & INSTITUTIONAL COMPLEXITIES (Q.19 TO Q.25) [7 MARKS]
Q.19

Assertion (A): As an economy develops over time, the share of the Primary sector in national income and employment typically increases.

Reason (R): Economic development induces structural transformation where manufacturing (Secondary) and services (Tertiary) expand their contribution while agriculture's relative share declines.

Q.20

Assertion (A): Division of labour and occupational specialization increases overall productive efficiency.

Reason (R): Specialization allows workers to develop mastery in specific repetitive tasks, saves time lost in switching tools, and encourages mechanization.

Q.21

Assertion (A): The Tertiary sector produces tangible physical goods that can be stored in warehouses.

Reason (R): The Tertiary sector consists of intangible services such as banking, transport, insurance, education, and healthcare that support production and consumption.

Q.22

Assertion (A): Sunk costs are historical expenditures that have already been incurred and cannot be recovered.

Reason (R): Rational economic decision-makers must consider future marginal costs and marginal benefits while ignoring past unrecoverable sunk costs.

Q.23

Assertion (A): In economics, 'capital' includes paper currency notes and financial bank deposits directly as physical factors of production.

Reason (R): Physical capital refers to real tangible goods (factories, machines, tools) used to produce other goods, whereas money is a financial medium of exchange.

Q.24

Assertion (A): An economy operating on its PPC is achieving productive efficiency.

Reason (R): On the boundary of the PPC, it is impossible to produce more of one good without sacrificing some amount of another good.

Q.25

Assertion (A): Public goods like street lighting and national defence are efficiently provided by unregulated free private markets.

Reason (R): Public goods are non-excludable and non-rivalrous in consumption, leading to the 'free-rider problem' where private firms cannot profitably exclude non-payers.
