OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET A)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & Conceptual Rationale
Q.1 (A) The Palaeolithic Age (spanning from over 2 million years ago to ~12,000 BCE) was characterized by nomadic hunter-gatherers using unpolished stone tools.
Q.2 (A) The Neolithic Revolution marked the transition from foraging to food production (agriculture, cattle domestication, pottery, and permanent dwellings).
Q.3 (A) Microliths are small geometric stone blades (1 to 5 cm long) characteristic of the Mesolithic transition, mounted as arrowheads or sickle teeth.
Q.4 (A) Bhimbetka in Madhya Pradesh is a UNESCO World Heritage site renowned for its Mesolithic and Upper Palaeolithic rock art and cave dwellings.
Q.5 (A) The Harappan Civilisation was a mature Bronze Age urban civilisation that alloyed copper with tin to produce bronze tools and ornaments.
Q.6 (A) Harappa, located along the Ravi River (in modern Punjab, Pakistan), was excavated first in 1921, giving the civilisation its name.
Q.7 (A) Mohenjo-daro translates to 'Mound of the Dead' in Sindhi and was one of the largest metropolitan centres of the Harappan civilization.
Q.8 (A) Harappan settlements typically featured an elevated, fortified western section known as the Citadel, containing monumental public structures.
Q.9 (A) Harappan town planning was based on an organized grid system where straight, wide streets intersected at approximate 90-degree right angles.
Q.10 (A) The Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro is an iconic brick-built water-tight public bathing pool lined with gypsum mortar and natural bitumen.
Q.11 (A) Lothal in Gujarat had a tidal dockyard, bead-making factories, and storehouses, serving as a primary maritime trading hub with Mesopotamia.
Q.12 (A) Dholavira (in the Rann of Kutch) had a tripartite urban design and a sophisticated rainwater harvesting system of huge rock-cut reservoirs.
Q.13 (A) Kalibangan in Rajasthan revealed evidence of ploughed agricultural fields and ritual brick fire altars.
Q.14 (A) Harappans used standardized kiln-burnt bricks with uniform dimensions (thickness, width, and length in a 1:2:4 ratio) ensuring longevity.
Q.15 (A) Harappan cities had covered brick drains with inspection sumps linking private house bathrooms to main street drains, showing advanced hygiene.
Q.16 (A) Harappan seals were predominantly carved from steatite (soapstone), engraved with animal figures and script, and coated with a white glaze.
Q.17 (A) The 'Dancing Girl' of Mohenjo-daro is an exquisite bronze masterpiece cast using the lost-wax (cire perdue) casting technique.
Q.18 (A) The 'unicorn' motif (a single-horned mythical bovine creature) appears most frequently on Harappan seals, followed by the humped bull.
Q.19 (A) Harappan script consists of roughly 400 pictographic signs written right-to-left, and despite extensive research, it remains undeciphered.
Q.20 (A) Mesopotamian textual records refer to trade with 'Meluhha' (widely recognized by historians as the Indus Valley/Harappan region).
Q.21 (A) Harappans had standard cubical chert weights following a binary system (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 up to 12,800) and decimal multiples for precision.
Q.22 (A) Dholavira yielded a remarkable wooden inscription board embedded with 10 large white crystalline gypsum signs, often called the 'Dholavira Signboard'.
Q.23 (A) The Harappan trading colony of Shortughai in northeastern Afghanistan was established to secure precious Lapis Lazuli gemstones.
Q.24 (A) Harappans were a Bronze Age society and were unaware of iron metallurgy; the Iron Age in India began much later (~1000 BCE).
Q.25 (A) Archaeological consensus points to multiple ecological factors: shifting river channels, prolonged aridity, deforestation, and collapse of regional trade networks.
