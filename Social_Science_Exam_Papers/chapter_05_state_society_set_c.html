OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET C)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & In-Depth Conceptual Solution
Q.1 (A) The Purusha Sukta occurs in the 10th Mandala of the Rigveda, describing the metaphorical emergence of the four Varnas from the cosmic being Purusha.
Q.2 (A) Assaka (with its capital at Potana/Bodhan) was the southernmost Mahajanapada situated along the banks of the Godavari River in modern Maharashtra/Telangana.
Q.3 (A) Under the Arthashastra, the *Samaharta* was the supreme fiscal officer in charge of revenue assessment and collection across the empire, while the *Sannidhata* was the Chief Treasurer.
Q.4 (A) *Vishti* was unpaid forced labor extracted by the state or feudal land-grant beneficiaries from rural villagers and craftsmen for public works and military logistics.
Q.5 (A) Uttaramerur rules: candidates had to be between 35 and 70 years of age, own more than 1/4 *veli* of land, live in a house built on their own site, and possess Vedic and Dharmashastric learning.
Q.6 (A) Failure to render transparent public accounts during a previous committee term disqualified the individual and all their close relatives from future elections.
Q.7 (A) Kautilya divided the espionage apparatus (*Gudhapurushas*) into *Sanstha* (stationary institutions disguised as monks, merchants, or ascetics) and *Sanchara* (itinerant roving agents).
Q.8 (A) Sudraka's *Mrichchhakatika* is a masterwork of Sanskrit dramatic realism, capturing the romance between the impoverished merchant Charudatta and courtesan Vasantasena in ancient Ujjain.
Q.9 (A) *Brahmadeya* was land alienated by kings to Brahmins, exempt from royal taxes (*Sarvaparihara*), serving to expand settled agriculture and Vedic cultural influence into frontiers.
Q.10 (A) Chandragupta II's court at Ujjain boasted the legendary *Navaratnas* (Nine Gems), including Kalidasa, Varahamihira, Amarasimha, Dhanvantari, and Vararuchi.
Q.11 (A) The Sangam five-fold landscape (*Thinai*): Kurinji (mountains), Mullai (forests/pastures), Marutham (fertile agrarian plains), Neythal (coastal seashore), and Palai (arid wilderness).
Q.12 (A) Sushruta is hailed as the 'father of surgery': the *Sushruta Samhita* describes plastic surgery, operative instruments, surgical incisions, and wound disinfection in ancient India.
Q.13 (A) The *Pautavadhyaksha* was the Superintendent of Standard Weights and Measures, inspecting commercial scales and standardizing weights to prevent market fraud.
Q.14 (A) Vallabhi in Saurashtra (Gujarat), patronized by the Maitraka dynasty, was an international Buddhist university renowned for administrative and medical sciences alongside Nalanda.
Q.15 (A) Merchant and artisan guilds were led by a hereditary or elected chief known as the *Jetthaka* (elder) or *Sresthin* (prominent merchant-banker).
Q.16 (A) Prince-ascetic Ilango Adigal composed *Silappadikaram*, narrating Kannagi's quest for justice against the Pandya king of Madurai over her husband Kovalan's wrongful execution.
Q.17 (A) The Gupta Empire was divided into provinces called *Bhuktis* governed by *Uparikas*; *Bhuktis* were further subdivided into districts called *Vishayas* under *Vishayapatis*.
Q.18 (A) Fa-Hien observed Gupta peace and prosperity: governance was mild without corporal execution, and Buddhist ahimsa influenced broad vegetarian habits in urban centers.
Q.19 (A) *Ghatikas* (like the famous Ghatika of Kanchipuram under the Pallavas) were renowned collegiate centers of higher theological, legal, and philosophical debates.
Q.20 (A) Major Rock Edict XIII expresses Ashoka's profound grief over the slaughter of 100,000 people and deportation of 150,000 in Kalinga, proclaiming his shift to conquest by righteousness.
Q.21 (A) The Uttaramerur inscription details: palm-leaf tickets (*Kudavolai*) carrying candidates' names were bundled and placed into an earthen pot (*Kuda*), then picked by a young boy.
Q.22 (A) Wootz (from Tamil *urukku* / Kannada *ukku*) was high-carbon crucible steel exported across the Indian Ocean to Damascus, famed for hardness and sharp cutting edges.
Q.23 (A) The *Manusmriti* is the most widely cited classical legal treatise (*Dharmashastra*), compiling customary jurisprudence, varna-ashrama duties, and kingly justice (*Rajadharma*).
Q.24 (A) In ancient Tamil urban typology, *Pattinam* specifically denoted a flourishing maritime port settlement engaged in coastal and trans-oceanic shipping.
Q.25 (A) Kushana numismatic iconography was cosmopolitan and syncretic, depicting the emperor in nomadic dress making offerings at altars while featuring Greek, Zoroastrian, and Indian deities.
