SECTION A: CORE THEORETICAL PRINCIPLES (Q.1 TO Q.10) [10 MARKS]
Q.1

Assertion (A): Endogenic geomorphic forces derive their energy primarily from radioactivity, rotational friction, and primordial heat within the Earth.

Reason (R): Endogenic forces induce diastrophism and volcanism, leading to the elevation and deformation of the Earth's lithosphere.

Q.2

Assertion (A): The Himalayas are classified as young fold mountains formed by the collision of tectonic plates.

Reason (R): The Indian plate collided with the Eurasian plate at a convergent boundary, crumpling marine sediments of the Tethys geosyncline into massive folds.

Q.3

Assertion (A): Seismic S-waves can propagate freely through the Earth's liquid outer core.

Reason (R): Transverse shear waves (S-waves) require shear elasticity and cannot propagate through fluids lacking shear rigidity.

Q.4

Assertion (A): The epicentre of an earthquake experiences the earliest arrival of seismic body waves.

Reason (R): The epicentre is the point on the Earth's surface situated perpendicularly above the subterranean hypocentre (focus).

Q.5

Assertion (A): Mid-oceanic ridges are formed along divergent plate boundaries where tectonic plates pull apart.

Reason (R): Upwelling basaltic magma from the asthenosphere cools and solidifies along the rift zone, continuously generating new oceanic crust.

Q.6

Assertion (A): Transform faults neither create nor destroy lithospheric crust.

Reason (R): At transform plate boundaries, lithospheric plates slide horizontally past one another along strike-slip fractures.

Q.7

Assertion (A): Chemical weathering proceeds at the most rapid rate in hyper-arid hot desert environments.

Reason (R): Chemical weathering processes such as carbonation, hydration, and oxidation require liquid water as a primary solvent and reaction medium.

Q.8

Assertion (A): Frost wedging is a potent mechanical weathering agent in high-altitude periglacial mountain regions.

Reason (R): Water expands by approximately 9% in volume when freezing into ice, exerting immense tensile pressure within rock joints.

Q.9

Assertion (A): Weathering and erosion are identical geomorphic terms that can be used interchangeably.

Reason (R): Weathering involves the in-situ disintegration of rocks without transport, whereas erosion involves the detachment and dynamic transportation of debris.

Q.10

Assertion (A): Deep-sea oceanic trenches are located predominantly along convergent subduction plate boundaries.

Reason (R): When a dense oceanic plate collides with a lighter continental or island arc plate, the oceanic slab flexes and subducts into the asthenosphere.

SECTION B: EVIDENTIARY PROOFS & MECHANISMS (Q.11 TO Q.18) [8 MARKS]
Q.11

Assertion (A): A river forms a V-shaped valley during its youthful stage in mountainous terrain.

Reason (R): In its upper course, a steep hydraulic gradient causes vertical downward erosion to vastly exceed lateral bank erosion.

Q.12

Assertion (A): Oxbow lakes are formed along the upper youthful course of mountain torrents.

Reason (R): Oxbow lakes originate on low-gradient mature floodplains when meander loops are cut off during seasonal flood discharges.

Q.13

Assertion (A): Glaciers carve distinct U-shaped valleys with flat valley floors and steep vertical walls.

Reason (R): Glacial ice fills preexisting river valleys and erodes simultaneously across the base and lateral rock margins via plucking and abrasion.

Q.14

Assertion (A): Cirques are amphitheatre-like hollows carved by glaciers high on mountain slopes.

Reason (R): Freeze-thaw nivation and rotational basal ice plucking excavate deep bedrock depressions below mountain crests.

Q.15

Assertion (A): Mushroom rocks (pedestal rocks) in arid deserts exhibit narrow stems and wide upper caps.

Reason (R): Wind-driven sand abrasion is concentrated primarily within the first 1 to 2 metres above the ground surface.

Q.16

Assertion (A): Barchan sand dunes have their crescent tips or horns pointing against the prevailing wind direction.

Reason (R): Wind pushes sand up the gentle windward slope, allowing sand grains to avalanche down the steep leeward slip face, advancing the horns downwind.

Q.17

Assertion (A): Alluvial fans develop at the base of mountain fronts where streams debouch onto flat plains.

Reason (R): An abrupt reduction in slope gradient causes a sudden drop in stream velocity and immediate deposition of coarse sediment loads.

Q.18

Assertion (A): Deltas are formed at the river mouth when sediment deposition exceeds removal by ocean tides and currents.

Reason (R): River velocity approaches zero upon entering a standing body of water, triggering flocculation of clay and deposition of suspended bedload.

SECTION C: ADVANCED NUANCES & INSTITUTIONAL COMPLEXITIES (Q.19 TO Q.25) [7 MARKS]
Q.19

Assertion (A): Earthquake shadow zones exist for both P-waves and S-waves.

Reason (R): P-waves are refracted at the core-mantle boundary creating a shadow between 105° and 145°, while S-waves are blocked entirely by the liquid outer core beyond 105°.

Q.20

Assertion (A): Exfoliation domes form in granitic rock masses due to unloading and pressure release.

Reason (R): When overlying rock burdens are removed by long-term erosion, underlying plutonic rocks expand outward and spall along curved joints.

Q.21

Assertion (A): Biological weathering is caused exclusively by the physical growth of tree roots inside rock fissures.

Reason (R): Lichens, mosses, and burrowing organisms secrete organic chelating acids that chemically decompose rock silicate minerals.

Q.22

Assertion (A): River meanders continually migrate laterally across their floodplains.

Reason (R): Centrifugal water acceleration causes higher velocity and erosion on the concave outer bank, while lower velocity causes deposition on the convex inner bank.

Q.23

Assertion (A): Soil liquefaction during an earthquake occurs in well-consolidated bedrock foundations.

Reason (R): Liquefaction occurs when saturated loose sandy sediments lose shear strength under cyclic seismic shaking and behave like a fluid.

Q.24

Assertion (A): A wave-cut platform is a depositional coastal landform composed of loose sand dunes.

Reason (R): Wave-cut platforms are flat erosional bedrock benches carved at the base of retreating sea cliffs by hydraulic wave action and abrasion.

Q.25

Assertion (A): The Pacific Rim is referred to as the 'Ring of Fire' due to intense volcanic and seismic activity.

Reason (R): The perimeter of the Pacific Ocean is bounded almost entirely by convergent plate boundaries characterized by subduction zones and deep ocean trenches.
