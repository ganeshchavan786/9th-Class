OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & RAPID RECALL EXPLANATIONS (SET F)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Concept & Rapid Recall Analysis
Q.1 (B) The Law of Demand states that as price rises, quantity demanded falls, and vice versa, other things held constant.
Q.2 (B) Due to the inverse price-quantity relationship, the demand curve slopes downward to the right.
Q.3 (B) Higher prices improve unit profit margins, incentivizing sellers to increase quantity supplied (upward-sloping supply curve).
Q.4 (B) Market equilibrium occurs at the intersection of demand and supply curves where the market clears ($Q_d = Q_s$).
Q.5 (B) Above equilibrium, sellers offer more than buyers want ($Q_s > Q_d$), creating an unsold surplus that forces prices down.
Q.6 (B) Below equilibrium, buyers want more than sellers offer ($Q_d > Q_s$), creating a shortage that bids prices up.
Q.7 (B) Substitute goods satisfy similar consumer needs; an increase in the price of one increases demand for the other.
Q.8 (B) Complementary goods have joint demand; an increase in the price of one reduces demand for the other.
Q.9 (B) A price hike in tea encourages consumers to substitute coffee, shifting the demand curve for coffee rightward.
Q.10 (B) Surging fuel operational costs depress joint consumer demand for petrol cars, shifting the car demand curve leftward.
Q.11 (B) Own-price changes cause movements along the existing curve; non-price factors cause shifts of the curve.
Q.12 (B) Higher income raises consumer purchasing power, shifting the demand curve for normal goods rightward.
Q.13 (B) Inferior goods have a negative income elasticity; as consumers grow richer, they abandon them for superior alternatives.
Q.14 (B) Giffen goods are extreme inferior staples where a strong negative income effect overwhelms substitution, causing an upward-sloping demand curve.
Q.15 (B) Veblen goods derive appeal from exclusivity and high prices, signaling status to society.
Q.16 (B) Inelastic demand ($E_d < 1$) indicates low responsiveness, typical of necessities like salt and life-saving medicines.
Q.17 (B) Readily available close substitutes allow consumers to switch easily if price rises, making demand highly price-elastic.
Q.18 (B) A price ceiling is a legally enforced maximum price set below equilibrium to make essential goods affordable.
Q.19 (B) Artificial price caps expand consumer demand while contracting producer supply, creating chronic shortages.
Q.20 (B) A price floor is a legally mandated minimum price set above equilibrium to guarantee remunerative producer returns.
Q.21 (B) The Minimum Support Price (MSP) guarantees farmers a minimum price floor for mandated agricultural crops.
Q.22 (B) The Food Corporation of India (FCI) procures food grains at MSP and manages national buffer stock reserves.
Q.23 (B) Cost-reducing technology shifts the supply curve rightward, lowering equilibrium price and expanding output.
Q.24 (B) Taxes increase marginal production costs, shifting the supply curve leftward and raising retail prices.
Q.25 (B) The 'Invisible Hand' describes how competitive price signals channel individual self-interest into socially efficient resource allocation.
