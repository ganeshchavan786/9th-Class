OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET A)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & Conceptual Rationale
Q.1 (B) Social Science is the systematic study of human society, social relationships, human behavior, civic institutions, economic systems, and historical evolution.
Q.2 (B) Geography is the study of Earth's physical environments, landforms, atmospheres, and the spatial distribution of human populations and their interaction with the environment.
Q.3 (C) Primary sources are first-hand, contemporary records created during the time period under study, such as inscriptions, coins, royal edicts, and original letters.
Q.4 (B) Epigraphy is the scientific discipline that deals with the study and decipherment of inscriptions carved on stone, metal, or other durable materials.
Q.5 (A) Numismatics is the study of coins, currencies, and medals, providing crucial information about kings, dates, metallurgical skills, and economic trade networks.
Q.6 (B) Economics analyzes how individuals, firms, and governments make choices to allocate scarce resources among competing alternatives.
Q.7 (B) Political Science explores the theory and practice of politics, the functioning of government systems, constitutional structures, and the rights and duties of citizens.
Q.8 (B) Rigorous historical methodology requires corroborating and cross-referencing multiple independent sources (inscriptions, texts, archaeology) to establish historical accuracy.
Q.9 (B) A secondary source is created by someone who did not experience first-hand or participate in the events, but analyzes and interprets primary sources.
Q.10 (A) Human societies are complex; an event like a migration or famine requires geographical, economic, historical, and political insights to understand fully.
Q.11 (B) Archaeology is the scientific study of human history and prehistory through the excavation of sites and analysis of artifacts and physical remains.
Q.12 (A) Oral history captures the lived experiences of marginalized groups and societies that lacked written scripts or formal archives.
Q.13 (A) Radiocarbon dating measures the decay of the radioactive isotope Carbon-14 in organic materials like wood, charcoal, bone, and seeds.
Q.14 (B) Ethical social research requires honesty, integrity, informed consent, respect for cultural diversity, and objective reporting of evidence.
Q.15 (A) Demography is the statistical study of human populations, including size, density, distribution, fertility, mortality, and migration patterns.
Q.16 (A) Anthropology is the scientific study of humans, human behavior, cultures, societies, and both past and present linguistic and physical development.
Q.17 (B) Social Science cultivates analytical thinking, understanding of rights and duties, tolerance of diverse views, and active participation in democratic processes.
Q.18 (B) Archives preserve primary historical government records, state treaties, royal decrees, gazettes, and authentic administrative correspondence.
Q.19 (B) Historiography is the study of historical methodology and how interpretations of the past change across different historical eras and viewpoints.
Q.20 (A) Human-Environment Geography examines the reciprocal interactions between environmental processes and human societal adaptations and modifications.
Q.21 (B) Court chroniclers wrote under the patronage of monarchs, meaning their narratives must be read with awareness of potential political bias and contemporary perspectives.
Q.22 (A) Sociological and historical inquiry explores how institutions, values, and practices transform as societies adopt new tools, economic modes, and ideas.
Q.23 (A) Qualitative research involves descriptive, narrative, and interpretive methods such as interviews, participant observation, and textual analysis.
Q.24 (B) An artifact is an object made, modified, or used by human beings, particularly an item of cultural or historical interest.
Q.25 (A) Solving global crises requires understanding human behavior, economic policies, equitable resource distribution, and democratic policymaking.
