Q1. The Moment Magnitude scale ($M_w$) quantifies an earthquake's energy release using seismic moment ($M_0 = \mu A D$). What do $\mu$, $A$, and $D$ represent?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Seismic moment $M_0$ equals rock shear rigidity ($\mu$) multiplied by fault rupture surface area ($A$) and average slip ($D$).
Q2. An earthquake of magnitude $M_w = 7.0$ releases approximately how many times more seismic energy than an earthquake of magnitude $M_w = 5.0$?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Each unit increase on the moment magnitude scale corresponds to a $pprox 31.62$-fold ($10^{1.5}$) jump in energy; two units increase energy by $10^3 = 1,000$ times.
Q3. Why is there an S-wave shadow zone on seismographs extending from 105° to 180° around the Earth from an earthquake epicenter?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: S-waves require shear strength and cannot propagate through the liquid outer core, casting an S-wave shadow beyond 105°.
Q4. In the Mohr-Coulomb equation for mass wasting shear strength ($\tau = c + \sigma_n \tan \phi$), how does water saturation trigger catastrophic landslides?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Elevated pore-water pressure ($u$) offsets normal overburden stress, reducing frictional shear strength and triggering slope failure.
Q5. On a Hjulström curve diagram, what does the critical relationship between water velocity and sediment particle size reveal?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Medium sand entrains easiest; cohesive electrostatic bonds between fine clay particles require surprisingly high velocities to erode.
Q6. In fluvial geomorphology, a river channel is classified as 'meandering' when its Sinuosity Index (Channel Length / Valley Length) is:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: A stream channel is formally defined as meandering when its sinuosity index (actual channel path length / valley axis length) exceeds 1.5.
Q7. What hydrodynamic mechanism drives the lateral migration of a meandering river loop across its floodplain?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Helicoidal secondary flow accelerates velocity at the outer concave cut-bank (erosion) and decelerates flow on the inner convex point bar (deposition).
Q8. What is the Gutenberg Discontinuity in global geophysics?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Gutenberg discontinuity at ~2,900 km depth marks the mantle-core boundary where P-waves decelerate sharply and S-waves terminate.
Q9. In glaciated terrain, what is the 'Bergschrund'?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Bergschrund is the gaping crevasse at the head of a cirque glacier separating moving ice from bedrock-anchored ice.
Q10. Why does chemical dissolution of limestone bedrock in karst terrain proceed faster in cold groundwater than in hot water?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Gases dissolve better in cold liquids (Henry's law); cold groundwater holds more dissolved $CO_2$, forming stronger carbonic acid.
Q11. What sharp, knife-edge, serrated rock ridge is formed between two adjacent glaciated cirques eroding toward each other?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: An arête is a razor-sharp bedrock ridge separating two adjoining glacial cirques or valleys.
Q12. A steep, pointed, pyramid-shaped mountain peak formed when three or more glacial cirques erode headward toward a central summit is a:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Glacial horns (pyramidal peaks) form when three or more cirques carve headward into a common mountain crest.
Q13. What streamlined, asymmetrical, tear-drop-shaped hill of glacial till has a steep blunt upstream side and a gentle tapered downstream tail?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Drumlins are streamlined, elongated subglacial hills of till shaped by overriding ice, indicating direction of glacier movement.
Q14. Sinuous, winding ridges of stratified sand and gravel deposited by subglacial meltwater streams running inside ice tunnels are:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Eskers are long, meandering ridges of glacio-fluvial gravel and sand deposited in subglacial meltwater tunnels.
Q15. Streamlined, wind-carved, knife-edged ridges of rock aligned parallel to prevailing desert winds, separated by scoured furrows, are called:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Yardangs are aerodynamic, keel-shaped rock ridges sculpted by wind deflation and abrasion parallel to prevailing winds.
Q16. What coastal landform consists of a narrow ridge of sand or shingle connected to the mainland at one end and terminating in open water at the other?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: A spit is an extended depositional coastal bar formed by longshore drift, projecting out from the coast into an estuary or bay.
Q17. A coastal sand spit that grows completely across a bay mouth, sealing it off from the open ocean and enclosing a shallow lagoon, is a:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: A baymouth bar completely bridges an embayment, enclosing an isolated brackish coastal lagoon.
Q18. What coastal depositional landform connects an offshore rocky island directly to the mainland with a narrow sand spit?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: A tombolo is a sand bar or spit linking an offshore island to the mainland through wave diffraction deposition.
Q19. What volcanic landform is produced by fluid, low-viscosity, basaltic lava flows that spread over vast distances, creating gentle broad domes?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Shield volcanoes are broad, low-profile volcanic mountains formed by successive layers of highly fluid basaltic lava.
Q20. Catastrophic collapse of a volcano's summit magma chamber following an explosive eruption creates a gigantic depression called a:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: A caldera is a massive volcanic depression (often miles across) formed by the structural collapse of an emptied magma reservoir.
Q21. What is the Mohorovičić (Moho) Discontinuity?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Moho marks the crust-mantle boundary where seismic P-waves accelerate from ~6 km/s to ~8 km/s.
Q22. In the chemical weathering of granite, which rock-forming mineral undergoes hydrolysis to transform into soft kaolinite clay?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Feldspar reacts with acidic water through hydrolysis ($2KAlSi_3O_8 + 2H_2CO_3 + 9H_2O \to Al_2Si_2O_5(OH)_4 + 4H_4SiO_4 + 2K^+$), yielding clay.
Q23. The expansion and peeling of curved concentric rock shells from granitic plutons due to tectonic pressure release upon erosion is called:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Exfoliation occurs when unburdening of deep plutonic rock causes outward expansion along curved pressure-release fractures.
Q24. A semi-circular, bowl-shaped depression at the head of a glacial valley that contains an alpine glacial lake after the ice melts is called a:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: A tarn is a small mountain lake nestled within an amphitheater-like cirque evacuated by a melted glacier.
Q25. Which plate boundary interaction is responsible for the formation of the volcanic Cascade Range and the Andes Mountains?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Oceanic-continental convergence forces the dense oceanic plate to subduct, generating magma that builds volcanic cordilleras.
