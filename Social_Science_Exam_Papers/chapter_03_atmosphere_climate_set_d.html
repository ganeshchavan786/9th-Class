SECTION A: CORE THEORETICAL PRINCIPLES (Q.1 TO Q.10) [10 MARKS]
Q.1

Assertion (A): Almost all convective weather phenomena such as clouds, rain, and storms are restricted to the troposphere.

Reason (R): The troposphere contains approximately 75% of the total atmospheric mass and virtually all of its atmospheric water vapour.

Q.2

Assertion (A): Commercial jet airliners frequently prefer cruising in the lower stratosphere.

Reason (R): The lower stratosphere is relatively free from vertical convective turbulence and severe tropospheric storm disturbances.

Q.3

Assertion (A): Temperature decreases systematically with increasing altitude throughout every layer of the Earth's atmosphere.

Reason (R): In the stratosphere, temperature increases with altitude due to the absorption of solar ultraviolet radiation by ozone molecules.

Q.4

Assertion (A): The normal environmental lapse rate in the troposphere averages a decrease of approximately 6.5°C per 1,000 metres of elevation.

Reason (R): The atmosphere is heated from below primarily by longwave terrestrial radiation emitted by the Earth's surface rather than direct solar insolation.

Q.5

Assertion (A): The Coriolis force deflects moving air masses to their right in the Northern Hemisphere and to their left in the Southern Hemisphere.

Reason (R): The Coriolis effect is an apparent inertial deflection generated by the Earth's rotation on its axis from west to east.

Q.6

Assertion (A): The Coriolis force attains its maximum magnitude at the equator and drops to zero at the geographic poles.

Reason (R): The magnitude of the Coriolis force is directly proportional to the sine of the latitude ($2\Omega v \sin \phi$).

Q.7

Assertion (A): Equatorial regions experience a permanent low-pressure trough known as the Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ).

Reason (R): Intense solar insolation year-round heats the equatorial surface, causing air to expand, become buoyant, and ascend convectively.

Q.8

Assertion (A): Subtropical high-pressure belts located around 30°N and 30°S are thermally induced by extreme cold surface temperatures.

Reason (R): Subtropical high-pressure ridges are dynamically induced by the descending limb of the Hadley cell circulation where upper-tropospheric air subsides.

Q.9

Assertion (A): Naturally occurring greenhouse gases are vital for sustaining complex life on planet Earth.

Reason (R): Without the natural greenhouse effect, Earth's mean surface temperature would drop to approximately -18°C, freezing global hydrospheric systems.

Q.10

Assertion (A): Nitrogen and oxygen gases are the primary greenhouse gases responsible for absorbing infrared heat in the lower atmosphere.

Reason (R): Diatomic homonuclear molecules like $N_2$ and $O_2$ cannot absorb infrared wavelengths because they lack changing dipole moments during vibration.

SECTION B: EVIDENTIARY PROOFS & MECHANISMS (Q.11 TO Q.18) [8 MARKS]
Q.11

Assertion (A): The Southwest Monsoon brings torrential summer rains to the Indian subcontinent.

Reason (R): Intense summer heating of the continental landmass creates a thermal low over Northwest India, drawing moisture-laden winds from the Indian Ocean.

Q.12

Assertion (A): The Tibetan Plateau acts as a thermal heat engine that reinforces the South Asian summer monsoon circulation.

Reason (R): Intense summer insolation on the elevated Tibetan plateau produces a strong convective thermal low, pumping air aloft and generating the Tropical Easterly Jet stream.

Q.13

Assertion (A): Tamil Nadu's Coromandel Coast receives the majority of its annual rainfall during the winter months (October to December).

Reason (R): The Northeast retreating monsoon winds absorb moisture while crossing the warm waters of the Bay of Bengal before hitting the Coromandel coast.

Q.14

Assertion (A): Western Rajasthan receives negligible rainfall from the Arabian Sea branch of the Southwest Monsoon.

Reason (R): The Aravalli Range lies parallel to the direction of the Arabian Sea monsoon winds, providing no orographic barrier to induce precipitation.

Q.15

Assertion (A): Tropical cyclones develop readily over cold polar ocean waters below 10°C.

Reason (R): Tropical cyclogenesis requires a sea surface temperature of at least 26.5°C to 27°C to sustain vigorous evaporation and latent heat release.

Q.16

Assertion (A): The eye of a mature tropical cyclone is a region of violent storm winds and torrential downpours.

Reason (R): The cyclone eye experiences subsiding dry air, light winds, and clear or partly cloudy skies with the lowest central surface pressure.

Q.17

Assertion (A): Mawsynram in Meghalaya receives the highest average annual rainfall in the world.

Reason (R): The funnel-shaped Khasi Hills trap moisture-bearing Bay of Bengal monsoon winds, forcing them to rise abruptly and undergo intense orographic condensation.

Q.18

Assertion (A): Pre-monsoon thunder squalls in West Bengal are locally referred to as 'Kalbaisakhi'.

Reason (R): Kalbaisakhi storms originate from the clash of hot, dry continental air with moist maritime Bay of Bengal air, producing severe squalls and hailstorms.

SECTION C: ADVANCED NUANCES & INSTITUTIONAL COMPLEXITIES (Q.19 TO Q.25) [7 MARKS]
Q.19

Assertion (A): An El Niño event in the equatorial Pacific Ocean is often correlated with weak or deficit monsoon rainfall in India.

Reason (R): El Niño warms the eastern Pacific, displacing the atmospheric Walker circulation and weakening low-pressure convective cells over the Indian subcontinent.

Q.20

Assertion (A): The ozone hole over Antarctica is primarily caused by elevated emissions of carbon dioxide ($CO_2$).

Reason (R): Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) release reactive chlorine radicals under polar stratospheric cloud chemistry that catalytically destroy ozone ($O_3$) molecules.

Q.21

Assertion (A): Melting of sea ice in the Arctic ocean triggers a positive feedback loop that accelerates global warming.

Reason (R): White reflective sea ice with high albedo is replaced by dark ocean water with low albedo, causing the ocean to absorb more solar radiation.

Q.22

Assertion (A): Places located near maritime coasts experience a much lower diurnal and annual range of temperature than inland continental locations.

Reason (R): Water has a significantly higher specific heat capacity than land, causing oceans to heat up and cool down much slower than continental land surfaces.

Q.23

Assertion (A): Global warming causes thermal expansion of ocean water, contributing to global sea-level rise.

Reason (R): Liquid water expands in volume as its temperature rises above 4°C, increasing the volumetric height of global oceanic basins.

Q.24

Assertion (A): The 'Loo' winds of North India in May and June are cool, humid maritime sea breezes.

Reason (R): The Loo consists of strong, extremely hot, and dry westerly winds blowing from the arid Thar desert across the northern plains.

Q.25

Assertion (A): A high carbon footprint indicates that an individual or activity contributes substantially to anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions.

Reason (R): The carbon footprint quantifies total direct and indirect emissions of greenhouse gases, expressed in equivalents of carbon dioxide ($CO_2e$).
