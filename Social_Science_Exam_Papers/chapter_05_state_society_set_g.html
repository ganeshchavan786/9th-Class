Q1. In Kautilya's Saptanga Theory of State expounded in the Arthashastra, which of the following represents the correct organic hierarchy of the seven structural limbs of a sovereign state?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Kautilya's Saptanga theory models the state as an organic body with seven essential elements: Swami (head/king), Amatya (eyes/ministers), Janapada (legs/territory), Durga (arms/fort), Kosha (mouth/treasury), Danda (brain/army), and Mitra (ears/allies).
Q2. The Uttaramerur Inscriptions of Chola King Parantaka I (dated 919 and 921 CE) provide unprecedented constitutional details regarding village assemblies. Through which specific democratic mechanism were committee members (Variyars) chosen?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Kudavolai system was an ancient Chola democratic lottery where palm-leaf ballots of qualified candidates were shuffled in an earthen pot (kuda) and drawn by a child before the assembly.
Q3. According to the Uttaramerur Inscription, which of the following conditions disqualified a citizen from standing for election to the village executive committees (Variyams)?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The inscriptions strictly disqualified anyone who had not submitted financial accounts of past committee service, anyone convicted of major sins (Patakas), or their close agnatic relatives.
Q4. In the administrative hierarchy of the Gupta Empire, administrative divisions were organised in descending order as:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Gupta administrative system divided the empire into Bhuktis (provinces governed by Uparikas), further subdivided into Vishayas (districts headed by Vishayapatis), Vithis, and Gramas.
Q5. Which distinctive feature characterized land grants (Agrahara and Brahmadeya) issued during the Gupta and Post-Gupta periods according to epigraphic copper plate records?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Agrahara grants transferred royal revenue collection rights, administrative immunity from royal officers (Apraveshyam), and judicial authority over the village to Brahmana scholars or temples in perpetuity.
Q6. In the Sangam Age of Tamilakam, who were the 'Muventar' referenced in Sangam anthologies?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Muventar ('the three crowned kings') refers to the Chera, Chola, and Pandya dynasties who held sway over the ancient Tamil country during the Sangam era.
Q7. What archaeological evidence firmly confirms extensive oceanic maritime trade between Southern India and the Roman Empire during the first two centuries CE?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Excavations at Arikamedu (Puducherry) and Pattanam (ancient Muziris) yielded Roman Mediterranean amphorae, rouletted ware, Roman glass, and vast hoards of Imperial Roman gold coins (denarii).
Q8. Which royal official was explicitly created by Mauryan Emperor Ashoka in his 5th Major Rock Edict to promote ethical conduct, inter-faith harmony, and social welfare across diverse communities?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: In his 5th Major Rock Edict, Ashoka created the special cadre of Dhamma Mahamattas (Ministers of Dhamma) to propagate moral precepts, dispense welfare, and supervise sectarian harmony.
Q9. In the Mauryan administrative machinery described in Megasthenes' Indica, the municipal administration of the capital Pataliputra was managed by:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Megasthenes recorded that Pataliputra was governed by a 30-member municipal commission divided into 6 boards of 5 members each: arts/crafts, foreigners, births/deaths, trade, manufacturing, and sales taxes.
Q10. What was the economic role of 'Shrenis' (merchant and craft guilds) in ancient Indian urban society up to 1000 CE?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Shrenis exercised corporate self-governance, laid down trade codes (Shreni-dharma recognized by kings), operated apprentice systems, issued seal-tokens, and accepted permanent cash deposits (Akshayanivi).
Q11. The Junagadh Rock Inscription of Western Kshatrapa ruler Rudradaman I (c. 150 CE) is historically extraordinary because:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Rudradaman's Junagadh inscription (the earliest long, chaste Sanskrit inscription) details how he repaired the breached embankment of Sudarshana Lake out of his own treasury without emergency levies (Vishti or Pranaya).
Q12. What does the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang) describe regarding the academic admission standards at Nalanda Mahavihara in the 7th century CE?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Xuanzang noted that Nalanda's gatekeeper tested candidates on advanced logic and philosophy at the outer gate, and only 2 or 3 out of 10 passed the rigorous preliminary viva to gain admittance.
Q13. The transition from the Early Historic to the Early Medieval period in Indian historiography (c. 300–1000 CE) has been interpreted by historians like R.S. Sharma as:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Indian Feudalism thesis argues that royal land grants created a hierarchy of landed intermediaries (Samantas), leading to political decentralization and serf-like agrarian dependence.
Q14. In the Chola revenue system, what were the primary classifications of taxes denoted by 'Kadamai' and 'Kudimai'?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: In Chola epigraphy, Kadamai was the basic land rent/assessment paid by landholders, while Kudimai represented customary obligations of public labour and service owed by agrarian tenants.
Q15. Which prominent Buddhist council was patronized by Kushana Emperor Kanishka I in Kashmir, leading to the formal codification of Mahayana Buddhism and compilation of the Mahavibhasha?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Fourth Buddhist Council was convened under Kanishka at Kundalavana (Kashmir), presided over by Vasumitra and Ashvaghosha, consolidating Mahayana doctrine and Sanskrit canons.
Q16. What distinguishes the Gandhara school of art from the Mathura school of art in ancient sculpture during the Kushana era?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Gandhara art was Greco-Buddhist (Hellenistic drapery and features in blue-grey schist/stucco), while the Mathura school was deeply indigenous, spiritual, and sculpted from spotted red sandstone.
Q17. In the administration of ancient South Indian villages, what was the structural difference between an 'Ur' and a 'Sabha' (or Mahasabha)?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: In Chola rural governance, the Ur was the assembly of ordinary agrarian peasants (Vellanvagai villages), whereas the Sabha/Mahasabha was the specialized gathering in Brahmana tax-free gift villages (Brahmadeya).
Q18. Which major rock edict of Emperor Ashoka contains his poignant expression of remorse and profound transformation from 'Digvijaya' (military conquest) to 'Dhammavijaya' (conquest through righteousness) following the Kalinga War?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Major Rock Edict XIII describes the tragic devastation of the Kalinga War (100,000 killed, 150,000 carried away captive) and proclaims Ashoka's renunciation of warfare in favour of Dhammavijaya.
Q19. What was the significance of the Aihole Inscription of 634 CE composed by the court poet Ravikirti?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Ravikirti's poetic Prashasti at the Meguti temple in Aihole praises Pulakeshin II of Badami and records his military triumph halting northern emperor Harsha's expansion at the Narmada.
Q20. The concept of 'Samanta', which originally meant an adjoining neighbour or boundary ruler, evolved in Indian polity by the 6th century CE into:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The term Samanta shifted semantically from 'neighbouring king' to 'subordinate feudatory/tributary vassal' within the emerging medieval hierarchy of political subordination.
Q21. What was the primary function of the 'Nagaram' in the urban political economy of ancient South India?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Nagaram was the organized corporate body and commercial assembly of traders and merchants (like the Manigramam and Ayyavole 500) governing market towns.
Q22. The Arthashastra prescribes that an ideal king must follow which principle regarding royal duty and public governance?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Kautilya asserts: 'In the happiness of the subjects lies the king's happiness; in their welfare his welfare. The king shall not consider as good only that which pleases him, but what pleases the subjects.'
Q23. In ancient Indian numismatics, what is the defining characteristic of 'Punch-Marked Coins' (Ahad-mudra) from the Mahajanapada and Mauryan eras?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Punch-marked coins (Karshapanas/Puranas) were pieces of silver/copper alloy cut to a standard weight and impressed with multiple independent symbolic punches (sun, six-armed symbol, arched hill), lacking legends.
Q24. Which famous traveler from Tang Dynasty China visited Harsha's empire and described the grand quinquennial assembly of religious tolerance and state philanthropy held at Prayaga?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Xuanzang witnessed Harsha's grand assembly at the confluence of the Ganga and Yamuna at Prayaga (Maha Moksha Parishad), where Harsha gave away accumulated imperial treasures to scholars, ascetics, and the needy.
Q25. In the Pallava and Chola architectural traditions, what structural milestone marked the transition from rock-cut architecture to structural stone temples?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Under Pallava king Rajasimha (Narasimhavarman II), Dravidian temple architecture shifted from rock-cut cave rathas to free-standing structural masonry stone temples like the Shore Temple and Kanchi Kailasanatha.
