Q.1 Why did the middle Gangetic plain witness the emergence of powerful centralized territorial kingdoms (Mahajanapadas) by the 6th century BCE?
Q.2 How did the political structure of the Vajji 'Gana-Sangha' (republic) differ fundamentally from the monarchy of Magadha?
Q.3 In Kautilya's *Arthashastra*, what is the 'Saptanga' theory of the state?
Q.4 Why did Emperor Ashoka promote 'Dhamma' as an overarching state ideology rather than imposing sectarian Buddhist theological doctrines on his subjects?
Q.5 In Major Rock Edict XII, what fundamental principle of religious governance does Emperor Ashoka articulate?
Q.6 How did the geographical location of Magadha's two capitals, Rajagriha and Pataliputra, provide exceptional strategic military advantages?
Q.7 What significant diplomatic and cultural consequence followed the peace treaty between Chandragupta Maurya and the Hellenistic Seleucid emperor Seleucus I Nicator around 303 BCE?
Q.8 Why did the Gupta Empire issue large volumes of high-purity gold coins (Dinaras) while copper coins from the Gupta period are relatively scarce in the archaeological record?
Q.9 In the Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayag Prashasti), how does court poet Harishena describe Samudragupta's contrasting imperial policies toward the kings of Aryavarta versus the kings of Dakshinapatha (South India)?
Q.10 What transformative scientific breakthrough did the astronomer-mathematician Aryabhata propose in his 499 CE treatise *Aryabhatiya*?
Q.11 How did the practice of royal land grants (*Agrahara* and *Brahmadeya*) to temples and Brahmins under the Guptas and subsequent dynasties transform early medieval agrarian society?
Q.12 What unique democratic mechanism was recorded in the 10th-century CE Uttaramerur inscription of Chola King Parantaka I for selecting village committee members?
Q.13 Under the Chola local administrative system, what was the structural difference between the 'Ur' and the 'Sabha' (or Mahasabha)?
Q.14 Why is the monolithic Kailash Temple (Cave 16) at Ellora considered one of the most astonishing architectural achievements in world history?
Q.15 What does the flourishing of ancient South Indian maritime trade guilds like the *Manigramam* and *Ayyavole 500* (Ainnurruvar) demonstrate about the Indian Ocean economy up to 1000 CE?
Q.16 How does Megasthenes's division of Indian society into seven classes in his *Indica* reflect the lens of a foreign observer interpreting Indian social realities?
Q.17 What military and economic impact did the Chola maritime naval expeditions to Sri Vijaya (modern Sumatra and the Malayan peninsula) under Rajendra Chola I around 1025 CE have?
Q.18 In ancient Indian historical epigraphy, what is the significance of the Junagadh rock inscription of Saka ruler Rudradaman I (c. 150 CE)?
Q.19 Why was the Nalanda Mahavihara considered an intellectual epicenter of the ancient world that attracted thousands of scholars like Hiuen Tsang and I-Tsing?
Q.20 How does the play *Mudrarakshasa* by Vishakhadatta dramatize the political founding of the Mauryan Empire?
Q.21 Which South Indian architectural style is characterized by colossal pyramidal gate towers called 'Gopurams', pillared mandapas, and water tanks within massive temple enclosures?
Q.22 What socio-economic transformation distinguished the Later Vedic period (c. 1000–600 BCE) from the Early Rigvedic pastoral society?
Q.23 How did the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang) describe law and order, administration, and public welfare during King Harshavardhana's reign in the 7th century CE?
Q.24 What was the administrative and economic role of ancient Indian merchant guilds (*Shrenis*) in urban commercial life?
Q.25 Why does the Iron Pillar at Mehrauli, Delhi, remain a metallurgical wonder of the ancient world after 1,600 years?

OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET B)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & Analytical Rationale
Q.1 (A) Iron metallurgy (clearing hardwood sal forests, deep tilling with iron ploughs) generated agricultural surpluses that sustained standing armies, urban craft centers, and state revenue.
Q.2 (A) Gana-Sanghas like Vajji and the Shakyas were aristocratic republics where power resided in a corporate assembly of Kshatriya clan elders, distinct from Magadhan autocratic kingship.
Q.3 (A) Kautilya's Saptanga theory views the state as an organic body with seven interdependent organs, where the king is the head, ministers are eyes, and the treasury is the mouth.
Q.4 (A) Ashoka's Dhamma transcended sectarian dogmas, functioning as a civic-moral glue to unite a vast multi-cultural empire and resolve social tensions between Brahmins, Shramanas, and diverse sects.
Q.5 (A) Rock Edict XII is a foundational declaration of religious pluralism and mutual respect, warning that sectarian chauvinism harms the essence (*saravriddhi*) of all religious traditions.
Q.6 (A) Rajagriha's pentagonal hill barriers made it impregnable to sieges, while Pataliputra's riverine convergence enabled swift troop movements, naval logistics, and river toll collections.
Q.7 (A) The treaty established formal diplomatic parity, transferred modern eastern Afghanistan and Balochistan to the Mauryas in exchange for 500 war elephants, and initiated Greek envoys.
Q.8 (A) High-value gold dinaras financed state prestige, external trade, and merchant guilds, while the scarcity of base-metal copper coins indicates localized demonetization and cowrie shell usage.
Q.9 (A) Recognizing logistical limits in controlling distant southern kingdoms from Pataliputra, Samudragupta annexed nearby Gangetic kings directly but restored southern rulers as tributary feudatories.
Q.10 (A) Aryabhata pioneered rational heliometric mathematics: earth's diurnal axial rotation, calculating $\pi \approx 3.1416$, and explaining eclipses as geometric shadows (*Chhaya*).
Q.11 (A) Land grants alienated fiscal and judicial rights to donees, creating localized landlord intermediaries, agrarian expansion into tribal hinterlands, and an early medieval feudal hierarchy.
Q.12 (A) Chola local self-governance utilized the *Kudavolai* lottery: qualified elders (possessing land, Vedic education, clean accounts) were chosen via randomized palm-leaf drawings from pots.
Q.13 (A) The *Ur* functioned in peasant agricultural villages (*Vellanvagai*), while the *Sabha* governed elite *Brahmadeya* villages through specialized sub-committees (*Variyams*).
Q.14 (A) Commissioned by Rashtrakuta monarch Krishna I (c. 756–774 CE), the Kailash temple is a monolithic marvel carved vertically downward from the living cliff with intricate multi-tiered Dravidian vimana.
Q.15 (A) Chola-era guilds (*Ayyavole*, *Manigramam*) financed trans-oceanic shipping, maintained private security guards, and operated permanent trading settlements in Sumatra, Java, and southern China.
Q.16 (A) Megasthenes observed real occupational functional groups in imperial Pataliputra, mapping them onto a 7-class schema familiar to Hellenistic Greeks rather than the scriptural four Varnas.
Q.17 (A) Rajendra Chola's naval campaign secured crucial maritime choke points (Strait of Malacca, Sunda Strait) to defend Chola mercantile profits in the Sino-Indian maritime trade network.
Q.18 (A) Rudradaman's Junagadh inscription is a milestone: the earliest lengthy public Sanskrit epigraph, recording centuries of public hydraulic engineering at Sudarshana Lake without taxing subjects.
Q.19 (A) Nalanda's gatekeeper (*Dwarapandita*) conducted fierce entrance examinations; admitted students debated advanced epistemology (*Nyaya*), Mahayana metaphysics, and secular sciences.
Q.20 (A) Vishakhadatta's political drama depicts Chanakya's ruthless realpolitik diplomacy, espionage, and reconciliation with the loyal minister Rakshasa to stabilize Chandragupta's state.
Q.21 (A) The Dravidian architectural idiom, perfected under the Pallavas, Cholas, and Pandyas, features tall storied Vimanas, pillared halls (*Mandapas*), and towering gateway *Gopurams*.
Q.22 (A) Later Vedic society shifted from pastoral semi-nomadism to settled farming, rigidified the Varna-Jati order, expanded sacrificial Brahmanical hegemony, and crystallized territorial states.
Q.23 (A) Xuanzang noted Harsha's energetic travels, patronization of the Kanauj assembly, and charitable *Punya-shalas*, but reported being robbed multiple times, indicating localized feudal decay.
Q.24 (A) Shrenis operated with judicial autonomy under guild chiefs (*Jetthakas*), regulated production, minted tokens, issued commercial loans, and acted as public charitable trustees.
Q.25 (A) Advanced Gupta metallurgy forged pure wrought iron with high phosphorus slag inclusions, creating an adherent passive protective film that withstands weathering without rusting.
