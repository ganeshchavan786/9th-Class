OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & RAPID RECALL EXPLANATIONS (SET F)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Concept & Rapid Recall Analysis
Q.1 (B) The Arthashastra is Kautilya's classic Sanskrit treatise on political economy, statecraft, and military strategy.
Q.2 (C) The Saptanga theory articulates the seven limbs of the state: King, Ministers, Territory, Fort, Treasury, Army, and Ally.
Q.3 (B) The Samaharta was the superintendent responsible for assessing and collecting all state revenues and taxes.
Q.4 (A) The Sannidhata was the royal treasurer in charge of imperial storehouses, granaries, and precious reserves.
Q.5 (C) Emperor Ashoka experienced moral remorse after Kalinga, adopting the conquest of righteousness (Dhammaghosha).
Q.6 (B) Major Rock Edict XIII preserves Ashoka's poignant account of remorse following the bloodshed in Kalinga.
Q.7 (B) Dhamma Mahamattas were appointed by Ashoka to supervise public welfare, judicial fairness, and religious harmony.
Q.8 (B) Rajagriha (modern Rajgir), surrounded by five natural hills, was Magadha's first fortified capital.
Q.9 (B) Vaishali was the capital of the Vajji confederacy, home to the Lichchhavi clan's republican assembly.
Q.10 (B) Harishena, court poet and minister of Samudragupta, composed the Sanskrit eulogy on the Allahabad pillar.
Q.11 (C) Chandragupta II adopted the title Vikramaditya after defeating the Western Kshatrapas; Fa-Hien visited during his reign.
Q.12 (B) In the Aryabhatiya, Aryabhata established that the Earth is a rotating sphere and accurately calculated pi (3.1416).
Q.13 (B) Chalukya king Pulakeshin II defeated Harsha at the Narmada River, as recorded in the Aihole inscription.
Q.14 (B) Jain poet Ravikirti composed the Sanskrit Aihole inscription in 634 CE.
Q.15 (B) Banabhatta was Harsha's court poet who wrote the famous biography Harshacharita and Kadambari.
Q.16 (B) Rajaraja Chola I commissioned the monumental granite Brihadisvara Temple dedicated to Lord Shiva.
Q.17 (B) Rajendra Chola I built a formidable navy that crossed the Bay of Bengal, raiding Srivijaya (Sumatra/Malaya).
Q.18 (B) The Uttaramerur inscriptions outline the Kudavolai lottery system for electing village administrative committees.
Q.19 (B) The Sabha was the autonomous assembly of scholarly Brahmin landholders in agrahara villages.
Q.20 (A) Shrenis were powerful trade and craft guilds that regulated manufacturing, quality, credit, and apprenticeship.
Q.21 (B) Gold coins in the Gupta and Kushana eras were called dinaras (modeled on the Roman denarius).
Q.22 (B) Nalanda was a world-renowned residential monastic university patronized by the Guptas and Harsha.
Q.23 (B) The Mehrauli iron pillar commemorates King Chandra, widely recognized as Chandragupta II Vikramaditya.
Q.24 (B) Kalidasa was the preeminent poet and playwright of the Gupta classical age.
Q.25 (B) Brahmadeyas were tax-exempt royal land grants given to Brahmins, initiating early medieval decentralization.
