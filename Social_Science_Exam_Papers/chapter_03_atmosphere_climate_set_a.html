OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET A)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & Conceptual Rationale
Q.1 (B) Nitrogen makes up roughly 78.08% of Earth's atmosphere by volume, followed by oxygen at 20.95%.
Q.2 (A) Oxygen accounts for roughly 21% of dry atmospheric air and is vital for animal respiration and combustion.
Q.3 (A) Carbon dioxide makes up approximately 0.04% (around 420 ppm) of the Earth's atmosphere, functioning as a vital greenhouse gas.
Q.4 (A) The troposphere extends up to about 8 km at the poles and 18 km at the equator; it contains 75% of atmospheric mass and all weather phenomena.
Q.5 (A) The normal lapse rate in the troposphere is approximately 6.5 °C drop in temperature for every 1,000 meters (1 km) increase in elevation.
Q.6 (A) The stratosphere (extending from roughly 12 to 50 km) houses the ozone layer, which shields Earth from damaging UV-B and UV-C solar radiation.
Q.7 (A) The stratosphere has stable, stratified air without the turbulent vertical convection currents and storm clouds of the troposphere.
Q.8 (A) The mesosphere (50 to 80 km) is the coldest atmospheric layer; meteors entering from space burn up here due to atmospheric friction.
Q.9 (A) The ionosphere contains ionized particles that reflect shortwave radio transmissions back to Earth, enabling wireless communication.
Q.10 (A) A barometer (mercury or aneroid) measures the force exerted per unit area by the weight of the air above.
Q.11 (A) Ferrel's law states that due to the Coriolis force arising from Earth's rotation, winds deflect to their right in the Northern Hemisphere and left in the Southern Hemisphere.
Q.12 (A) The ITCZ is a broad equatorial trough of low pressure where converging trade winds ascend, creating calm conditions historically known as the doldrums.
Q.13 (A) Jet streams are fast-flowing narrow air currents in the upper troposphere (speeds from 110 km/h in summer to 184 km/h in winter) steering weather systems.
Q.14 (A) 'Monsoon' originates from the Arabic word 'Mausim', referring to the seasonal reversal in the direction of winds over a year.
Q.15 (A) Intense summer heating of the Indian subcontinent creates a deep low-pressure system over northern plains, drawing moisture-laden winds from the high-pressure southern Indian Ocean.
Q.16 (A) 'Loo' refers to the scorching, dusty, dry summer winds blowing during May and June across the northern plains of India.
Q.17 (A) Pre-monsoon thunderstorms in Kerala and Karnataka are known as 'Mango Showers' because they aid the early ripening of mango crops.
Q.18 (A) El Niño is a warm ocean current replacing the cold Peruvian current periodically, associated with the Southern Oscillation (ENSO), often suppressing Indian monsoon rains.
Q.19 (A) Western disturbances originate over the Mediterranean and bring critical winter rain to Punjab/Haryana, highly beneficial for Rabi crops (wheat).
Q.20 (A) Mawsynram in the Khasi Hills of Meghalaya receives the world's highest average annual rainfall (over 11,800 mm) due to relief orographic funnelling.
Q.21 (A) During the Southwest monsoon, Tamil Nadu lies parallel to the Bay of Bengal branch and in the rain shadow of the Western Ghats; it gets rainfall when winds retreat across the Bay of Bengal in winter.
Q.22 (A) The greenhouse effect occurs when atmospheric gases like $CO_2$, methane, and water vapour trap outgoing long-wave terrestrial infrared radiation.
Q.23 (A) Methane is a powerful greenhouse gas with a high global warming potential, produced by anaerobic decomposition in wetlands, rice paddies, and livestock digestion.
Q.24 (A) A carbon footprint measures the total greenhouse gas emissions caused directly or indirectly by an individual, product, or institution.
Q.25 (A) The Montreal Protocol (1987) is a landmark environmental treaty that successfully phased out ozone-depleting substances, notably CFCs.
