OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET B)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & Analytical Rationale
Q.1 (A) The troposphere is warmed by longwave infrared radiation reradiated from Earth's surface; in the stratosphere, ozone photolysis ($O_3 + h\nu$) releases heat, creating a temperature inversion.
Q.2 (A) According to Ferrel's Law, Earth's eastward rotation deflects northern hemisphere winds to their right; winds flowing south towards the equator become Northeast Trade Winds.
Q.3 (A) The rapid withdrawal of the westerly jet stream from northern India and the simultaneous onset of the tropical easterly jet stream trigger the dynamic 'burst' of the monsoon.
Q.4 (A) Warm eastern Pacific water suppresses the normal western Pacific convective cell; this disrupts the Walker Circulation and descending air anomalies weaken the Indian monsoon trough.
Q.5 (A) Orographic rainfall leaves leeward slopes dry: descending air warms by compression at the dry adiabatic lapse rate, raising its saturation deficit and suppressing rain (rain shadow).
Q.6 (A) Temperate shallow cyclonic depressions steered by the westerly jet stream bring vital 20–50 mm of winter rain to Punjab, Haryana, and UP, ensuring high Rabi wheat yields.
Q.7 (A) The unique topography of the Meghalaya plateau forms a horseshoe funnel open to the south, forcing moisture-saturated Bay of Bengal winds to ascend abruptly and dump torrential rains.
Q.8 (A) Solar declination shifts the equatorial thermal trough 25°N in July (Monsoon Trough); cross-equatorial trade winds deflected right by Coriolis become the Southwest Monsoon.
Q.9 (A) Shortwave insolation passes through atmospheric gases to warm the crust; the crust reradiates longwave infrared, which is absorbed by asymmetric dipolar molecules ($CO_2, CH_4$).
Q.10 (A) Ozone photochemically absorbs UV-B (280–315 nm). Excess UV-B induces pyrimidine dimers in cellular DNA, promoting mutations, skin carcinomas, and marine ecosystem collapses.
Q.11 (A) Water's high specific heat capacity buffers coastal areas (maritime moderation); interior continental locations like Delhi lack maritime moderation and exhibit extreme temperature swings.
Q.12 (A) Radiative winter cooling leaves cold, dense air near the ground beneath warm air; this thermal inversion suppresses vertical atmospheric mixing, trapping vehicular emissions as toxic smog.
Q.13 (A) As the monsoon trough retreats, skies clear, raising insolation; high soil moisture and rising temperatures generate stifling atmospheric humidity known as 'October Heat'.
Q.14 (A) The Tibetan Plateau functions as an elevated heat source, generating strong sensible heat fluxes in summer that create upper-tropospheric anticyclogenesis and power the monsoon engine.
Q.15 (A) Post-monsoon tropical depressions intensify over warm waters; their ferocious cyclonic winds and shallow coastal bathymetry produce storm surges that inundate flat deltaic plains.
Q.16 (A) Weather represents short-term atmospheric fluctuations (hours/days); climate represents long-term statistical meteorological distributions over standard 30-year epochs.
Q.17 (A) The Aravalli hills align parallel to the southwest monsoon winds without obstructing them, and intense desert heat lowers relative humidity, preventing condensation.
Q.18 (A) Combustion of fossil reserves oxidized millions of years of stored carbon, raising atmospheric $CO_2$ from pre-industrial ~280 ppm to over 420 ppm today.
Q.19 (A) Ice-albedo feedback is a self-reinforcing loop: high-albedo sea ice (~0.6–0.9) reflects sunlight; open water (albedo ~0.06) absorbs 94% of insolation, heating the ocean.
Q.20 (A) In the thermosphere, individual gas atoms absorb high-energy solar X-rays (high kinetic temperature), but molecular density is so low that heat cannot transfer effectively.
Q.21 (A) Subtropical high pressure drives descending dry air; easterly trade winds shed moisture on eastern margins and blow dry offshore in the west, reinforced by cold ocean upwelling.
Q.22 (A) The Southern Oscillation Index (SOI) measures the pressure gradient between Tahiti and Darwin; negative values signify El Niño and disrupted rainfall across the Indo-Pacific.
Q.23 (A) Biological carbon sequestration captures atmospheric $CO_2$ and locks it into woody biomass and stable humus, reducing net atmospheric radiative forcing.
Q.24 (A) While the fractional composition remains 21% $O_2$, barometric pressure drops from 1013 hPa at sea level to ~330 hPa at 8,848 m, reducing oxygen partial pressure by two-thirds.
Q.25 (A) The 2015 Paris Agreement sets a legally binding international target to limit global mean surface warming to well below 2 °C, ideally 1.5 °C, via Nationally Determined Contributions.
