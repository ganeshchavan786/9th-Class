OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET C)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & In-Depth Conceptual Solution
Q.1 (A) Resources are specialized; shifting fertile land from wheat to sugarcane requires reallocating increasingly unsuitable resources, causing the marginal opportunity cost to rise.
Q.2 (A) Marginal product ($MP_L = \Delta TP / \Delta L$) measures the incremental addition to total physical output resulting from adding one additional worker.
Q.3 (A) When variable inputs (labor) are combined with a fixed factor (land), the fixed factor becomes overutilized, causing marginal output to diminish and eventually turn negative.
Q.4 (A) $\text{GNP} = \text{GDP} + \text{Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)}$. GDP is territorial; GNP accounts for ownership and factor earnings of national citizens worldwide.
Q.5 (A) GER measures demographic participation across schooling tiers; favorable PTR ensures personalized pedagogical interaction, essential for foundational cognitive skill development.
Q.6 (A) Sunk costs are irrecoverable past outlays; rational decision-makers look forward ($MC$ vs $MB$), avoiding the 'sunk cost fallacy' of throwing good money after bad.
Q.7 (A) Consumer sovereignty asserts that consumer preferences dictate resource allocation: producers who fail to satisfy consumer demand face bankruptcy.
Q.8 (A) Capital accumulation requires deferred consumption (savings): foregoing consumer gratification today finances capital goods that expand the future PPF frontier.
Q.9 (A) The informal sector employs over 85% of India's workforce in precarious livelihoods without written contracts, paid leave, workplace safety, or institutional pension safety nets.
Q.10 (A) Financial intermediation bridges savers and investors: banks convert dispersed household deposits into productive capital loans, driving capital formation.
Q.11 (A) $MRTS_{LK} = -\Delta K / \Delta L = MP_L / MP_K$. It represents the slope of an isoquant, showing how much capital can be replaced by one unit of labor at constant output.
Q.12 (A) Neurodevelopmental biology confirms that 80% of brain architecture forms before age 3; stunting permanently reduces adult cognitive ability, depressing individual earnings by ~10–20%.
Q.13 (A) Ernst Engel demonstrated that as household income rises, the proportion spent on food declines (Engel's Law), shifting expenditure toward manufactured goods and services.
Q.14 (A) Nurkse's circle: low real income $\rightarrow$ low savings capacity $\rightarrow$ capital deficiency $\rightarrow$ low labor productivity $\rightarrow$ low real income. Breaking it requires state capital 'big push'.
Q.15 (A) Capital widening keeps capital-per-worker constant as the workforce grows; capital deepening increases capital endowment per worker ($K/L$), driving labor productivity growth.
Q.16 (A) The Green Revolution was a technical package: HYV Mexican wheat seeds (Norman Borlaug) were responsive to heavy inputs, requiring chemical fertilizers and controlled irrigation.
Q.17 (A) A monopsonist (sole buyer of labor) faces an upward-sloping labor supply curve and exploits market power to set wages below the marginal revenue product of labor ($W < MRP_L$).
Q.18 (A) The UNDP's HDI combines three essential capabilities: health (life expectancy), knowledge (education indices), and standard of living (log GNI per capita).
Q.19 (A) Jobless growth occurs when productivity gains stem from automation, ICT, or capital intensity, resulting in GDP growth with minimal elasticity of employment.
Q.20 (A) Social overhead capital provides non-excludable infrastructural foundations that generate widespread positive production externalities across all economic sectors.
Q.21 (A) Usurious informal interest outpaces the marginal return on enterprise, siphoning economic surplus to moneylenders and precipitating debt traps.
Q.22 (A) Renewable assets possess sustained replenishment rates if harvested below sustainable yield; fossil stocks are finite and depleted permanently upon extraction.
Q.23 (A) Principle 15 of the Rio Declaration mandates proactive environmental stewardship: potential catastrophic ecosystem collapse justifies regulatory intervention even amidst empirical uncertainty.
Q.24 (A) Cost of capital represents the hurdle rate: the foregone return on the next best alternative deployment of financial capital.
Q.25 (A) Scarcity necessitates institutional coordination: societies must establish legal, ethical, and market frameworks to determine who gets what, how resources are used, and how justice is preserved.
