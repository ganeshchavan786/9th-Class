OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & RAPID RECALL EXPLANATIONS (SET F)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Concept & Rapid Recall Analysis
Q.1 (B) The Palaeolithic era encompasses over 98% of human technological history, characterized by chipped stone tools.
Q.2 (B) Microliths are miniature stone blades characteristic of the Mesolithic transitional hunter-gatherer era.
Q.3 (B) The Neolithic revolution marked the invention of agriculture, animal domestication, pottery, and sedentary villages.
Q.4 (B) Bhimbetka in Raisen district, MP, features extraordinary prehistoric rock art from Upper Palaeolithic to Mesolithic times.
Q.5 (B) Mehrgarh on the Kachi plain documented early South Asian transitions from foraging to sedentary farming.
Q.6 (B) Harappa was an advanced urban Bronze Age civilization; iron technology was completely unknown to Harappans.
Q.7 (B) Harappan bricks exhibited remarkable standard proportions of 1:2:4 throughout disparate cities from Gujarat to Punjab.
Q.8 (B) The Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro is an engineered public tank lined with bitumen and gypsum for water retention.
Q.9 (B) Lothal on the Gulf of Khambhat possessed a tidal dockyard connected by an inlet channel to the Bhogavo river.
Q.10 (B) Dholavira featured an advanced water-harvesting reservoir circuit and a tripartite city layout (Citadel, Middle, Lower).
Q.11 (B) Kalibangan yielded a pre-Harappan ploughed field showing grid furrows for simultaneous mixed cropping (mustard and gram).
Q.12 (B) Chanhudaro was a specialized industrial craft township renowned for bead-making workshops and lack of fortifications.
Q.13 (B) The Dancing Girl was crafted using lost-wax casting: modeling in wax, encasing in clay, melting wax, and pouring molten bronze.
Q.14 (B) Harappan seals were carved from soft steatite, engraved with pictograms and animals, and baked to white hardness.
Q.15 (B) Mesopotamian records designated the Indus region as 'Meluhha', a major source of carnelian, ivory, and lapis lazuli.
Q.16 (B) Smaller Harappan chert cubical weights followed a binary progression (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64), where unit 16 weighed ~13.7g.
Q.17 (B) The Dholavira Signboard with ten large glyphs was discovered at the northern gateway of the city's Citadel.
Q.18 (A) The single-horned mythical bull/unicorn motif accounts for over 60% of all zoomorphic engravings on Harappan seals.
Q.19 (A) Shortughai in northeastern Afghanistan was a dedicated Harappan trading colony situated near lapis lazuli mines.
Q.20 (B) The Khetri region of Rajasthan was the major copper mining source for the Harappan Civilisation.
Q.21 (A) Harappans prepared fine gypsum and lime mortar to create watertight seals for public drainage systems.
Q.22 (B) Kalibangan in northern Rajasthan features clay fire altars and early ploughed field furrows.
Q.23 (B) Decipherment is blocked by brief inscriptional lengths and the total absence of bilingual texts.
Q.24 (A) Around 1900 BCE, mature Harappan cities entered the Post-Urban decline phase, marked by abandonment of large urban hubs.
Q.25 (B) The drying of the Ghaggar-Hakra river system starved central Harappan settlements of agricultural water.
