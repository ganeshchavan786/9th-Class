OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET B)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & Analytical Rationale
Q.1 (A) The Ring of Fire is ringed by oceanic subduction zones where descending tectonic plates produce intense frictional stick-slip earthquakes and explosive volcanism.
Q.2 (A) P-waves compress and dilate rock particles in their direction of travel (passing through solids, liquids, and gases); S-waves shear particles perpendicular and cannot penetrate fluids.
Q.3 (A) Alfred Wegener highlighted paleontological continuity: small freshwater reptiles like *Mesosaurus* could not swim across saline oceans, proving the continents were once contiguous.
Q.4 (A) High kinetic energy and steep hydraulic gradients drive vertical corrasion in youthful upper reaches, whereas reduced velocity in mature stages shifts work to lateral corrasion.
Q.5 (A) During flood surges, increased discharge and stream power breach the narrow neck (chute cutoff), establishing a straighter path and leaving the isolated loop as an oxbow lake.
Q.6 (A) Continent-continent collisions involve buoyant granitic crust that resists subduction into the asthenosphere, resulting in intense crustal thickening/folding rather than volcanic arc magmatism.
Q.7 (A) Deltas emerge when fluvial deposition outpaces marine erosion; estuaries feature powerful tidal flushing that removes river silt into deep waters without forming alluvial lobes.
Q.8 (A) The anomalous 9% volumetric expansion of water during crystallization exerts pressures exceeding 2,000 kg/cm$^2$, wedging joints apart over repeated diurnal cycles.
Q.9 (A) Saltating sand particles move up the gradual windward face (10°–15°) and avalanche down the sheltered slip-face at the critical angle of repose (~32°–34°).
Q.10 (A) Massive glacial ice erodes both valley floor and valley walls simultaneously across its entire contact width, transforming V-shaped canyons into broad, steep-walled U-shaped troughs.
Q.11 (A) Hydraulic wave refraction notches fractures into caves; opposing caves breach to form arches; roof collapse leaves isolated stacks; further basal abrasion reduces stacks to stumps.
Q.12 (A) Chemical kinetics dictate that reaction rates double for every 10 °C rise in temperature; high rainfall and dense biological decay produce carbonic/humic acids that accelerate weathering.
Q.13 (A) Glaciers deposit unsorted till ranging from massive boulders to fine flour without hydraulic sorting; flowing water deposits sediments graded by decreasing stream competency.
Q.14 (A) Megathrust fault rupture abruptly uplifts or down-drops the oceanic crust, transferring kinetic energy to the water column and propagating open-ocean waves at jet speeds (~800 km/h).
Q.15 (A) The sharp velocity drop at the mantle-core boundary refracts P-waves inward, creating an angular shadow zone between 105° and 145° from the earthquake focus.
Q.16 (A) Root wedging is a potent biophysical agent: growing root systems exert lateral pressures up to 15 bars, splitting bedrock along pre-existing joints.
Q.17 (A) Loess is wind-deposited (aeolian) dust transported in atmospheric suspension from arid deserts and peri-glacial plains and trapped by semi-arid vegetation.
Q.18 (A) Magma is subsurface molten material charged with dissolved gases; upon breaching the surface through vents or fissures, it degasses and is termed lava.
Q.19 (A) Faulting involves fracture and shear displacement of rock strata along a plane under tensional or compressional tectonic stresses.
Q.20 (A) Narmada and Tapi flow through hard fault-trough rift valleys with low silt loads, meeting strong Arabian Sea tidal currents that scour river mouths into deep estuaries.
Q.21 (A) Wind-driven saltation carries dense sand grains within 1 meter of the desert floor, focusing abrasive corrasion on the rock's lower stalk.
Q.22 (A) Carbonic acid in rainwater reacts with insoluble calcite ($CaCO_3$) to form soluble calcium bicarbonate ($Ca(HCO_3)_2$), excavating subterranean caves and sinkholes.
Q.23 (A) Compressional tectonic forces fold horizontal strata into convex-upward arches (anticlines) and concave-downward troughs (synclines).
Q.24 (A) Alluvial floodplains provide nutrient-rich silt, high water tables, navigable channels, and flat topography, sustaining dense agricultural civilisations.
Q.25 (A) The San Andreas Fault is a right-lateral transform fault where the Pacific Plate slides horizontally past the North American Plate without creating or destroying crust.
