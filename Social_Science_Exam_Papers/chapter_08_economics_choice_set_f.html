Q. No. Correct Curricular Concept & Rapid Recall Analysis
Q.1 (B) Scarcity arises because productive resources are finite while human wants are unlimited, necessitating economizing and choice.
Q.2 (D) The three central problems are What to produce, How to produce, and For whom to produce.
Q.3 (B) Opportunity cost represents the forfeited benefit of the next best alternative choice foregone.
Q.4 (B) The four factors of production are Land (natural gifts), Labour, Capital, and Enterprise/Organization.
Q.5 (B) Land earns rent, labour earns wages, physical capital earns interest, and enterprise earns profit.
Q.6 (D) Profit is the residual reward earned by an entrepreneur for organizing production and bearing uninsurable commercial risk.
Q.7 (B) Fixed capital consists of durable physical capital assets that can be utilized repeatedly over many years.
Q.8 (B) Working capital consists of inputs consumed and operating funds transformed within a single cycle.
Q.9 (C) Human capital refers to the productive abilities, health, education, and technical expertise embodied in people.
Q.10 (B) The Production Possibility Frontier (PPF) shows maximum alternative output combinations given technology and resources.
Q.11 (B) Scarcity dictates trade-offs: increasing output of good X requires diverting resources away from good Y.
Q.12 (B) Concavity reflects increasing marginal opportunity cost as specialized inputs are shifted into alternative sectors.
Q.13 (B) Points inside the curve indicate inefficient resource allocation or involuntary underemployment.
Q.14 (A) Points beyond the frontier exceed current productive capacity and can only be attained through future growth.
Q.15 (B) Technological improvements, capital investment, and workforce skilling expand capacity, shifting the PPC outward.
Q.16 (B) The Primary sector involves direct exploitation of natural endowments (agriculture, forestry, fishing, mining).
Q.17 (B) The Secondary sector transforms raw materials into manufactured finished goods.
Q.18 (C) The Tertiary sector consists of intangible commercial, social, and personal services.
Q.19 (B) Sunk costs are unrecoverable historical expenditures that rational decision-makers should ignore when evaluating future choices.
Q.20 (B) Division of labour enables dexterity, task specialization, time savings, and mechanization, boosting productivity.
Q.21 (B) Money is financial capital facilitating exchange, not a physical productive input like machines or labour.
Q.22 (A) A high working-age population relative to dependents creates a demographic dividend if supported by education and jobs.
Q.23 (B) Disguised unemployment occurs when marginal labour productivity is zero; workers appear employed but add no net output.
Q.24 (B) Structural transformation describes the reallocation of economic activity across primary, secondary, and tertiary sectors.
Q.25 (C) The Tertiary (services) sector contributes over 54% of India's GDP/GVA.
