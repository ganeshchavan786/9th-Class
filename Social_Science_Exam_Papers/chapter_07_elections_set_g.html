Q1. Under Article 324 of the Indian Constitution, the Election Commission of India (ECI) is vested with the power of 'superintendence, direction, and control' of elections. To safeguard its constitutional independence, how can the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) be removed from office?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Under Article 324(5), the CEC enjoys constitutional security of tenure and can be removed only in like manner and on the like grounds as a Judge of the Supreme Court (special majority under Article 124(4)).
Q2. What is the primary difference in political outcomes between the 'First-Past-The-Post' (FPTP / Plurality) electoral system used in Indian Lok Sabha elections and a 'Proportional Representation' (PR) system?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Under FPTP (plurality), a candidate with the highest individual vote tally wins, which often produces seat shares significantly higher than popular vote shares, whereas PR mirrors nationwide popular voting percentages.
Q3. Under Section 126 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, what is the mandatory 'Silence Period' (Purdah period) enforced prior to voting?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: RPA Section 126 prohibits holding public meetings, processions, musical concerts, and displaying election matter via TV/media during the 48 hours ending with the conclusion of polling.
Q4. In the landmark Supreme Court ruling in Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013), what crucial electoral reform was established regarding convicted legislators?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Supreme Court invalidated Section 8(4) of the RPA 1951, which previously shielded convicted MPs and MLAs from disqualification if they filed an appeal within 3 months, ending special legislative immunity.
Q5. What was the significance of the Supreme Court's ruling in Association for Democratic Reforms (ADR) v. Union of India (2002)?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: In the ADR 2002 judgment, the Supreme Court declared that voters have a fundamental 'Right to Know' under Article 19(1)(a), making affidavit disclosure (Form 26) of criminal cases, assets, and education mandatory.
Q6. In the Delimitation of Parliamentary and Assembly Constituencies, what is the constitutional role of the Delimitation Commission established under Article 82 of the Indian Constitution?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Delimitation Commission is a high-powered statutory body headed by a retired Supreme Court judge that re-adjusts territorial boundaries; under Article 329, its final orders cannot be called into question in any court.
Q7. Why were the number of Lok Sabha seats frozen based on the 1971 Census through the 42nd Amendment (1976) and extended up to 2026 by the 84th Amendment (2001)?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The constitutional freeze ensured that states implementing effective population control (such as Kerala and Tamil Nadu) did not lose parliamentary seats to states with high population growth rates.
Q8. Under the Model Code of Conduct (MCC) enforced by the Election Commission of India, what restriction is placed on the ruling party from the moment elections are announced?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The MCC prevents ruling incumbents from misusing official apparatus (announcing new schemes, using state aircraft, advertising with public funds) to gain an unfair advantage over the opposition.
Q9. In the Indian electoral apparatus, who serves as the pivotal operational authority at the district level responsible for conducting free and fair parliamentary/assembly polls?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The District Magistrate serves as District Election Officer (DEO) and Returning Officer (RO), deemed on constitutional deputation to the ECI under Article 324, overseeing all polling stations and security.
Q10. What is the function of the Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT) system introduced following the Supreme Court judgment in Subramanian Swamy v. ECI (2013)?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: VVPAT prints a paper slip displaying the candidate name and symbol through a transparent window for 7 seconds before securely dropping into a sealed ballot box, enabling physical recount audits.
Q11. In People's Union for Civil Liberties (PUCL) v. Union of India (2013), what landmark option did the Supreme Court direct the Election Commission to provide on EVMs and ballot papers?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Supreme Court directed the provision of the NOTA button, holding that the right to reject candidates without compromising secret voting is an integral aspect of free expression under Article 19(1)(a).
Q12. What is the legal status and enforceability of the Model Code of Conduct (MCC)?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The MCC is not a statutory parliamentary act, but the Supreme Court has repeatedly affirmed that ECI's plenary powers under Article 324 permit strict enforcement of MCC to maintain free and fair elections.
Q13. Duverger's Law in political science hypothesizes which structural relationship between electoral systems and party dynamics?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Maurice Duverger observed that plurality single-ballot voting (FPTP) systematically marginalizes third parties (mechanical and psychological effects), favouring a two-party equilibrium.
Q14. What is 'Gerrymandering' in electoral geography?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Gerrymandering is the partisan manipulation of constituency boundaries (via 'cracking' and 'packing') to establish an artificial electoral advantage for a particular political party.
Q15. Under the Indian Constitution, how are constituencies reserved for Scheduled Castes (SC) and Scheduled Tribes (ST) structured in Lok Sabha elections?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: India operates joint electorates: in an SC or ST reserved constituency, every registered voter regardless of caste casts a vote, but only persons belonging to the designated SC/ST community can file nominations.
Q16. Under Section 77 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, what expenditure must be maintained by an election candidate?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Candidates must maintain daily expenditure registers and submit sworn accounts within 30 days of results. Exceeding statutory limits or false filings can lead to disqualification under RPA Section 10A.
Q17. What was the historical significance of the 61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988 in the expansion of Indian democratic franchise?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The 61st Amendment (effective March 1989) lowered the constitutional voting age from 21 to 18 years, enfranchising millions of youth and deepening universal adult suffrage under Article 326.
Q18. In the T.N. Seshan era (Chief Election Commissioner from 1990 to 1996), what transformative institutional reforms were enforced by the Election Commission of India?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: T.N. Seshan fiercely asserted the autonomy of Article 324, introducing Voter ID cards (EPIC), halting booth capturing, penalizing liquor distribution, and enforcing strict adherence to campaign rules.
Q19. What is the primary role of an 'Election Observer' appointed by the Election Commission of India under Section 20B of the RPA 1951?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: ECI Observers (General, Expenditure, and Police Observers) are senior civil servants from outside the state who monitor compliance and possess powers under Section 20B to halt counting if fairness is compromised.
Q20. What happens to the security deposit lodged by an election candidate if they fail to secure a minimum threshold of popular votes in a parliamentary constituency?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: To deter non-serious candidates, the RPA dictates that any candidate failing to secure at least one-sixth (1/6th or 16.67%) of the total valid votes polled forfeits their statutory security deposit.
Q21. How does the 'Systematic Voters' Education and Electoral Participation' (SVEEP) program designed by the ECI strengthen Indian democracy?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: SVEEP is the flagship voter education initiative of the ECI designed to eliminate voter apathy, enhance registration in electoral rolls, and promote informed, ethical, and inclusive voting.
Q22. Under Article 329(b) of the Constitution of India, how can the election of any returned candidate to Parliament or a State Legislature be legally challenged?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Article 329(b) stipulates that no election to Parliament or State Legislature can be challenged except through an Election Petition filed before the High Court under the RPA 1951.
Q23. Why is the use of 'Indelible Ink' (silver nitrate solution) applied to the left forefinger of voters on polling day an essential safeguard against electoral fraud?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Developed by National Physical Laboratory (NPL) and manufactured by Mysore Paints, indelible ink contains silver nitrate that stains skin/keratin permanently, physically blocking impersonation.
Q24. What is the critical function of 'Polling Agents' seated inside the polling booth on election day?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Polling agents appointed by candidates inspect the mock poll (confirming EVMs read zero before start), cross-check voter credentials against roll entries, and ensure tamper-proof seal application.
Q25. What is a 'Mock Poll' conducted by the Presiding Officer before the commencement of official voting on election day?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Presiding Officer must cast at least 50 test votes across all candidates in the presence of party polling agents, verify VVPAT paper slips against electronic tally, clear memory to zero, and seal the unit.
