SECTION A: CORE THEORETICAL PRINCIPLES (Q.1 TO Q.10) [10 MARKS]
Q.1

Assertion (A): Social science employs systematic empirical observation and critical inquiry rather than mere conjecture.

Reason (R): Social scientific hypotheses must be verifiable against documentary records, material artifacts, and statistical data.

Q.2

Assertion (A): Inscriptions engraved on stone pillars and copper plates are classified as primary historical sources.

Reason (R): Primary historical sources are contemporary records created during the exact period of the historical event being investigated.

Q.3

Assertion (A): Historians rely entirely on official royal court chronicles to reconstruct an objective history of ancient societies.

Reason (R): Court chroniclers were commissioned by ruling monarchs and frequently exaggerated imperial triumphs while omitting royal defeats.

Q.4

Assertion (A): Coins (numismatics) serve as vital evidence for delineating the geographic territorial extent of ancient kingdoms.

Reason (R): The widespread archaeological discovery of uniform royal coinage across distinct regions reflects active economic circulation and political hegemony.

Q.5

Assertion (A): Radiocarbon dating (Carbon-14) can be utilized to determine the age of ancient bronze statues and gold ornaments.

Reason (R): Carbon-14 dating measures the radioactive decay of carbon isotopes present exclusively in organic materials that once absorbed atmospheric carbon.

Q.6

Assertion (A): The Law of Superposition in archaeological stratigraphy states that lower undisturbed sedimentary strata are older than upper strata.

Reason (R): Natural sedimentary deposition accumulates sequentially over time, laying newer debris over preexisting cultural layers.

Q.7

Assertion (A): Foreign travellers' accounts, such as those of Megasthenes and Xuanzang, provide completely unbiased objective accounts of ancient India.

Reason (R): Foreign travellers were outsiders unencumbered by local royal patronage or caste affiliations.

Q.8

Assertion (A): Epigraphy is the scientific study and decipherment of inscriptions engraved on hard surfaces.

Reason (R): Palaeography examines the historical evolution of handwriting, script forms, and alphabets over successive centuries.

Q.9

Assertion (A): Dendrochronology establishes absolute calendar dates for wooden architectural beams found at archaeological sites.

Reason (R): Trees generate distinct annual growth rings whose width varies systematically with seasonal climatic fluctuations.

Q.10

Assertion (A): Social science disciplines such as economics, sociology, and political science are studied in complete isolation from one another.

Reason (R): Human societal behavior operates as an interconnected whole where economic policies, political governance, and social norms continuously interact.

SECTION B: EVIDENTIARY PROOFS & MECHANISMS (Q.11 TO Q.18) [8 MARKS]
Q.11

Assertion (A): Archaeological excavations employ the Wheeler box-grid method to preserve stratigraphic balks between excavated trenches.

Reason (R): Stratigraphic balks preserve vertical soil profiles that allow archaeologists to correlate layers across adjacent excavation pits.

Q.12

Assertion (A): A high correlation between two social variables always proves a direct cause-and-effect relationship.

Reason (R): Correlation merely signifies that two variables change simultaneously, which may be caused by an underlying third lurking variable.

Q.13

Assertion (A): Secondary sources are written after historical events by researchers interpreting and synthesizing primary evidence.

Reason (R): A modern textbook on Harappan urbanism written by an archaeologist is an example of a secondary source.

Q.14

Assertion (A): The study of place names (toponymy) can provide clues about ancient settlement patterns, linguistics, and environment.

Reason (R): Settlement names often preserve archaic vocabulary describing local geomorphic features, flora, or founding dynasties long after languages evolve.

Q.15

Assertion (A): Quantitative sample surveys completely eliminate all forms of research error in social scientific studies.

Reason (R): Sample surveys may suffer from sampling bias, non-response error, and poorly phrased interview questions.

Q.16

Assertion (A): Oral traditions and folklore are discarded by modern historians as having zero historiographical value.

Reason (R): Oral traditions can preserve authentic community memories and migration narratives when critically corroborated with archaeological evidence.

Q.17

Assertion (A): Cartography plays an indispensable role in geographic and historical social science analysis.

Reason (R): Spatial distribution maps allow researchers to visualize trade corridors, resource concentrations, and demographic shifts.

Q.18

Assertion (A): Microscopic analysis of pollen grains (palynology) allows researchers to reconstruct ancient environmental vegetation and agricultural history.

Reason (R): Pollen grains possess durable outer walls of sporopollenin that resist decay in waterlogged archaeological sediments.

SECTION C: ADVANCED NUANCES & INSTITUTIONAL COMPLEXITIES (Q.19 TO Q.25) [7 MARKS]
Q.19

Assertion (A): Scientific neutrality requires social scientists to abandon all ethical commitments to democratic constitutional values.

Reason (R): Objective scientific methodology requires factual honesty in data analysis, while social science education fosters informed, ethical citizenship.

Q.20

Assertion (A): Historiographical revisionism is a legitimate feature of academic historical research.

Reason (R): The discovery of unexamined primary archival documents and advanced archaeological techniques can modify existing historical interpretations.

Q.21

Assertion (A): Karl Popper proposed that a scientific hypothesis must be capable of empirical falsification.

Reason (R): A hypothesis that cannot be tested or proven false by any conceivable empirical observation is dogmatic rather than scientific.

Q.22

Assertion (A): Archaeological artifacts found in surface scatter are more chronologically reliable than artifacts sealed in stratified contexts.

Reason (R): Surface artifacts have been subjected to agricultural ploughing, weathering, and human displacement, destroying stratigraphic provenance.

Q.23

Assertion (A): Social scientists utilize triangulation by cross-checking multiple independent sources of data.

Reason (R): Relying on a single data source increases vulnerability to individual author bias, translation errors, and localized anomalies.

Q.24

Assertion (A): Ethnocentrism in social science research refers to the practice of evaluating other cultures strictly according to the standards of one's own culture.

Reason (R): Ethnocentric perspectives promote objective, universally valid cross-cultural generalizations.

Q.25

Assertion (A): Social science inquiry helps citizens identify misinformation and develop critical thinking in a constitutional democracy.

Reason (R): Democratic governance relies on informed citizens capable of analyzing government policies, socioeconomic disparities, and civil liberties.
