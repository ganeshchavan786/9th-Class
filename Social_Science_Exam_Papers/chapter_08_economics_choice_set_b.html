OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET B)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & Analytical Rationale
Q.1 (A) The PPF depicts boundary trade-offs: full resource utilization means more capital goods (tractors) necessitates foregoing consumer goods (bread), demonstrating opportunity cost.
Q.2 (A) Fiscal budgets face hard constraints; choosing one expenditure necessarily foregoes alternative high-value public goods, defining opportunity cost.
Q.3 (A) Fixed capital represents durable physical assets that amortize over years; working capital consists of single-use inputs and operational cash consumed within the crop cycle.
Q.4 (A) Human capital is the primary driver of total factor productivity: skills, scientific knowledge, and health enable societies to utilize physical capital effectively.
Q.5 (A) Price signals communicate consumer preferences: rising prices signal unmet demand, prompting profit-maximizing entrepreneurs to allocate resources toward those goods.
Q.6 (A) Developing nations with surplus labor balance employment creation (labour-intensive) against technical productivity and global competitiveness (capital-intensive).
Q.7 (A) Kuznets structural transformation: agricultural productivity growth releases surplus labor into higher-value manufacturing and tertiary services, driving economic modernization.
Q.8 (A) Disguised unemployment occurs when 8 family members work on a farm that requires only 5; withdrawing 3 workers leaves total agricultural output unchanged (marginal product = 0).
Q.9 (A) India's mixed economy integrates private enterprise for innovation and growth with state interventions (PDS, public healthcare, labor laws) to safeguard equitable welfare.
Q.10 (A) Depreciation measures the consumption of fixed capital over its economic lifespan due to wear, tear, and technological obsolescence, essential for calculating net investment.
Q.11 (A) Points inside the PPF indicate productive inefficiency or macroeconomic recession: factories operating below capacity and widespread involuntary unemployment.
Q.12 (A) Economic goods involve scarcity and positive opportunity cost ($MC > 0$); free goods exist in natural abundance at zero opportunity cost to society.
Q.13 (A) Joseph Schumpeter highlighted the entrepreneur as the agent of 'creative destruction' who innovates, bears non-insurable risks, and combines inert factors into productive enterprise.
Q.14 (A) Educating girls yields compounding multi-generational externalities: higher household income, delayed marriage, reduced child stunting, and improved schooling outcomes for offspring.
Q.15 (A) The PDS stabilizes food security by procuring grains at remunerative MSP and distributing them via Fair Price Shops to BPL families below market rates.
Q.16 (A) Garrett Hardin's 'Tragedy of the Commons' shows that open-access resources suffer rivalrous over-depletion because private extractors capture 100% of the benefit while socializing the costs.
Q.17 (A) Tertiary activities generate non-physical service utilities: financial intermediation, digital networking, freight logistics, and professional healthcare/education.
Q.18 (A) Real wage equals nominal wage divided by the price index ($W/P$); if a worker's cash wage rises by 5% while consumer inflation surges 10%, real purchasing power drops by 5%.
Q.19 (A) Printing fiat currency without expanding real goods and services inflates the money supply ($MV = PY$), precipitating catastrophic hyperinflation (as in Weimar Germany or Zimbabwe).
Q.20 (A) SHGs leverage social capital: group lending with joint liability substitutes for traditional physical collateral, enabling low-income women to access formal bank credit.
Q.21 (A) An outward expansion of the PPF frontier reflects sustained economic growth: technological breakthroughs and labor skill enhancements permit higher output of all goods.
Q.22 (A) Rain-fed single-crop farming creates peak labor demand during monsoon sowing/harvesting, leaving agricultural laborers seasonally unemployed during dry months.
Q.23 (A) Public rural infrastructure functions as a critical public good, reducing transport transaction costs, expanding market access, and stimulating non-farm enterprise.
Q.24 (A) Brain drain represents human capital flight: public funds educate specialists whose subsequent overseas emigration deprives the home country of essential expertise and tax revenues.
Q.25 (A) As Amartya Sen and Alfred Marshall emphasized, economics is the study of mankind in the ordinary business of life, seeking to enhance human flourishing and eliminate deprivation.
