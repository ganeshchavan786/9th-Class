Q. No. Correct Curricular Concept & Rapid Recall Analysis
Q.1 (B) The troposphere contains 75% of atmospheric mass and almost all water vapour, hosting all active weather.
Q.2 (B) The normal lapse rate in the troposphere averages a temperature drop of 6.5°C per kilometre of ascent.
Q.3 (B) The stratosphere houses the ozone layer (at 15–35 km altitude), which absorbs harmful solar UV rays.
Q.4 (C) The mesosphere is the coldest layer (plunging below -90°C) and incinerates meteoroids through friction.
Q.5 (B) Nitrogen constitutes approximately 78.08% of clean dry air by volume.
Q.6 (B) The Coriolis force deflects moving objects to their right in the Northern Hemisphere and to their left in the Southern Hemisphere.
Q.7 (B) Coriolis force is proportional to $\sin \phi$; at the equator ($\phi = 0^\circ$), $\sin 0^\circ = 0$, making Coriolis force zero.
Q.8 (B) The equatorial low-pressure convergence zone is historically known to sailors as the Doldrums.
Q.9 (B) The calm, dry subtropical high-pressure subsidence belts at 30°–35° latitude are called the Horse Latitudes.
Q.10 (B) The Southwest Monsoon delivers over 75% of India's annual precipitation between June and September.
Q.11 (B) The northeast retreating monsoon absorbs moisture over the Bay of Bengal, dropping rain on Tamil Nadu's Coromandel coast.
Q.12 (B) Mawsynram in the Khasi Hills of Meghalaya receives ~11,872 mm of annual precipitation, making it the wettest place on Earth.
Q.13 (B) The Loo consists of intensely hot, dry summer winds originating from the Thar desert across North India.
Q.14 (B) Kalbaisakhi ('calamity of the month of Baisakh') are severe pre-monsoon thunderstorms in Bengal.
Q.15 (B) Tropical cyclones require sea surface temperatures of at least 26.5°C–27°C to provide sufficient latent heat moisture fuel.
Q.16 (B) The eye is the central calm zone of subsiding air featuring clear skies and lowest central surface pressure.
Q.17 (B) Water vapour is the most abundant natural greenhouse gas, accounting for the largest share of natural infrared absorption.
Q.18 (B) Albedo is the reflectivity of a surface; fresh snow has a very high albedo (~80–90%), while open ocean water has a low albedo.
Q.19 (B) El Niño refers to anomalous eastern Pacific warming, often associated with deficit monsoon rainfall in India.
Q.20 (B) Thermal inversion reverses the normal lapse rate, creating a warm lid that halts vertical convective dispersal.
Q.21 (B) The Montreal Protocol (1987) successfully mandated global phase-outs of ozone-destroying substances.
Q.22 (B) Proximity to the ocean moderates coastal climates, preventing extreme diurnal and annual temperature swings.
Q.23 (B) Jet streams are high-velocity westerly or easterly geostrophic wind ribbons in the upper troposphere.
Q.24 (B) Pre-monsoon thunderstorms in Kerala and Karnataka are popularly called Mango Showers as they hasten mango ripening.
Q.25 (A) The carbon footprint measures total equivalent carbon dioxide emissions generated by an activity, organization, or lifestyle.
