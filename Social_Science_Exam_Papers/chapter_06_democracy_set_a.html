OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET A)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & Conceptual Rationale
Q.1 (A) Abraham Lincoln famously defined democracy as 'government of the people, by the people, for the people' in his Gettysburg Address.
Q.2 (A) A fundamental feature of democracy is that the final decision-making power must rest with those elected directly or indirectly by the people.
Q.3 (A) A democratic election requires that voters have genuine choices between competing political alternatives, and incumbents face real accountability.
Q.4 (A) Universal Adult Franchise guarantees that all adult citizens, regardless of gender, wealth, caste, or race, enjoy equal voting power.
Q.5 (A) Democratic governance operates under the 'Rule of Law', requiring the state to respect institutional checks and constitutional civil liberties.
Q.6 (A) In Pakistan under Musharraf's Legal Framework Order (2002), elected representatives lacked supreme power; decisions were dominated by the military council.
Q.7 (A) Before contesting elections in China, candidates must seek approval from the ruling Communist Party, denying voters any real competitive choice.
Q.8 (A) Although elections took place, the PRI monopolized state machinery, compelled schoolteachers to campaign, and manipulated polling booths.
Q.9 (A) Saudi Arabia granted women the right to vote and contest only in municipal elections starting in 2015.
Q.10 (A) In Fiji, the electoral system assigned unequal weight to votes, where an indigenous Fijian's vote counted more than an Indian-Fijian's vote.
Q.11 (A) Mugabe's regime used state-backed intimidation, banned peaceful protests, and censored news, proving that mere popularity does not guarantee democracy.
Q.12 (A) Democracy is based on consultation and discussion, making the government accountable to the electorate and preventing neglected famines.
Q.13 (A) Sen demonstrated that no major famine has ever occurred in a functioning democracy with a free press and opposition criticism.
Q.14 (A) Democratic decision-making involves broad discussion among many people, allowing mistakes to be identified and rectified before laws are enacted.
Q.15 (A) In a diverse country like India, democracy provides a peaceful framework where different communities can accommodate each other without violence.
Q.16 (A) In a democracy, citizens are not subjects of an absolute ruler; they are the sovereign masters of their own collective destiny.
Q.17 (A) Democracy has self-correcting mechanisms: mistakes are publicly debated, and if rulers refuse to reform, electors can vote them out of office.
Q.18 (A) Common criticisms of democracy include political instability from changing coalitions and procedural delays caused by extensive legislative deliberations.
Q.19 (A) Direct democracy involves all citizens gathering directly to deliberate and decide laws, feasible only in small city-state populations.
Q.20 (A) With massive populations, modern nation-states elect representative lawmakers to debate and administer public policies on behalf of the citizenry.
Q.21 (A) A democratic mindset applies to families, schools, and workplaces wherever decisions are made through mutual respect, dialogue, and consensus.
Q.22 (A) South Africa ended apartheid in 1994, drafting one of the world's most progressive democratic constitutions ensuring equal rights for all races.
Q.23 (A) The Rule of Law ensures legal equality: everyone is subject to the same laws administered by impartial courts.
Q.24 (A) The opposition keeps the government in check, scrutinizes budgets, defends public interests, and prevents executive authoritarianism.
Q.25 (A) Democracy allows citizens to participate in self-governance, protects personal freedom, accommodates diversity, and permits peaceful reform.
