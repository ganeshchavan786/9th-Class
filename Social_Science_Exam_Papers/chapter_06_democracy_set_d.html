SECTION A: CORE THEORETICAL PRINCIPLES (Q.1 TO Q.10) [10 MARKS]
Q.1

Assertion (A): Democracy is a form of government in which rulers are elected by the citizens.

Reason (R): The essential core principle of democracy is that political sovereignty resides with the people through competitive popular elections.

Q.2

Assertion (A): A country that merely conducts periodic elections cannot automatically be classified as a genuine democracy.

Reason (R): For elections to be democratic, they must offer a genuine choice between competing political alternatives with a fair chance for the ruling party to lose.

Q.3

Assertion (A): In Pakistan under General Pervez Musharraf, the 2002 Legal Framework Order made Pakistan a complete constitutional democracy.

Reason (R): The Legal Framework Order empowered the military President to dismiss national and provincial assemblies and subjected elected ministers to the National Security Council.

Q.4

Assertion (A): Democracy requires the principle of 'one person, one vote, one value'.

Reason (R): Equal political franchise ensures that every citizen possesses equal weight and equal moral worth in electing representative lawgivers.

Q.5

Assertion (A): In Fiji's electoral system prior to recent reforms, the vote of an indigenous Fijian had higher electoral value than that of an Indo-Fijian.

Reason (R): Democratic equality was fully respected because all citizens were permitted to cast their ballots on election day.

Q.6

Assertion (A): In China, elections are held regularly every five years for the National People's Congress.

Reason (R): China is a vibrant multi-party democracy where opposition candidates easily challenge and defeat Communist Party leaders.

Q.7

Assertion (A): A democratic government rules without any legal constraints or constitutional limitations.

Reason (R): A democratic government must function within constitutional laws passed by the legislature and respect citizen rights protected by an independent judiciary.

Q.8

Assertion (A): In Mexico, between 1930 and 2000, the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) won every presidential election.

Reason (R): The PRI utilized state resources, coerced public employees, pressured media outlets, and shifted polling booths to prevent free and fair competition.

Q.9

Assertion (A): Democracy improves the quality of decision-making compared to autocracy.

Reason (R): Democratic processes involve multi-stakeholder consultations, open legislative debates, and parliamentary committees, reducing the risk of rash, arbitrary policies.

Q.10

Assertion (A): In an autocratic dictatorship, decisions are made faster than in a democratic assembly.

Reason (R): Dictators do not have to deliberate with elected parliaments or negotiate consensus among diverse societal groups.

SECTION B: EVIDENTIARY PROOFS & MECHANISMS (Q.11 TO Q.18) [8 MARKS]
Q.11

Assertion (A): Democracy provides an institutionalized mechanism to resolve conflicts among diverse social groups peacefully.

Reason (R): In a pluralistic society with differing regional, linguistic, and religious identities, democratic negotiations allow power-sharing without violent repression.

Q.12

Assertion (A): Critics argue that democracy leads to frequent political instability.

Reason (R): Electoral competition, shifting party coalitions, and regular changes in leadership can interrupt long-term policy continuity.

Q.13

Assertion (A): Democracy guarantees that governments will never commit mistakes or implement flawed economic policies.

Reason (R): Democracy provides open public forums to debate policy errors and guarantees citizens the electoral mechanism to replace governments that perform poorly.

Q.14

Assertion (A): The Great Chinese Famine of 1958–1961 resulted in nearly 30 million deaths, whereas democratic India did not experience famine of such scale.

Reason (R): Economist Amartya Sen observed that democratic governments with free press and opposition parties cannot afford to ignore famines without facing electoral defeat.

Q.15

Assertion (A): Democracy enhances the dignity of citizens regardless of their socioeconomic status.

Reason (R): In a democracy, the poorest and most marginalized citizens enjoy the exact same constitutional status and voting power as the wealthiest aristocrat.

Q.16

Assertion (A): In Zimbabwe, Robert Mugabe's ZANU-PF government respected media independence and welcomed peaceful protests.

Reason (R): Mugabe's regime harassed opposition workers, censored independent radio and newspapers, amended the constitution repeatedly, and used state violence to stay in power.

Q.17

Assertion (A): Direct democracy is universally practiced in all large modern nation-states like India and the United States.

Reason (R): Massive modern populations across vast continental territories make it physically impossible for all citizens to sit together and decide every executive matter daily.

Q.18

Assertion (A): The Swiss cantons provide one of the few surviving modern examples of direct democracy through referendums and citizen assemblies.

Reason (R): Switzerland allows citizens to propose constitutional initiatives and overturn legislative laws via mandatory popular referendums.

SECTION C: ADVANCED NUANCES & INSTITUTIONAL COMPLEXITIES (Q.19 TO Q.25) [7 MARKS]
Q.19

Assertion (A): A democratic decision involves consultation with and consent of all those who are affected by that decision.

Reason (R): The democratic principle applies not only to state governments but also to decision-making within families, schools, classrooms, and workplaces.

Q.20

Assertion (A): In a true democracy, majority rule gives the majority group the absolute right to strip minorities of their basic civil rights.

Reason (R): Constitutional democracy safeguards fundamental human rights and minority protections against the 'tyranny of the majority'.

Q.21

Assertion (A): Dr. B. R. Ambedkar warned against hero-worship (Bhakti) in political life during his final address to the Constituent Assembly.

Reason (R): Ambedkar argued that hero-worship in politics is a sure road to degradation and eventual dictatorship.

Q.22

Assertion (A): The presence of an independent judiciary is unnecessary for maintaining a healthy democratic system.

Reason (R): An independent judiciary acts as the guardian of the constitution, checks executive overreach, and protects citizen rights from legislative infringements.

Q.23

Assertion (A): Estonia's citizenship rules made it difficult for people belonging to the Russian ethnic minority to acquire voting rights.

Reason (R): Democratic equality requires that minority language communities be automatically denied civic franchise in all democratic states.

Q.24

Assertion (A): Democracy is not a magical cure-all that automatically solves poverty, unemployment, and all social evils.

Reason (R): Democracy creates a political framework where citizens and elected leaders possess the freedom to deliberate, organize, and demand socioeconomic reforms.

Q.25

Assertion (A): In India, Article 32 of the Constitution was referred to by Dr. Ambedkar as the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution.

Reason (R): Article 32 guarantees the Right to Constitutional Remedies, empowering citizens to move the Supreme Court directly to enforce their Fundamental Rights.
