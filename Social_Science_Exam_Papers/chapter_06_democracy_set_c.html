Q.2 What was the famous independent trade union led by Lech Walesa in Poland in 1980 that sparked the democratic revolution against the Soviet-backed communist regime?

Q.7 What is the legal difference between 'Rule OF Law' and 'Rule BY Law' in political theory?
Q.8 Which independent constitutional officer in India is designated as the 'guardian of the public purse' to audit government expenditures and prevent fiscal corruption under Article 148?
Q.9 Why was the South African Constitution adopted in 1996 hailed globally as a model of democratic transformation?

Q.24 What is the constitutional meaning of 'secularism' in the Indian context compared to Western secularism?

OFFICIAL ANSWER KEY & STEP-BY-STEP EXPLANATIONS (SET C)
Q. No. Correct Curricular Reference & In-Depth Conceptual Solution
Q.1 (A) President Salvador Allende was deposed on 11 September 1973 in a brutal military coup by General Pinochet, leading to 17 years of military dictatorship in Chile.
Q.2 (A) Solidarity (*Solidarnosc*), founded at the Lenin Shipyard in Gdansk under Lech Walesa, was the first independent trade union in a Warsaw Pact nation, paving the way for multi-party democracy in 1989.
Q.3 (A) Nkrumah amended the Ghanaian constitution to establish a one-party state and declared himself president-for-life, until he was ousted in a military coup in 1966.
Q.4 (A) In the Swiss cantons of Appenzell Innerrhoden and Glarus, citizens gather annually in town squares to vote directly on local laws and budgets by a show of hands.
Q.5 (A) Dr. Ambedkar presciently cautioned that uncritical personal devotion or hero-worship (*Bhakti*) toward a political leader inevitably leads to the destruction of democracy and autocratic rule.
Q.6 (A) Article 32 guarantees direct recourse to the Supreme Court via prerogative writs (*habeas corpus, mandamus, etc.*) to enforce fundamental rights, without which rights remain mere paper promises.
Q.7 (A) Rule *of* Law implies justice, fairness, and institutional checks; Rule *by* Law is authoritarian legalism where despots pass arbitrary draconian statutes to legitimize oppression.
Q.8 (A) The CAG audits all receipts and expenditures of the Union and State governments, presenting objective reports directly to parliamentary public accounts committees.
Q.9 (A) Negotiated by the ANC and National Party, the 1996 South African Constitution pioneered non-racialism, reconciliation, and justiciable socio-economic rights (housing, healthcare, water).
Q.10 (A) *Habeas Corpus* is the bulwark of personal liberty: it compels law enforcement to bring an arrested person to court, preventing secret kidnapping, custodial torture, or indefinite extrajudicial detention.
Q.11 (A) When multi-party elections produce a hung parliament, parties negotiate a common minimum program and share ministerial berths to form a coalition government.
Q.12 (A) The Preamble encapsulates the soul of the Constitution: India is a 'Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic' committed to Justice, Liberty, Equality, and Fraternity.
Q.13 (A) Enacted after the 1975–77 Emergency, the 44th Amendment curbed executive abuse: the President can proclaim Emergency only upon unanimous *written* cabinet advice for 'armed rebellion' or external aggression.
Q.14 (A) Statutory regulatory bodies maintain level playing fields, curb anti-competitive cartels, safeguard consumer rights, and manage vital sectors based on objective rules rather than political whims.
Q.15 (A) Gerrymandering manipulates district boundaries for partisan advantage; India avoids this by vesting delimitation powers in an independent quasi-judicial Delimitation Commission.
Q.16 (A) Constitutionalism rejects unchecked legislative sovereignty: even laws passed by a 90% parliamentary majority are unconstitutional if they breach fundamental rights or constitutional design.
Q.17 (A) The *Gram Sabha* is the bedrock of direct grassroots democracy: all adult village electors meet periodically to scrutinize the elected Panchayat's expenditures, projects, and beneficiary rosters.
Q.18 (A) Justice Bhagwati ruled that Article 21's 'procedure' must satisfy the test of substantive justice: oppressive or arbitrary laws cannot justify taking away life or personal liberty.
Q.19 (A) Deliberative polling measures what citizens would think if they had the opportunity to study balanced evidence, engage with experts, and debate opposing views respectfully.
Q.20 (A) The Tenth Schedule prevents the cynical phenomenon of *Aaya Ram, Gaya Ram* by disqualifying MPs/MLAs who voluntarily defect from the party on whose ticket they were elected.
Q.21 (A) Though not a formal state branch, a free and independent press provides the investigative scrutiny and public exposure necessary to keep the three branches honest.
Q.22 (A) Parliamentary systems feature fusion of powers (Cabinet is drawn from and must maintain the confidence of the Lok Sabha); presidential systems feature strict separation of powers.
Q.23 (A) Article 21A recognizes that meaningful democratic citizenship requires literacy: without basic education, citizens cannot read laws, vote knowledgeably, or assert their legal rights.
Q.24 (A) Western secularism maintains a strict wall of separation; Indian secularism practices principled distance: equal respect for all faiths (*Sarva Dharma Sambhava*) alongside reform of social evils.
Q.25 (A) As Thomas Jefferson remarked, 'Eternal vigilance is the price of liberty': democratic institutions survive only when citizens actively defend their rights against encroachment.
