Q1. In neoclassical price theory, Alfred Marshall famously compared supply and demand to the 'two blades of a pair of scissors'. What was the core theoretical insight behind this metaphor?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Marshall resolved the classical vs marginalist debate by showing that just as both blades of a pair of scissors cut paper, market equilibrium price is determined jointly by demand (utility) and supply (marginal cost).
Q2. What is the primary underlying reason why the Demand Curve slopes downward from left to right for a normal commodity?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The downward slope reflects: (1) diminishing marginal utility (successive units yield less satisfaction), (2) substitution effect (cheaper relative to substitutes), and (3) income effect (higher real purchasing power).
Q3. What differentiates a 'Change in Quantity Demanded' from a 'Change in Demand' in microeconomic analysis?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: A price change causes movement along the curve (expansion/contraction of quantity demanded). Non-price factors (income, preferences, cross-prices) shift the entire demand curve (increase/decrease in demand).
Q4. If the price of commodity A increases by 10% and, as a direct consequence, the demand for commodity B increases by 15%, what is the economic relationship between goods A and B?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: When the price of one good rises, demand for its substitute rises as consumers switch away (positive cross-price elasticity, $E_{AB} > 0$). Hence, A and B are substitutes (e.g., tea and coffee).
Q5. What defines a 'Giffen Good' in consumer demand theory?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: A Giffen good is a highly inferior staple without close substitutes where a price rise impoverishes poor consumers so much (negative income effect) that they buy more of it, violating the Law of Demand.
Q6. In the case of a 'Veblen Good' (associated with Thorstein Veblen's Theory of the Leisure Class), why might quantity demanded increase as price rises?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Veblen goods (luxury designer jewelry, supercars) exhibit ostentatious prestige demand: higher prices enhance exclusivity and perceived status, inducing higher demand among wealthy buyers.
Q7. Using the Percentage Method, if a 20% increase in the price of a product leads to a 20% decrease in its quantity demanded, the Price Elasticity of Demand ($|E_d|$) is:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Price elasticity of demand is $|E_d| = |\% \Delta Q / \% \Delta P| = |-20\% / +20\%| = 1.0$. This represents unitary elastic demand, where total expenditure remains constant.
Q8. According to the Total Outlay (Total Expenditure) method, if a reduction in the price of a commodity leads to an INCREASE in total expenditure by consumers on that good, the demand is:
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: When price falls and total spending rises (or price rises and total spending falls), the percentage change in quantity exceeds the percentage change in price ($|E_d| > 1$), indicating elastic demand.
Q9. Which of the following goods is most likely to exhibit Highly Inelastic demand ($|E_d| < 1$)?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Life-saving medicines (like insulin) are absolute necessities with zero close substitutes and consume a modest fraction of income; consumers will buy them regardless of price increases.
Q10. Why does the Market Supply Curve typically slope upward from left to right?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Law of Supply states that, ceteris paribus, higher prices incentivize producers to supply more units because higher revenue covers rising marginal costs, yielding higher profits.
Q11. What happens in a competitive market if the prevailing market price is set ABOVE the equilibrium price?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: When price exceeds equilibrium, producers supply more than buyers wish to purchase ($Q_s > Q_d$). Unsold inventories pile up, forcing competing sellers to lower prices until equilibrium is restored.
Q12. What is the primary microeconomic consequence of the government imposing an effective 'Price Ceiling' (maximum legal price) BELOW the market equilibrium price for an essential staple food?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: A price ceiling below equilibrium makes the good cheaper, raising quantity demanded while reducing suppliers' willingness to produce ($Q_d > Q_s$), triggering shortages, rationing, and black marketing.
Q13. When the government establishes a 'Price Floor' (such as Minimum Support Price - MSP for wheat) ABOVE the free market equilibrium price, what problem arises unless the state intervenes?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: A price floor above equilibrium discourages consumer demand while encouraging farmer production ($Q_s > Q_d$). The state must step in to buy and store the surplus (buffer stocks) to maintain the MSP.
Q14. In microeconomics, what is 'Deadweight Loss' caused by market-distorting price controls or excise taxes?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Deadweight loss is the net loss of total social welfare (consumer + producer surplus) resulting from an inefficient level of production where marginal social benefit does not equal marginal social cost.
Q15. What is 'Consumer Surplus' in Alfred Marshall's cardinal utility analysis?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Consumer surplus is the net economic benefit to consumers: the area beneath the demand curve and above the market price, reflecting the excess utility enjoyed over what was actually paid.
Q16. If the government levies a per-unit excise tax on a commodity whose demand is Perfectly Inelastic ($E_d = 0$), who bears the ultimate economic burden (tax incidence) of the tax?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: When demand is perfectly inelastic (vertical demand curve), buyers must purchase the exact same quantity regardless of price; sellers shift the entire tax onto buyers via higher prices.
Q17. What happens to the market equilibrium price and quantity of electric cars if there is a SIMULTANEOUS breakthrough in battery technology (shifting supply right) and a surge in public environmental preference for clean vehicles (shifting demand right)?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Both rightward shifts work together to increase quantity ($Q^*$ rises unambiguously). However, supply increase lowers price while demand increase raises price, making the net price effect indeterminate.
Q18. In a market characterized by 'Perfect Competition', what is the nature of the demand curve faced by an individual firm?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Under perfect competition, firms sell identical products and represent an infinitesimal share of the market; they can sell any quantity at the market price, making their individual demand curve horizontal ($E_d = \infty$).
Q19. What is 'Monopoly Power' and what is its primary microeconomic consequence compared to a competitive market?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Monopolists face downward-sloping demand and produce where $MR = MC$, setting $P > MC$. This restricts output below competitive levels, transferring consumer surplus and creating deadweight loss.
Q20. What is an 'Inferior Good' in consumer theory?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Inferior goods (e.g., coarse grains, basic public bus transport) have negative income elasticity: as consumers become wealthier, they substitute toward superior alternatives (e.g., fine rice, private motorbikes).
Q21. What does a 'Shift of the Supply Curve to the Left' (decrease in supply) signify?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: A leftward supply shift means higher production costs or disrupted supply chains force sellers to offer lower quantities at every price, driving equilibrium price up and quantity down.
Q22. How does 'Time Horizon' affect the Price Elasticity of Supply for manufactured goods?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: In the momentary period, supply is fixed (inelastic). In the long run, firms can expand production lines, source inputs, and enter/exit the market, making long-run supply far more elastic.
Q23. What is 'Price Discrimination' practiced by a monopolist?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Price discrimination involves charging different prices to separate market segments with varying price elasticities (e.g., student airline discounts vs business fares), converting consumer surplus into monopoly profit.
Q24. When cross-price elasticity of demand between two goods X and Y is strictly NEGATIVE ($E_{xy} < 0$), what does this reveal about their consumption relationship?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: Negative cross-price elasticity ($E_{xy} < 0$) indicates complementary goods: a price rise in cars decreases car demand, which in turn reduces demand for fuel, causing both goods to move together.
Q25. In market equilibrium analysis, what is the 'Cobweb Model'?
Scholarly Concept & Analysis: The Cobweb phenomenon occurs in agriculture where farmers make planting decisions based on current harvest prices; lag between decision and harvest causes recurring price and supply oscillations.
